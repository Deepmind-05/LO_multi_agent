

TARGET-LANGUAGE: Lezgian (lez)

CORE

1. Concept: Dependent marking in possessive noun phrases (source 81A-1)
   - Target-language realization: The possessor receives genitive case (a suffix); the possessed noun (head) is unmarked for possession. WALS 24A confirms "possessor is dependent-marked." WALS 57A confirms no possessive affixes on the possessed noun.
   - Intended solver inference: From data showing possessive NPs, the solver identifies that one NP element bears a suffix (the possessor) while the other (the head noun) does not, and infers that the language marks the dependent (possessor) rather than the head.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

2. Concept: Noun before modifier (source 82A-1)
   - DROPPED. Lezgian is consistently modifier-before-noun (WALS 87A: AdjN; 88A: DemN; 89A: NumN; 90A: RelN; 86A: GenN). The source value does not apply. Replaced by target-specific concept below.

TARGET-SPECIFIC

1. Phenomenon: Modifier-before-noun order (inverse of source 82A-1)
   - Intended solver inference: All modifiers (genitive possessor, adjective, demonstrative, numeral, relative clause) precede the head noun. The solver recognizes a consistent head-final pattern in noun phrases.
   - Interaction with CORE: The genitive possessor is both the marked element (dependent marking) AND the pre-noun element (modifier-before-noun). The solver discovers that the marked element systematically precedes the unmarked head.
   - Difficulty contribution: Medium. Requires recognizing a consistent ordering pattern across multiple modifier types.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

2. Phenomenon: Ergative-absolutive alignment
   - Intended solver inference: In transitive clauses, the agent (A) bears ergative case (a suffix); the patient (P) is unmarked (absolutive). In intransitive clauses, the subject (S) is unmarked (absolutive). The solver infers a parallel to possessive dependent marking: the "agentive" or "dependent" role is marked, the "patientive" or "head" role is unmarked.
   - Interaction with CORE: Extends the dependent-marking pattern from possessive NPs to clause-level argument structure. The solver sees a unified principle: the language marks the semantically dependent/agentive role with case.
   - Difficulty contribution: High. Requires the solver to distinguish transitive from intransitive clauses and identify the ergative/absolutive contrast from data.
   - Priority: HIGH

3. Phenomenon: Suffixal case marking (strongly suffixing morphology)
   - Intended solver inference: All case markers (genitive, ergative, etc.) are suffixes attached to the noun stem. The solver identifies the morphological mechanism of dependent marking.
   - Interaction with CORE: Provides the observable morphological form of the dependent marking. The solver can segment noun + case suffix.
   - Difficulty contribution: Low. Directly observable from data.
   - Priority: HIGH

4. Phenomenon: No verbal person/number marking
   - Intended solver inference: Verbs do not encode person or number of any argument. Grammatical roles are identified solely through case marking on noun phrases.
   - Interaction with CORE: Reinforces the centrality of NP-internal case marking. The solver cannot rely on verbal agreement and must attend to case on NPs, making the dependent-marking pattern more salient.
   - Difficulty contribution: Medium. The solver must notice the absence of verbal agreement and recognize that case on NPs is the sole role-identifying mechanism.
   - Priority: HIGH

5. Phenomenon: Obligatory plural suffix on all nouns
   - Intended solver inference: Plural nouns always bear a plural suffix; singular nouns do not. This provides a reliable morphological boundary marker.
   - Interaction with CORE: The plural suffix helps the solver identify noun boundaries and distinguish the noun stem from case suffixes (plural suffix + case suffix stacking).
   - Difficulty contribution: Low. Provides a useful reference point for morphological analysis.
   - Priority: OPTIONAL

6. Phenomenon: SOV basic word order
   - Intended solver inference: The verb follows both subject and object. Combined with modifier-before-noun, the language shows a consistent head-final profile.
   - Interaction with CORE: Reinforces the head-final typological profile established by modifier-before-noun order.
   - Difficulty contribution: Low. Easily observable.
   - Priority: OPTIONAL

INTERACTIONS

1. Dependent marking (possessive) × Ergative alignment: Both constructions mark the "dependent" or "agentive" role with a case suffix while leaving the "head" or "patientive" role unmarked. The solver infers a unified dependent-marking principle operating across both NP-internal and clause-level syntax.

2. Modifier-before-noun × Dependent marking: The marked element (genitive possessor) is also the pre-noun element. The solver discovers that marking and ordering are aligned: the marked/dependent element precedes the unmarked/head element.

3. No verbal person marking × Ergative alignment: Since verbs carry no person/number information, case on NPs is the sole means of identifying grammatical roles. This makes the ergative/absolutive contrast more prominent and easier to isolate from data.

4. Suffixal case × Obligatory plural: Case suffixes stack on top of (or after) the plural suffix. The solver can use the obligatory plural as a fixed reference point to identify the variable case suffix.

CONSTRAINTS

1. The puzzle data must clearly show that the genitive suffix appears on the possessor, not on the possessed noun. No possessive affix may appear on the head noun.
2. Modifier-before-noun order must be demonstrated with at least two types of modifiers (e.g., genitive + adjective, or genitive + demonstrative) to establish it as a general pattern rather than a genitive-specific property.
3. If ergative alignment is included, the data must include both transitive and intransitive clauses with clear semantic roles, allowing the solver to identify the A-marked vs. S/P-unmarked contrast.
4. Verbs in the data must not show any person or number agreement morphology.
5. Case markers must be presented as suffixes (not prefixes, circumfixes, or independent words).
6. The puzzle should not require the solver to identify more than two case values (genitive and ergative). The richness of the full case system (10+ cases) is background context, not a puzzle requirement.
7. If the plural suffix is included, it must be shown to be obligatory (all plural nouns bear it) and distinct from case suffixes.
