

TARGET-LANGUAGE: Lezgian (lez)

CORE

1. Concept: Dependent marking in possessive NPs (source 81A-1)
   - Target-language realization: Lezgian marks the possessor with genitive case (WALS 24A-2); the possessed noun carries no possessive affix (WALS 57A-4). Predicative possession also uses genitive (WALS 117A-2).
   - Intended solver inference: In a possessive construction, the case-marked noun is the possessor, not the possessed. The possessed noun is unmarked.
   - Priority: HIGH

2. Concept: Noun before modifier (source 82A-1)
   - Target-language realization: NOT APPLICABLE. Lezgian has the opposite order: modifier-before-noun across all modifier types (AdjN 87A-1, DemN 88A-1, NumN 89A-1, RelN 90A-2, GenN 86A-1).
   - Decision: DROPPED. The specific source value does not hold in Lezgian. The feature (noun-modifier order) is captured as a target-specific concept below with the correct Lezgian value.

TARGET-SPECIFIC

1. Phenomenon: Ergative-absolutive alignment (WALS 98A-4, 99A-4)
   - Intended solver inference: In transitive clauses, the agent (A) bears ergative case; the patient (P) and intransitive subject (S) are unmarked (absolutive). The solver must identify which noun is the agent based on case marking, not position or verbal agreement.
   - Interaction with CORE: Parallel to dependent marking in possession — in both, the "higher" argument (A / possessor) is the one that is case-marked. This parallel is the puzzle's central structural insight.
   - Difficulty contribution: HIGH — this is the central typological inference.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

2. Phenomenon: No verbal person/number marking (WALS 102A-1, 100A-1, 103A-1)
   - Intended solver inference: The verb does not encode any information about its arguments' person or number. Grammatical roles must be recovered entirely from case marking on the noun phrases.
   - Interaction with CORE: Reinforces that case marking is the sole diagnostic for grammatical role, making the ergative-absolutive inference essential.
   - Difficulty contribution: HIGH — removes the "easy" strategy of reading agreement off the verb.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

3. Phenomenon: Case suffixes (WALS 51A-1); rich case system with 10+ cases (WALS 49A-8)
   - Intended solver inference: Case is marked by suffixes on the noun. The solver must identify which suffix corresponds to which case (ergative, genitive, dative, locative, etc.) from distributional evidence.
   - Interaction with CORE: Provides the morphological mechanism for both ergative alignment and genitive possession.
   - Difficulty contribution: HIGH — the solver must segment and identify multiple case suffixes.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

4. Phenomenon: SOV basic word order (WALS 81A-1, 82A-1, 83A-1)
   - Intended solver inference: The default clause order is Subject–Object–Verb. In transitive clauses, the ergative-marked NP (agent) precedes the absolutive NP (patient), which precedes the verb.
   - Interaction with CORE: Provides the structural frame within which case-marking patterns are observed.
   - Difficulty contribution: MEDIUM — helps the solver parse clause structure.
   - Priority: HIGH

5. Phenomenon: Modifier-before-noun order (WALS 87A-1, 88A-1, 89A-1, 90A-2, 86A-1)

   - Interaction with CORE: Complements the genitive-possession pattern (genitive precedes possessed noun).
   - Difficulty contribution: MEDIUM — helps the solver identify noun-phrase boundaries and heads.
   - Priority: HIGH

6. Phenomenon: Obligatory plural suffix on all nouns (WALS 33A-2, 34A-6)

INTERACTIONS

1. Ergative-absolutive + No verbal person marking + Case suffixes:
   - The solver must identify grammatical roles (A, S, P) solely from case marking on nouns because the verb provides no agreement information. This is the puzzle's central inference chain.

2. Ergative-absolutive + Dependent marking in possession (CORE):
   - In both transitive clauses and possessive NPs, the "higher" argument (A / possessor) is the one that is case-marked. This structural parallel allows the solver to use possession examples to build intuition for the ergative pattern, and vice versa.

3. Case suffixes + Obligatory plural:
   - Nouns may carry both a plural suffix and a case suffix. The solver must segment these correctly (e.g., Noun-Plural-Case vs. Noun-Case). This adds a layer of morphological parsing.

4. Tense + Aspect + Evidentiality + Negation + Interrogative (verbal morphology stack):
   - The verb may carry multiple suffixes encoding different categories. The solver must identify and segment each suffix. Suppletion (if included) complicates this by introducing stem alternations.

5. Modifier-before-noun + SOV + Postpositions:
   - The language exhibits a consistent "modifier-before-head" pattern at multiple levels: adjectives before nouns, objects before verbs, postpositions after NPs (but the postpositional phrase as a whole modifies the noun it follows). This creates a coherent structural profile the solver can use to parse complex sentences.

6. Genitive possessive + Ergative-absolutive:
   - The genitive case (used for possession) and the ergative case (used for transitive agents) are distinct suffixes. The solver must not conflate them. The genitive appears in pre-nominal position (GenN), while the ergative appears on the agent NP in clause-internal position.

7. Locational comparative + Case system:
   - The locative case has a non-spatial (comparative) function. The solver must recognize that a spatial case can be extended to a non-spatial domain, which is a common typological pattern but requires inference from the data.

CONSTRAINTS

1. The puzzle must provide sufficient examples for the solver to identify the ergative case suffix and distinguish it from other case suffixes (genitive, dative, locative, etc.). At minimum, the solver needs to see the ergative in multiple transitive clauses and the genitive in multiple possessive constructions.

2. Because Lezgian has no verbal person marking, the puzzle must NOT include any verbal agreement morphology. All grammatical role information must come from case marking on NPs.

3. The puzzle must make clear which nouns are singular vs. plural (via the obligatory plural suffix) so that the solver can correctly segment case suffixes from plural suffixes.

4. If suppletion is included, the puzzle must provide enough examples for the solver to recognize that different stems are in use, without making the stem identification arbitrary.

5. The modifier-before-noun order must be consistent across all examples (adjectives, demonstratives, numerals, genitives, relative clauses all precede the noun). Inconsistency would confuse the solver.

6. The puzzle should avoid using the passive, antipassive, or applicative constructions (none exist in Lezgian per WALS 107A-2, 108A-3, 109A-8). All transitive clauses must have an explicit ergative-marked agent.

7. If evidentiality is included, the puzzle must provide clear semantic context (e.g., "I heard that..." vs. "I saw that...") so the solver can map the formal marker to its semantic function.

8. The puzzle should not require the solver to know Lezgian-specific vocabulary. All content words should be glossed or their meaning inferable from context.
