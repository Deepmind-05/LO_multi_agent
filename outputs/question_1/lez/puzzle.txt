QUESTIONS

Translate the following between English and the target language.

**Q1.** Translate into English: *Nura lur kel sel*

**Q2.** Translate into English: *guza-l vur var*

**Q3.** Translate into English: *guza-l xan-n didə sel*

**Q4.** Translate into the target language: "The small woman's dogs see the men."

**Q5.** Translate into the target language: "Lemi's men take the big house."

**Q6.** Translate into the target language: "Nura sees the woman's small child."

**Q7.** Translate into the target language: "The big dogs drink Nura's water."

ANSWERS

**Q1.** Nura sees the small child.

**Q2.** The dogs drink the water.

**Q3.** The dogs see the man's women.

**Q4.** did-n lur guza-l xana sel

**Q5.** Lemi-n xana-l kur tar tur

**Q6.** Nura did-n lur kel sel

**Q7.** kur guza-l Nura-n vur var

SOLUTION

**Step 1: Identify the plural suffix and vowel harmony.**

Comparing singular and plural forms in the vocabulary table:
- xan (man) → xana (men): stem has back vowel /a/ → suffix is -a
- guz (dog) → guza (dogs): stem has back vowel /u/ → suffix is -a
- vur (water) → vura (waters): stem has back vowel /u/ → suffix is -a
- tar (house) → tara (houses): stem has back vowel /a/ → suffix is -a
- kel (child) → kelə (children): stem has front vowel /e/ → suffix is -ə
- did (woman) → didə (women): stem has front vowel /i/ → suffix is -ə

The plural suffix is conditioned by vowel harmony:
- Stems with back vowels (a, o, u) take **-a**
- Stems with front vowels (e, i, ə) take **-ə**

This is confirmed in the data: xana (2, 12, 16, 17), guza (19) are back-harmony plurals; kelə (1, 7, 11, 16), didə (4, 16) are front-harmony plurals.

**Step 2: Identify the genitive (possessive) suffix and dependent marking.**

In possessive noun phrases, the possessor bears a suffix and the possessed noun (head) is bare:
- Sentence 5: Lemi-n kel → "Lemi's child" (Lemi bears -n, kel is bare)
- Sentence 6: Nura-n guz → "Nura's dog" (Nura bears -n, guz is bare)
- Sentence 7: did-n kelə → "the woman's children" (did bears -n, kelə is bare)
- Sentence 8: xan-n kur guz → "the man's big dog" (xan bears -n, guz is bare)

The genitive suffix is **-n**, appearing on the **possessor** (dependent element). The head noun is unmarked. This is **dependent marking**.

**Step 3: Identify modifier-before-noun order.**

In every noun phrase, modifiers precede the head noun:
- Genitive possessor before head: Lemi-n kel (5), Nura-n guz (6), did-n kelə (7), xan-n kur guz (8)
- Adjective before head: kur guz (8, 17), lur kel (Q1)
- Genitive + adjective before head: xan-n kur guz (8), Nura-n kur guz (17)

The order is consistently: [possessor-gen] [adjective] [head noun].

**Step 4: Identify the ergative suffix and ergative-absolutive alignment.**

In transitive clauses with common-noun agents, the agent bears a suffix:
- Sentence 9: xan-l kel sel → "The man sees the child" (xan bears -l)
- Sentence 10: did-l guz tur → "The woman takes the dog" (did bears -l)
- Sentence 11: kelə-l vur var → "The children drink the water" (kelə bears -l)
- Sentence 12: xana-l did sel → "The men see the woman" (xana bears -l)
- Sentence 19: guza-l vur var → "The dogs drink the water" (guza bears -l)

The ergative suffix is **-l**, appearing on the **agent (A)** of transitive clauses. The patient (P) is unmarked.

In intransitive clauses, the subject is unmarked:
- Sentence 1: kelə kan → S = kelə (unmarked)
- Sentence 2: xana kan → S = xana (unmarked)
- Sentence 3: guz par → S = guz (unmarked)
- Sentence 4: didə lar → S = didə (unmarked)

The intransitive subject (S) is unmarked, just like the transitive patient (P). This gives: **S = P (absolutive, unmarked) ≠ A (ergative, marked with -l)**.

**Step 5: Identify ergative suppression on proper names.**

Sentences 13 and 14 present a critical contrast:
- Sentence 13: Lemi kel sel → "Lemi sees the child" (Lemi is the agent but bears NO -l)
- Sentence 14: Nura guz tur → "Nura takes the dog" (Nura is the agent but bears NO -l)

Compare with common-noun agents in sentences 9–12, 19, which all bear -l. The proper names Lemi and Nura are unmarked in the agent position.

Sentence 18 confirms that proper names are also unmarked in the patient position:
- Sentence 18: kel-l Nura sel → "The child sees Nura" (Nura is the patient, unmarked)

Since the patient is always unmarked (absolutive), sentence 18 is expected. The critical observation is from sentences 13–14: proper names in the **agent** position lack the ergative suffix that common nouns receive. The rule is: **the ergative suffix -l is suppressed on proper names.**

Word order (SOV: Agent–Patient–Verb) resolves the ambiguity of which unmarked NP is the agent when both are proper names or when a proper name is the agent.

**Step 6: Confirm no verbal agreement.**

Verbs are invariant regardless of argument number or identity:
- kan: with kelə (1), xana (2), Lemi-n kel (5), xan-n kur guz (8) — all identical
- sel: with xan-l kel (9), xana-l did (12), Lemi kel (13), Lemi-n kel-l did (15), did-n kelə-l xana (16), kel-l Nura (18), guza-l xan-n didə (Q3) — all identical
- var: with kelə-l vur (11), guza-l vur (19) — all identical
- tur: with did-l guz (10), Nura guz (14), xana-l Nura-n kur guz (17) — all identical

Grammatical roles are identified solely through case marking on noun phrases.

**Step 7: Confirm suffix stacking order.**

When a plural noun bears a case suffix, the order is: stem + plural + case:
- kelə-l (11, 16): kel + -ə (pl) + -l (erg)
- xana-l (12, 17): xan + -a (pl) + -l (erg)
- guza-l (19): guz + -a (pl) + -l (erg)
- did-n (7, 16): did + -n (gen) [no plural here, but the pattern holds]

**Solving the questions:**

**Q1.** Nura lur kel sel:
- Nura: proper name in first (agent) position → agent, ergative suppressed
- lur kel: adjective + bare noun → "the small child" (patient, absolutive)
- sel: see
→ "Nura sees the small child."

**Q2.** guza-l vur var:
- guza-l: guz + -a (back pl) + -l (erg) → "the dogs" (agent)
- vur: bare noun → "the water" (patient, absolutive)
- var: drink
→ "The dogs drink the water."

**Q3.** guza-l xan-n didə sel:
- guza-l: guz + -a (back pl) + -l (erg) → "the dogs" (agent)
- xan-n: xan + -n (gen) → "the man's" (possessor)
- didə: did + -ə (front pl), bare → "the women" (head of possessive NP, patient, absolutive)
- sel: see
→ "The dogs see the man's women."

**Q4.** "The small woman's dogs see the men."
- Agent: "the small woman's dogs" = did-n (gen possessor) + lur (adjective) + guza-l (guz + -a back pl + -l erg) = did-n lur guza-l
- Patient: "the men" = xana (xan + -a back pl, bare)
- Verb: sel
- Order: A + P + V → **did-n lur guza-l xana sel**

**Q5.** "Lemi's men take the big house."
- Agent: "Lemi's men" = Lemi-n (gen possessor) + xana-l (xan + -a back pl + -l erg) = Lemi-n xana-l
- Patient: "the big house" = kur (adjective) + tar (bare head) = kur tar
- Verb: tur
- Order: A + P + V → **Lemi-n xana-l kur tar tur**

**Q6.** "Nura sees the woman's small child."
- Agent: "Nura" = Nura (proper name, ergative suppressed)
- Patient: "the woman's small child" = did-n (gen possessor) + lur (adjective) + kel (bare head) = did-n lur kel
- Verb: sel
- Order: A + P + V → **Nura did-n lur kel sel**

**Q7.** "The big dogs drink Nura's water."
- Agent: "the big dogs" = kur (adjective) + guza-l (guz + -a back pl + -l erg) = kur guza-l
- Patient: "Nura's water" = Nura-n (gen possessor) + vur (bare head) = Nura-n vur
- Verb: var
- Order: A + P + V → **kur guza-l Nura-n vur var**

DIFFICULTY

Hard. The solver must integrate multiple interacting systems with non-obvious constraints: (1) identify a vowel-harmony-conditioned plural suffix (-a after back-vowel stems, -ə after front-vowel stems) from the data; (2) discover dependent marking in possessive NPs (genitive -n on possessor, head unmarked); (3) recognize consistent modifier-before-noun order; (4) infer ergative-absolutive alignment by contrasting transitive and intransitive clauses; (5) identify the ergative suppression rule for proper names — a non-obvious exception that creates ambiguity requiring resolution via word order; (6) recognize the absence of verbal agreement; (7) apply suffix stacking (stem + plural + case) in production. The production questions (Q4–Q7) require simultaneous application of at least four systems, with Q4 and Q7 being the most demanding as they require vowel-harmony-correct plural formation, ergative stacking, genitive marking, and adjective placement all at once. Q6 specifically tests the ergative suppression rule in production, requiring the solver to correctly leave a proper name unmarked in the agent position.
