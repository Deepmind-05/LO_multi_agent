

TARGET-LANGUAGE

Supyire (sup), Niger-Congo > Senufo

CONSTRUCTION RATIONALE

1. **Design the noun class system (5 classes).** Establish five noun classes, each with a distinct set of allomorphs that appear in four positions: (a) plural suffix, (b) definite suffix, (c) possessive suffix, and (d) pre-nominal agreement prefix on post-nominal modifiers (adjectives and numerals). Additionally, assign each class a distinct 3rd-person singular pronoun form. The classes should be assigned by a mix of semantic and formal criteria (e.g., one class for humans, one for natural features, one for abstracta, one for body parts/small objects, one for plants/cultivated items), but the semantic assignments must NOT be stated to the solver. The solver will infer class membership purely from which marker set a noun takes. Ensure that all five marker sets are phonologically distinct from one another in every position so that class identification is unambiguous. Choose at least two nouns per class for the data set (10+ nouns total) so the solver can confirm each class by seeing the pattern repeat.

2. **Fix the noun-internal morphological order.** Establish a fixed linear order for suffixes on the noun stem: stem + plural suffix + definite suffix + possessive suffix. All three suffixes are class-specific (their form depends on the noun's class). The plural suffix is obligatory whenever the noun is plural (no unmarked plurals exist). The definite suffix is optional (marks definiteness). The possessive suffix is optional (marks that the noun is possessed). This ordering must be consistent across all data. The solver will need to determine this order by seeing nouns in combinations: singular-indefinite (bare stem), plural-indefinite (stem + Pl), singular-definite (stem + Def), plural-definite (stem + Pl + Def), and possessed forms (stem + [Pl] + [Def] + Poss). Provide at least one example of each combination for at least two different classes so the solver can confirm the order is invariant.

3. **Design the modifier system with mixed order.** Establish that the noun phrase has two modifier positions: a pre-nominal position (immediately before the noun stem) and a post-nominal position (immediately after the noun's internal morphology). Assign the following modifiers to each position:
   - Pre-nominal: demonstrative (e.g., "this/that") and possessor (genitive noun or pronoun).
   - Post-nominal: adjective and numeral.
   
   Post-nominal modifiers (adjectives, numerals) carry a class-specific agreement prefix that matches the noun's class. Pre-nominal modifiers (demonstrative, possessor) are class-invariant (they do not change form based on the noun's class). This asymmetry is important: it gives the solver a clear diagnostic for identifying which modifiers are pre-nominal vs. post-nominal (the post-nominal ones change form, the pre-nominal ones do not). The full NP template is:
   
   [Dem] [Possessor] N-stem [Pl] [Def] [Poss] [Adj-prefix + Adj-stem] [Num-prefix + Num-stem]
   
   (All bracketed elements are optional except N-stem; Pl is obligatory if plural; Poss is obligatory if possessed.)

4. **Design the possessive construction (head marking).** The possessive relationship is marked on the possessed noun (the head) via the possessive suffix described in step 2. The possessor is a free form (pronoun, proper noun, or common noun) that appears in the pre-nominal position. The possessor carries NO special possessive morphology (no genitive case marker, no possessive prefix). The possessive suffix on the head is class-specific (reflects the class of the possessed noun, not the possessor). There is no possessive classification (the suffix does not encode features of the possessor). This means:
   - "the child's house" = [child] [house + Poss(class of house)]
   - "my house" = [1sg-pronoun] [house + Poss(class of house)]
   - "the house of the child" is NOT the structure; the possessor always precedes.
   
   The solver must infer that the possessive suffix is on the head (not the possessor) by observing that the possessor form is identical whether it appears in a possessive construction or in a non-possessive context (e.g., as a subject or object).

5. **Design the clausal structure (SOV with postpositions).** Establish that the basic clause order is Subject – Object – Verb. The verb is uninflected for person/number in this design (to keep the focus on nominal morphology); instead, person is expressed by free pronouns or by the noun itself. Locative and directional complements are expressed by postpositions (the noun precedes the postposition, e.g., "house in" = "in the house"). This postpositional order is typologically consistent with the post-nominal modifier order and the SOV clause order, providing a cross-confirmation pattern the solver can use to verify their analysis. Provide at least two postpositions (e.g., one for "in/at" and one for "to/toward") in the data.

6. **Design the negation system (optional double negation).** Establish two negative particles: a preverbal particle (appearing between S and O, or between O and V) and a postverbal particle (appearing after V). Negation can be expressed by either particle alone or by both simultaneously. The double form is optional (not obligatory). The data must include: (a) at least two examples with only the preverbal particle, (b) at least two examples with only the postverbal particle, (c) at least two examples with both particles, and (d) at least two affirmative examples with no negation. This allows the solver to infer that both particles are individually sufficient for negation and that their co-occurrence is optional. The preverbal particle's position (between S and O, or between O and V) should be fixed and consistent in the data so the solver can identify its slot.

7. **Construct the data set in progressive complexity.** Organize the sentences (or glossed examples) in a sequence that introduces systems gradually:
   
   **Phase A – Basic nouns and number (sentences 1–4):** Present simple noun phrases in isolation or as subjects/objects of minimal clauses. Show singular and plural forms of nouns from at least three different classes. The solver should be able to identify: (a) the plural suffix and its class-specific allomorphs, (b) the existence of multiple noun classes (by noticing that different nouns take different plural suffixes), and (c) the basic SOV clause order.
   
   **Phase B – Definiteness (sentences 5–7):** Introduce the definite suffix. Show the same noun in definite and indefinite forms. Show definite + plural combinations. The solver should identify the definite suffix, its class-specific allomorphs, and its position relative to the plural suffix (confirming the order from step 2).
   
   **Phase C – Post-nominal modifiers (sentences 8–11):** Introduce adjectives and numerals. Show them following the noun with class-specific agreement prefixes. Show at least two different adjectives and two different numerals modifying nouns from at least three classes. The solver should identify: (a) that adjectives and numerals are post-nominal, (b) that they carry class-specific prefixes, and (c) that this provides additional evidence for the noun class system.
   
   **Phase D – Pre-nominal modifiers and possession (sentences 12–16):** Introduce demonstratives and possessive constructions. Show a demonstrative preceding a noun. Show a possessive construction with a pronoun possessor and a noun possessor. Show the possessive suffix on the head noun. The solver should identify: (a) that demonstratives are pre-nominal, (b) that the possessor precedes the possessed noun, (c) that the possessive suffix is on the head (not the possessor), and (d) that the possessive suffix is class-specific.
   
   **Phase E – Full NP combinations (sentences 17–20):** Present noun phrases that combine multiple elements: demonstrative + possessor + noun + plural + definite + possessive + adjective + numeral. These "maximal" NPs allow the solver to verify the complete template and confirm the linear order of all elements. Include at least one example from each of the five noun classes in this phase to ensure all class marker sets are fully attested.
   
   **Phase F – Clausal context with postpositions and negation (sentences 21–26):** Embed the NPs in full clauses. Show postpositions. Show the negation system (single and double). Show the preverbal negative's position. The solver should identify: (a) postpositional case marking, (b) the two negative particles and their positions, and (c) the optionality of double negation.
   
   **Phase G – Complex sentences (sentences 27–30):** Combine multiple systems in single sentences: e.g., a possessive NP with a demonstrative and adjective as subject, a postpositional phrase as object, a negated verb, and a numeral. These sentences test the solver's ability to integrate all rules simultaneously.

8. **Design the questions with progressive difficulty.** Create 8–10 questions that test the inferred rules in increasing combinations:
   
   - **Q1 (easy, target→English):** A simple SOV sentence with a plural noun and a post-nominal adjective. Tests: SOV order, plural suffix, post-nominal adjective position, class agreement.
   
   - **Q2 (easy, target→English):** A sentence with a possessive NP (pronoun possessor + possessed noun with possessive suffix) and a postposition. Tests: head-marking possessive, pre-nominal possessor, postposition.
   
   - **Q3 (medium, target→English):** A sentence with a demonstrative + possessed noun + adjective + numeral, all in one NP, in an SOV clause. Tests: full NP template, mixed modifier order, class agreement on post-nominal modifiers.
   
   - **Q4 (medium, target→English):** A negated sentence (double negation) with a definite plural noun and a postpositional phrase. Tests: negation optionality, definite + plural order, postposition.
   
   - **Q5 (medium, English→target):** Translate a sentence requiring the solver to select the correct class-specific plural suffix, definite suffix, and adjective prefix for a noun whose class must be identified from context (the noun appears earlier in the data with its markers). Tests: class identification, noun-internal morphology order, post-nominal modifier agreement.
   
   - **Q6 (medium-hard, English→target):** Translate a sentence with a possessive NP (noun possessor + possessed noun) modified by an adjective, in a clause with a postposition. The solver must: place the possessor before the head, add the correct possessive suffix (class of the possessed noun), place the adjective after the noun with the correct prefix, and use the correct postposition. Tests: possessive structure, modifier order, class agreement, postposition.
   
   - **Q7 (hard, English→target):** Translate a sentence with a demonstrative + possessive NP + adjective + numeral, in a negated clause (the solver must choose whether to use single or double negation—accept either). Tests: full NP template, possessive head-marking, class agreement, negation.
   
   - **Q8 (hard, English→target):** Translate a sentence that requires the solver to identify the noun class of a noun that has NOT been seen with all its markers in the data (only seen in one or two contexts), then apply the correct class-specific forms for plural, definite, possessive, and adjective agreement. This tests whether the solver has truly internalized the class system or is merely pattern-matching.
   
   - **Q9 (hard, analysis question):** Present a novel NP (not seen in the data) and ask the solver to identify the noun class, number, definiteness, possession, and modifiers, explaining their reasoning. This tests metalinguistic awareness and the ability to apply the full system to new data.
   
   - **Q10 (optional, very hard, English→target):** A sentence combining all systems: a complex NP (demonstrative + possessive + plural + definite + adjective + numeral) as subject, a postpositional phrase as object, double negation, and a second clause (if a conjunction is included). Tests full integration.

9. **Verify solvability and disambiguation.** Before finalizing the puzzle, check the following:
   
   - **Class identifiability:** For each of the five classes, confirm that at least two nouns are attested with enough marker combinations (plural, definite, possessive, adjective agreement) that the solver can unambiguously group them. No two classes should share any allomorph in any position.
   
   - **Modifier order disambiguation:** Confirm that no sentence in the data could be reanalyzed with a different modifier order. Specifically: (a) the demonstrative must never appear after the noun; (b) the adjective must never appear before the noun; (c) the possessor must never appear after the possessed noun. If a noun and an adjective could be confused (e.g., if an adjective looks like a noun), ensure that context (e.g., the presence of a class-specific prefix on the adjective) disambiguates.
   
   - **Possessive structure disambiguation:** Confirm that the possessive suffix cannot be reanalyzed as a plural or definite suffix. The possessive suffix must be phonologically distinct from both the plural and definite suffixes in every class. Additionally, confirm that the possessor is never marked (no genitive case on the possessor) so the solver cannot confuse the possessive suffix with a case marker on the possessor.
   
   - **Noun-internal order disambiguation:** Confirm that the order Pl > Def > Poss is the only order consistent with the data. If a noun appears with both Pl and Def, the solver must be able to determine which is which (they should be phonologically distinct). If a noun appears with Pl, Def, and Poss, the three suffixes must be mutually distinguishable.
   
   - **Negation optionality:** Confirm that the data contains both single-negation and double-negation examples, so the solver can infer that both particles are individually sufficient and that co-occurrence is optional. If only double negation appeared, the solver might incorrectly infer that both are obligatory.
   
   - **No rule is stated:** Verify that no gloss, label, or metadata in the puzzle directly states any rule the solver is meant to discover (e.g., do not label a suffix as "PL" or "DEF" in the data; present the forms without morphological labels).
   
   - **Question coverage:** Confirm that every rule tested in the questions is supported by at least one example in the data. Confirm that the two CORE concepts (mixed modifier order and head-marking possession) are each tested in at least three questions and are always tested in combination with at least one other rule.

10. **Ensure typological consistency and avoid contradictions.** Verify that all design choices are mutually consistent:
    - SOV clause order is consistent with post-nominal modifiers (NAdj, NNum) and postpositions.
    - Head-marking possession is consistent with the possessor being a free pre-nominal element (the head bears the marker; the dependent is unmarked).
    - The noun class system is non-sex-based (no class is defined by biological sex; classes are based on semantic categories like animate/inanimate, natural/artificial, etc.).
    - The plural suffix is obligatory (no zero-plural exists), which means every plural noun in the data must show the suffix—this is a strong diagnostic for the solver.
    - The definite suffix is optional (both definite and indefinite NPs appear in the data), so the solver must distinguish the definite suffix from the plural suffix and possessive suffix.
    - The optional double negation does not interact with the noun class system (negation is a clause-level phenomenon independent of nominal morphology), keeping the systems separable for the solver.
