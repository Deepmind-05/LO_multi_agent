Supyire is a Senufo language spoken in Côte d'Ivoire. Below are sentences in Supyire with English translations.

| bɛ | be / exist |
| ɣɔ | flow |
| pɔ | fall |

**Note on homophones:** The form ɲɛ appears both as a noun ("hand") and as a demonstrative ("this"); the form ɲɔ appears both as a noun ("mountain") and as a demonstrative ("that"). In each sentence, the syntactic position determines which reading is intended: a demonstrative appears immediately before a noun phrase, while a noun appears in subject or object position. Similarly, the postposition ɔ ("in/at") is a separate word following a noun, while the sequence -ɔ may appear as a suffix attached to a noun stem. The same distinction applies to ɛ (postposition "to/toward" as a separate word vs. -ɛ as a suffix attached to a noun stem).

Q9. The noun phrase means "That your (sg) three big stones."
- Noun: pɛ "stone"
- Class: IV (identified by the plural suffix -o, definite suffix -ɔ, possessive suffix -õ, and agreement prefix ɣ- on the post-nominal modifiers)
- Number: plural (suffix -o)
- Definiteness: definite (suffix -ɔ)
- Possession: possessed; possessor is mɛ "you (sg)" (marked by the possessive suffix -õ on the head)
- Demonstrative: ɲɔ "that" (pre-nominal)
- Post-nominal modifiers: ɣ-bɔ "big" (adjective) and ɣ-sɛ "three" (numeral), both with Class IV agreement prefix ɣ-
- Reasoning: The form pɛoɔõ is segmented as pɛ + o + ɔ + õ. The suffix -o matches the plural marker of Class IV (cf. ɲɛo in sentence 33 "hands exist"). The suffix -ɔ matches the definite marker of Class IV (cf. ɲɛɔ in sentence 29). The suffix -õ matches the possessive marker of Class IV (cf. ɲɛɔõ in sentence 29). The prefix ɣ- on the following modifiers matches the Class IV agreement prefix (cf. ɣ-bɔ in sentence 29, ɣ-kɔ in sentence 40). The pre-nominal element mɛ is the 2sg pronoun functioning as possessor. The pre-nominal element ɲɔ is the demonstrative "that."

SOLUTION

**1. Noun Class System**

Supyire has five noun classes. Each class is characterized by a unique set of allomorphs appearing in four morphological positions plus a distinct 3rd-person pronoun:

| Class | Plural suffix | Definite suffix | Possessive suffix | Agreement prefix | 3rd person pronoun |
|-------|:-----------:|:-------------:|:---------------:|:--------------:|:-----------------:|
| I     | -ɛ          | -ɔ            | -ɛ̃             | k-             | ɔ́                |
| II    | -i          | -u            | -ĩ              | ɲ-             | í                 |
| III   | -a          | -e            | -ã              | w-             | á                 |
| IV    | -o          | -ɔ            | -õ              | ɣ-             | ó                 |
| V     | -u          | -i            | -ũ              | s-             | ú                 |

**Class membership (inferred from data):**
- Class I: tɔ "child", mɔ "person"
- Class II: sɔ "river", ɲɔ "mountain"
- Class III: bal "word", kɛl "path"
- Class IV: ɲɛ "hand", pɛ "stone"
- Class V: ɲu "tree", sɛ "plant"

**Evidence for class identification:**
- Class I: tɔɛ (2), mɔɛ (2,6) show Pl -ɛ; tɔɔ (5), mɔɔ (6,14) show Def -ɔ; tɔɛ̃ (13,14) shows Poss -ɛ̃; k-bɔ (8,12), k-tɔ (10) show Agr k-; ɔ́ (35) is the pronoun.
- Class II: sɔi (4,11) shows Pl -i; sɔu (7,18) shows Def -u; sɔĩ (16), sɔiuĩ (18) show Poss -ĩ; ɲ-bɔ (9,15), ɲ-sɛ (11) show Agr ɲ-; í (36) is the pronoun.
- Class III: bala (19,30) shows Pl -a; bale (32) shows Def -e; balã (31) shows Poss -ã; w-bɔ (19,30), w-tɔ (19,30), w-kɔ (41) show Agr w-; á (37) is the pronoun.
- Class IV: ɲɛo (33) shows Pl -o; ɲɛɔ (in ɲɛɔõ, 29) shows Def -ɔ; ɲɛɔõ (29) shows Poss -õ; ɣ-bɔ (29), ɣ-kɔ (40) show Agr ɣ-; ó (38) is the pronoun.
- Class V: ɲuu (20) shows Pl -u; ɲui (34) shows Def -i; sɛũ (30) shows Poss -ũ; s-bɔ (20), s-sɛ (20) show Agr s-; ú (39) is the pronoun.

**2. Noun-Internal Morphological Order**

The linear order of suffixes on the noun stem is fixed:

**stem + Plural + Definite + Possessive**

All three suffixes are class-specific. The plural suffix is obligatory when the noun is plural. The definite suffix is optional (marks definiteness). The possessive suffix is optional (marks that the noun is possessed by a preceding possessor).

**Evidence for ordering:**
- Pl + Def: tɔɛɔ (6) = tɔ + ɛ + ɔ; mɔɛɔ (6) = mɔ + ɛ + ɔ
- Pl + Def + Poss: tɔɛɔɛ̃ (17) = tɔ + ɛ + ɔ + ɛ̃; sɔiuĩ (18) = sɔ + i + u + ï
- Def + Poss: ɲɛɔõ (29) = ɲɛ + ɔ + õ

**3. Noun Phrase Structure (Mixed Modifier Order)**

The full NP template is:

**[Demonstrative] [Possessor] N-stem [Pl] [Def] [Poss] [Adj-prefix + Adj-stem] [Num-prefix + Num-stem]**

- **Pre-nominal modifiers** (class-invariant): demonstrative (ɲɛ "this", ɲɔ "that") and possessor (pronoun or noun). These do not change form based on the noun's class.
- **Post-nominal modifiers** (class-specific): adjectives and numerals. These carry an agreement prefix matching the noun's class.

**Adjectives:** -bɔ "big", -sɔ "small", -kɔ "good"
**Numerals:** -tɔ "two", -sɛ "three"

**Evidence for mixed order:**
- Demonstrative is pre-nominal: ɲɛ tɔ (12), ɲɔ sɔ (15), ɲɛ bala (19)
- Possessor is pre-nominal: nɛ tɔɛ̃ (13), mɔɔ tɔɛ̃ (14), mɔɔ sɔiuĩ (18)
- Adjective is post-nominal: tɔ k-bɔ (8), sɔ ɲ-bɔ (9), ɲɛɔõ ɣ-bɔ (29)
- Numeral is post-nominal: tɔɛ k-tɔ (10), sɔi ɲ-sɛ (11), bala w-tɔ (19)

**4. Possessive Construction (Head Marking)**

The possessive relationship is marked on the possessed noun (the head) via the class-specific possessive suffix. The possessor is a free form (pronoun or noun) that appears in the pre-nominal position. The possessor carries no special possessive morphology.

**Structure:** [Possessor] [Head + (Pl) + (Def) + Poss]

**Evidence:**
- nɛ tɔɛ̃ (13): "my child" — possessor nɛ (1sg) precedes head tɔ with Poss suffix -ɛ̃
- mɔɔ tɔɛ̃ (14): "the person's child" — possessor mɔɔ (definite noun) precedes head tɔ with Poss suffix -ɛ̃
- nɛ sɔĩ (16): "my river" — possessor nɛ precedes head sɔ with Poss suffix -ĩ
- mɔɔ sɔiuĩ (18): "the person's rivers" — possessor mɔɔ precedes head sɔ with Pl+Def+Poss
- mɔɔ ɲɛɔõ (29): "the person's hand" — possessor mɔɔ precedes head ɲɛ with Def+Poss
- mɔɔ sɛũ (30): "the person's plant" — possessor mɔɔ precedes head sɛ with Poss

The possessor form is identical whether it appears as a possessor or in other clause positions (e.g., mɔɔ in 14 as possessor vs. mɔɔ in 6 as object), confirming that the possessor is unmarked.

**5. Clausal Structure (SVO with Preverbal Adjuncts)**

The basic transitive clause order is **Subject – Verb – Object**. The verb is uninflected for person or number. Intransitive clauses and clauses with postpositional phrases follow the order **S [PP] V**, where the postpositional phrase is a preverbal adjunct.

**Postpositions:**
- ɔ "in / at" (locative)
- ɛ "to / toward" (directional)

The noun precedes the postposition (postpositional order).

**Evidence:**
- SVO (transitive): tɔ dɛ mɔ (1) = S V O; tɔɛ dɛ mɔɛ (2) = S V O
- S PP V (intransitive with adjunct): tɔ sɔ ɛ kɔ (21) = S PP V; mɔ ɲu ɔ bɛ (22) = S PP V
- S V (intransitive): sɔ ɣɔ (3) = S V; ɲui bɛ (34) = S V

**6. Negation System (Optional Double Negation)**

Two negative particles exist:
- **ɔɔ** (preverbal): appears immediately before the verb.
- **ɛ̃** (postverbal): appears at the end of the clause (after the verb and any object).

Either particle alone is sufficient to express negation. Both may co-occur (optional double negation).

**Evidence:**
- Preverbal only: tɔ ɔɔ dɛ mɔ (23); ɲu ɔɔ bɛ (26)
- Postverbal only: mɔ dɛ tɔ ɛ̃ (24); tɔ dɛ mɔ ɛ̃ (27)
- Both: sɔ ɔɔ ɣɔ ɛ̃ (25); ɲɔ mɔɔ ɲɛɔõ ɣ-bɔ ɔɔ sɔ ɔ bɛ ɛ̃ (29); ɲɛ bala w-bɔ w-tɔ ɔɔ mɔɔ sɛũ ɔ bɛ ɛ̃ (30)

The presence of both single-negation and double-negation examples confirms that co-occurrence is optional.
