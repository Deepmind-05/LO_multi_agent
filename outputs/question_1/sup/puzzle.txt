SOLUTION

**Noun Class System**

Supyire has a 4-class noun system. Every head noun carries two obligatory suffixes in a fixed order: a definite article suffix followed by a class suffix. The forms are class-specific:

| Class | Semantic domain | Definite suffix | Class suffix | Possessive suffix |
|-------|----------------|----------------|-------------|-------------------|
| 1 | humans/kinship | -na | -ba | -ta |
| 2 | animals | -lo | -go | -ko |
| 3 | plants | -mi | -si | -ri |
| 4 | other | -be | -de | -pe |

Evidence: Sentences 1–9 show four distinct suffix pairs. Sentences 1 and 4 share -na + -ba (Class 1); sentences 2 and 6 share -lo + -go (Class 2); sentences 3 and 7 share -mi + -si (Class 3); sentences 5, 8, and 9 share -be + -de (Class 4). The suffixes are obligatory: every head noun in every sentence carries them.

**Non-Semantic Class Assignment**

Class assignment is not purely semantic. While Classes 1–3 show relatively consistent semantic domains (humans, animals, plants), Class 4 includes semantically heterogeneous nouns (house, water, stone). The noun "stone" (sɔgɔ) is a natural object like "tree" (kɔrɔ) and "grass" (mɔrɔ), yet it belongs to Class 4 rather than Class 3. The solver must rely on morphological evidence (the actual suffixes observed in the data) rather than semantic expectation to determine class membership. Sentence 9 (sɔgɔbede) and sentence 14 (sɔgɔpebede) provide the morphological evidence that stone is Class 4.

**Suffix Order**

The order on the head noun is: root + possessive + definite + class.

Evidence: Compare sentence 1 (kɔla + na + ba = kɔlanaba, "the child") with sentence 10 (kɔla + ta + na + ba = kɔlatanaba, "Ama's child"). The possessive suffix -ta is inserted between the root and the definite suffix, giving the order root + possessive + definite + class.

**Possessive Head-Marking**

In a possessive noun phrase, the possessed noun (head) carries a class-specific possessive suffix, while the possessor appears in its bare form (no suffixes of any kind).

Evidence: In sentence 10, "Ama" is bare and "kɔlatanaba" carries the possessive suffix -ta. In sentence 12, "kɔla" (child) is bare as possessor and "tɛmɛpebede" (house) carries the possessive suffix -pe. The possessor never changes form regardless of whether it is a proper noun (Ama) or a common noun (kɔla, tɛnɛ).

**Mixed Modifier Order**

Modifier order is not uniform. Demonstratives and possessors precede the head noun; adjectives and numerals follow it.

The full NP structure is: [Demonstrative] [Possessor] [Noun + suffixes] [Adjective] [Numeral]

Evidence: Sentence 15 shows the demonstrative kɛ before the noun. Sentence 16 shows the adjective bɔgɔ after the noun. Sentence 17 shows the numeral tɛn after the noun. Sentence 19 combines all: kɛ (demonstrative) + Ama (possessor) + tɛmɛpebede (head) + bɔgɔ (adjective).

**Clause Structure**

Basic word order is SOV. Negation (mɛ) is preverbal. The conjunction ma ("but") links clauses. Postpositions follow the noun (e.g., tɛmɛbede nɔ = "in the house"). The progressive aspect is marked by the verbal suffix -a.

Evidence: All sentences show S V order. Sentence 21 shows mɛ before the verb. Sentence 22 shows ma between clauses. Sentence 23 shows the postposition nɔ after the noun. Sentence 20 shows the aspect suffix -a on the verb.

**Question Solutions**

Q1: kɔla + na + ba (Class 1: definite + class) + dɔ (be) → "The child is (here)."

Q2: Ama (bare possessor) + kɔla + ta + na + ba (Class 1: possessive + definite + class) + dɔ → "Ama's child is (here)."

Q3: kɛ (this) + Ama (bare possessor) + tɛmɛ + pe + be + de (Class 4: possessive + definite + class) + bɔgɔ (big) + dɔ → "This big house of Ama is (here)."

Q4: kɔrɔ + mi + si (Class 3: definite + class) + bɔgɔ (big) + tɛn (two) + dɔ → "Two big trees are (here)."

Q5: tɛnɛ (woman, bare possessor) + sɔgɔ + pe + be + de (Class 4: possessive + definite + class) + bɔgɔ (big) + dɔ → tɛnɛ sɔgɔpebede bɔgɔ dɔ. Note: "stone" is Class 4 (evidenced by sentences 9 and 14), not Class 3, despite being a natural object. A solver who assigns it to Class 3 by semantic expectation would incorrectly produce *tɛnɛ sɔgɔrismi bɔgɔ dɔ.

Q6: kɛ (this) + Kofi (bare possessor) + tɛmɛ + pe + be + de (Class 4: possessive + definite + class) + tɛlɛ (small) + dɔ → kɛ Kofi tɛmɛpebede tɛlɛ dɔ

Q7: kɔla + na + ba (Class 1: definite + class) + kɔrɔ + mi + si (Class 3: definite + class) + bɔgɔ (big) + tɛn (two) + mɛ (not) + fɛ (see) → kɔlanaba kɔrɔmisi bɔgɔ tɛn mɛ fɛ

Q8: sɛ (that) + Ama (bare possessor) + tɛmɛ + pe + be + de (Class 4: possessive + definite + class) + bɔgɔ (big) + kɔrɔ + mi + si (Class 3: definite + class) + nɔ (in) + dɔ (be) + ma (but) + kɔla + na + ba (Class 1: definite + class) + kɔ + a (go + progressive) → sɛ Ama tɛmɛpebede bɔgɔ kɔrɔmisi nɔ dɔ ma kɔlanaba kɔa
