

TARGET-LANGUAGE

Supyire (sup), Niger-Congo > Senufo

CORE

1. Noun-modifier order (from source 82A-1, adapted)
   - Target-language realization: Mixed modifier order. Genitive precedes noun (86A-1: GenN), demonstrative precedes noun (88A-1: DemN), but adjective follows noun (87A-2: NAdj) and numeral follows noun (89A-2: NNum).
   - Intended solver inference: Solver identifies that modifier order is not uniform; some modifiers (genitive, demonstrative) are pre-nominal while others (adjective, numeral) are post-nominal. The solver must classify which type of modifier falls in which position.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

2. Possessive marking (from source 81A-1, adapted)
   - Target-language realization: Head marking (24A-1). The possessed noun (head) hosts the possessive construction. No possessive affixes on pronouns (57A-4); no possessive classification (59A-1). The possessive marker is a separate element associated with the head noun, followed by the possessor.
   - Intended solver inference: Solver identifies that the possessive relationship is marked on the head (possessed noun), not on the dependent (possessor). This is the opposite of the source language's dependent marking. The solver must recognize the structural pattern: possessed_noun + possessive_element + possessor.
   - Priority: HIGH

TARGET-SPECIFIC

1. Noun class (gender) system with 5+ classes
   - Phenomenon: Five or more genders (30A-5), non-sex-based (31A-3), assigned by both semantic and formal criteria (32A-3). Gender distinctions appear in 3rd person pronouns in both singular and non-singular (44A-2).
   - Intended solver inference: Solver identifies a multi-class noun system from agreement patterns (e.g., different forms of modifiers, possessive markers, or pronouns associated with different nouns). The solver must classify nouns into classes based on the evidence.
   - Interaction with CORE: Gender likely affects the form of post-nominal modifiers (adjectives, numerals) and possibly the possessive element, creating a system where the solver must track class-specific allomorphy.
   - Difficulty contribution: HIGH — requires systematic classification and pattern recognition across multiple data points.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

2. Obligatory plural suffix on all nouns
   - Phenomenon: Plural marked by suffix (33A-2), obligatory in all nouns (34A-6).
   - Intended solver inference: Solver identifies a consistent plural marker and recognizes its obligatoriness (every plural noun shows it; no plural is unmarked).
   - Interaction with CORE: Plural suffix may be class-specific (different allomorphs per noun class), interacting with the gender system. The position of the plural suffix relative to other noun-internal morphology (definite marker, possessive element) creates ordering constraints.
   - Difficulty contribution: MEDIUM — the pattern is regular but its interaction with gender adds complexity.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

3. Definite affix on noun
   - Phenomenon: Definiteness marked by affix on the noun (37A-3).
   - Intended solver inference: Solver identifies a definite marker and distinguishes it from the plural marker and any possessive element.
   - Interaction with CORE: The definite affix occupies a specific position in the noun's internal morphology, interacting with plural suffix and potentially the possessive element. The solver must determine the linear order of noun-internal markers.
   - Difficulty contribution: MEDIUM — adds a layer to the nominal morphology that must be parsed alongside gender, number, and possession.
   - Priority: HIGH

4. SOV word order with postpositions
   - Phenomenon: SOV (81A-1), SV (82A-1), OV (83A-1), postpositions (85A-1), OVX (84A-5).
   - Intended solver inference: Solver identifies the basic clause word order as SOV and recognizes postpositions (as opposed to prepositions).
   - Interaction with CORE: Provides the clausal context in which NPs (with their internal modifier order and possessive structure) are embedded. The OV order is consistent with the post-nominal modifier pattern (NAdj, NNum), creating a typological harmony that the solver can note.
   - Difficulty contribution: LOW-MEDIUM — relatively straightforward but provides essential context.
   - Priority: HIGH

5. Optional double negation
   - Phenomenon: Negative particle (112A-2), both preverbal (143E-1) and postverbal (143F-1) negative words, optional double negation (143A-15, 144A-19). Pattern: S(Neg)OVNeg (144O-2).
   - Intended solver inference: Solver identifies that negation can be expressed by a single particle (preverbal or postverbal) or optionally by both simultaneously. The solver must recognize that the double form is optional, not obligatory.
   - Interaction with CORE: The negation pattern is independent of the nominal morphology but adds a clause-level pattern that the solver must parse. The preverbal negative's position relative to S and O provides additional word-order evidence.
   - Difficulty contribution: MEDIUM-HIGH — the optionality creates ambiguity that the solver must resolve.
   - Priority: OPTIONAL

INTERACTIONS

1. Gender × Modifier order: Post-nominal modifiers (adjectives, numerals) likely show class-specific allomorphy, meaning the solver must identify both the modifier's position AND its class-specific form. This creates a two-dimensional classification task.

2. Gender × Possessive marking: The possessive element associated with the head noun may vary by noun class, requiring the solver to track class-specific possessive forms alongside the structural pattern (head + possessive + possessor).

3. Gender × Plural: The plural suffix may have class-specific allomorphs, so the solver must identify both the plural marker and its interaction with the noun class system.

4. Modifier order × Possessive structure: The genitive (possessor) precedes the head noun (86A-1: GenN), while the possessive element is associated with the head. The solver must distinguish between the genitive order (possessor before head) and the internal possessive construction (head + possessive element + possessor), or recognize that these are the same structure described differently.

5. Noun-internal morphology ordering: The definite affix, plural suffix, and possessive element all attach to or follow the noun. The solver must determine their linear order, creating a morphological ordering puzzle.

6. SOV × Post-nominal modifiers: The typological harmony between OV clause order and NAdj/NNum NP-internal order provides a cross-confirmation that helps the solver verify their analysis.

CONSTRAINTS

- The puzzle must provide sufficient examples to demonstrate the full gender system (all 5+ classes must be attested in the data).
- The mixed modifier order must be clearly demonstrated: at least one pre-nominal modifier type (genitive or demonstrative) and at least one post-nominal modifier type (adjective or numeral) must appear in the data.
- The possessive construction must be structurally distinguishable from other NP-internal patterns (e.g., from relative clauses or apposition).
- Plural and definite marking must be independently identifiable (i.e., there must be examples showing plural without definite, definite without plural, and both together).
- The gender system must be inferable purely from the data — the solver should not need to know Supyire's semantic class assignments in advance. Formal cues (agreement patterns, allomorphy) must be sufficient.
- If optional double negation is included, the data must include both single-negation and double-negation examples to demonstrate optionality.
- The puzzle must not require knowledge of Supyire's tone system to solve the core morphological/syntactic patterns (tone can be present in the data but should not be essential to the solution).
- All target-language facts used must be consistent with the WALS evidence provided; no contradictions with established Supyire grammar.
