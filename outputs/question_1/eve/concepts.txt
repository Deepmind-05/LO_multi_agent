

TARGET-LANGUAGE: Evenki (eve)

CORE

1. Possessive marking: Double marking
   - Source concept: 81A-1 "Dependent marking" (adapted)
   - Target-language realization: Evenki marks possession with BOTH a genitive case suffix on the possessor AND a possessive suffix on the possessed noun (WALS 24A-3, 57A-2, 86A-1).
   - Intended solver inference: The solver should identify that both the possessor and the possessed noun receive morphological marking, determine the specific suffix forms, and recognize that this is a "double marking" strategy rather than pure dependent or head marking.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

2. Modifier-head order: Consistent prenominal modification
   - Source concept: 82A-1 "Noun before modifier" (adapted/reversed)
   - Target-language realization: Evenki consistently places modifiers before the head noun across all modifier types: AdjN (87A-1), GenN (86A-1), DemN (88A-1), NumN (89A-1), RelN (90A-2).
   - Intended solver inference: The solver should identify the consistent modifier-before-head pattern from multiple modifier types and recognize it as a systematic word order property.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

TARGET-SPECIFIC

1. Obligatory double negation (SONeg[V-Neg])
   - Phenomenon: Evenki requires both a preverbal negative word AND a negative verbal suffix for clause negation; both are obligatory (WALS 143A-14, 143B-2, 144N-4, 144L-16).
   - Intended solver inference: The solver should identify that negation involves two separate morphological elements (a free word and a bound suffix), both of which must appear, and determine their relative positions.
   - Interaction with CORE: The preverbal negative word's position between S/O and V provides additional evidence for SOV word order; the negative suffix is another instance of the suffixing tendency.
   - Difficulty contribution: HIGH — creates a distinctive "aha" moment; the obligatoriness of both elements is non-obvious and requires careful data analysis.
   - Priority: HIGH

2. Rich case system (10+ cases, no syncretism, suffixal)
   - Phenomenon: Evenki has 10 or more case categories (49A-8), each with a distinct suffix (51A-1), and no syncretism between any case forms (28A-4).
   - Intended solver inference: The solver should identify multiple distinct case suffixes, recognize that no two cases share the same form, and map each suffix to its semantic function.
   - Interaction with CORE: The genitive case is the possessor marking in the possessive system (CORE 1); the case suffixes demonstrate the suffixing tendency and interact with the modifier-head order (genitive NPs precede the head noun).
   - Difficulty contribution: MEDIUM — provides rich morphological data; the no-syncretism property means the solver can unambiguously identify each case from form alone.
   - Priority: HIGH

3. Strongly suffixing inflectional morphology
   - Phenomenon: Evenki is strongly suffixing (26A-2); case, possessive, plural, and verbal markers are all suffixes.
   - Intended solver inference: The solver should recognize that morphological marking is predominantly (or exclusively) suffixal, and identify the consistent suffixal order on nouns.
   - Interaction with CORE: Unifies the possessive suffix, case suffixes, and (if included) plural suffix into a single morphological pattern; the suffixal chain order (root + plural + possessive + case) is inferable from data.
   - Difficulty contribution: MEDIUM — provides a unifying structural principle that makes the morphological system more tractable and creates a clear pattern for the solver to discover.
   - Priority: HIGH

4. Obligatory plural marking on all nouns
   - Phenomenon: Plural is marked by a suffix (33A-2) and is obligatory on all nouns (34A-6).
   - Intended solver inference: The solver should notice that plural is never zero-marked and identify the plural suffix; the obligatoriness means the solver can use its presence/absence as a reliable signal.
   - Interaction with CORE: The plural suffix occupies a fixed position in the suffixal chain (before possessive and case suffixes), adding a layer to the morphological analysis.
   - Difficulty contribution: MEDIUM — adds a morphological layer without requiring semantic inference about plurality (since it's always present).
   - Priority: OPTIONAL

5. Inclusive/exclusive distinction in 1st person plural
   - Phenomenon: Evenki distinguishes inclusive and exclusive 1st person plural in both independent pronouns (39A-5) and verbal inflection (40A-5).
   - Intended solver inference: The solver should identify two distinct 1st person plural forms and infer the inclusive/exclusive semantic distinction from context.
   - Interaction with CORE: The inclusive/exclusive forms appear as possessors in possessive constructions (CORE 1), providing a semantic dimension to the possessive data.
   - Difficulty contribution: MEDIUM — adds a semantic inference layer; the distinction is inferable from context (e.g., "we including you" vs. "we excluding you").
   - Priority: OPTIONAL

INTERACTIONS

1. Possessive double marking × Case system × Suffixing morphology:
   - The genitive case suffix (one of 10+ distinct case forms) on the possessor and the possessive suffix on the possessed noun are both suffixes, demonstrating the suffixing tendency. The solver can identify the genitive case form from possessive data and then recognize it in non-possessive contexts (e.g., "from the house" vs. "the house's roof").
   - Additional inference: The solver can build a case paradigm from possessive data and verify it against other case-marked NPs.

2. Modifier-head order × Case system:
   - The genitive-noun order (GenN) is one instance of the consistent modifier-before-head pattern. The case suffix on the genitive NP does not alter its prenominal position.
   - Additional inference: The solver can confirm the word order pattern from multiple independent modifier types (adjective, genitive, demonstrative, numeral, relative clause), making the pattern robust.

3. Double negation × SOV word order:
   - The negation pattern SONeg[V-Neg] places the preverbal negative word between the arguments and the verb, providing independent evidence for SOV order.
   - Additional inference: The solver can use the negation pattern as a cross-check for word order identification, especially in sentences where argument roles are ambiguous.

4. Suffixing morphology × Obligatory plural × Possessive × Case:
   - The noun suffixal chain (root + plural + possessive + case) creates a fixed morphological order that the solver can discover.
   - Additional inference: The consistent ordering of suffixes provides a structural constraint that helps the solver segment complex noun forms and identify individual morphemes.

5. Inclusive/exclusive × Possessive system:
   - The two 1st person plural forms appear as possessors, and their inclusive/exclusive semantics can be inferred from the broader discourse context.
   - Additional inference: The solver can use the inclusive/exclusive distinction to disambiguate possessive constructions where the possessor is a 1st person plural pronoun.

CONSTRAINTS

1. The puzzle must present sufficient possessive data (at least 3-4 examples with different possessors and possessed nouns) for the solver to identify both the genitive case suffix and the possessive suffix independently.

2. The puzzle must include at least 3 different modifier types (e.g., adjective, genitive, demonstrative or numeral) to establish the consistent modifier-before-head pattern as a systematic property rather than an isolated instance.

3. If double negation is included, the puzzle must show at least 2 negative sentences to establish that BOTH the preverbal word and the verbal suffix are obligatory (not optional or variable).

4. The case system should be represented with enough examples to show at least 4-5 distinct case forms, demonstrating the no-syncretism property.

5. All suffixal forms must be consistent across examples; the suffixal order on nouns (if plural, possessive, and case all appear) must be the same in every instance.

6. The puzzle must not rely on the solver knowing that Evenki is a Tungusic/Altaic language or having any prior knowledge of the language. All properties must be inferable from the presented data.

7. The source concept of "dependent marking" (81A-1) does NOT directly apply to Evenki; the puzzle must present the double marking system accurately and not mislead the solver into thinking only one element is marked.

8. The source concept of "noun before modifier" (82A-1) is the OPPOSITE of Evenki's actual pattern; the puzzle must present the modifier-before-head order clearly and not create confusion with the source language's pattern.
