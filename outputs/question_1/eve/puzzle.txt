SOLUTION

**Morphological system:**

Nouns have a fixed suffixal chain: root + plural + possessive + case.

- Plural suffix: -r (obligatory on all plural nouns)
- Possessive suffixes (on the possessed noun, agreeing with possessor): -m (1sg), -t (2sg), -s (3sg), -p (1pl incl), -b (1pl excl), -d (2pl), -l (3pl)
- Case suffixes: -a (nominative), -n (accusative), -i (genitive), -o (locative), -u (allative), -y (ablative)

Pronouns: na (I), ta (you sg), sa (he/she), pa (we incl), ba (we excl), da (you pl), la (they). When used as possessors, pronouns take the genitive suffix -i.

**Key rules:**

1. **Double possessive marking:** In a possessive construction, the possessor takes the genitive case suffix (-i) AND the possessed noun takes a possessive suffix agreeing with the possessor's person/number. Evidence: sentences 5–7, 15–19. For example, in (5) na-i kura-m-a, the possessor "I" is marked with genitive -i and the possessed noun "dog" is marked with possessive -m (1sg).

2. **Modifier-before-head word order:** Adjectives, demonstratives, and numerals all precede the noun they modify. Evidence: (8) biga kura-a (Adj-Nom), (9) taka tura-a (Dem-Nom), (10) turu nana-r-a (Num-Nom). In possessive constructions with an attributive adjective, the order is: possessor-Gen + adjective + possessed-noun-poss-Case. Evidence: (21) sa-i biga kura-s-a (Poss-Adj-Nom).

3. **SOV word order:** The verb appears at the end of the clause. Evidence: all transitive sentences (3, 4, 5, 13, 17, 18) and intransitive sentences (1, 2, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, 14, 20, 21).

4. **Double negation:** Negation requires both a preverbal negative word (ega) and a negative suffix on the verb (-na). Both are obligatory. Evidence: (13) kura-a nana-n ega kama-na and (14) nana-a ega nura-na. In both, ega appears between the object/subject and the verb, and -na is attached to the verb stem.

5. **Inclusive/exclusive 1st person plural:** The language distinguishes inclusive (pa, -p) and exclusive (ba, -b) 1st person plural. Evidence: (15) pa-i tura-p-a vs. (16) ba-i tura-b-a.

**Derivations:**

Q1: kura-r-a = dog-pl-Nom ("the dogs"), nana-n = child-Acc ("the child"), kama = "see." SOV order → "The dogs see the child."

Q2: ta-i = you-Gen ("your"), kura-t-a = dog-poss2sg-Nom ("dog"), nana-n = child-Acc, kama = "see." → "Your dog sees the child."

Q3: sa-i = he-Gen ("his"), biga = "big" (prenominal adjective), kura-s-a = dog-poss3sg-Nom, nana-n = child-Acc, kama = "see." Order: possessor + adjective + possessed noun (cf. 21). → "His big dog sees the child."

Q4: kura-a = dog-Nom, tura-r-n = house-pl-Acc ("the houses"), ega = NEG, kama-na = see-NEG. → "The dog does not see the houses."

Q5: "The child" = nana-a (Nom), "your dogs" = ta-i (you-Gen) + kura-r-t-n (dog-pl-poss2sg-Acc), "sees" = kama. → nana-a ta-i kura-r-t-n kama.

Q6: "The big dog's child" = [biga kura-i] (big dog-Gen, adjective before its head within the genitive NP) + nana-s-a (child-poss3sg-Nom), "goes to the house" = tura-u (house-All) + vura (go). → biga kura-i nana-s-a tura-u vura.
