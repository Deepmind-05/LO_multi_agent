

TARGET-LANGUAGE: Amele (Trans-New Guinea, Madang, Mabuso)

CONSTRUCTION RATIONALE

1. **Vocabulary and basic clause structure.** Select a controlled vocabulary of 8–12 nouns spanning at least three semantic categories that will map onto distinct possessive classes (e.g., body parts, kinship terms, natural objects, tools). Include 4–6 adjectives that can modify these nouns, and 3–4 dynamic verbs. Establish basic clause structure (SVO) through simple transitive and intransitive sentences. The solver should be able to identify subject, object, and verb positions from the data without prior knowledge. Person/number marking on the verb (if present) should be introduced here but kept simple so it does not interfere with the possessive discoveries that follow.

2. **First possessive NPs: establishing dependent marking.** Introduce 2–3 possessive noun phrases in which the possessor is a pronoun (e.g., 1sg, 2sg, 3sg) and the possessed noun belongs to a single class. The possessor must carry a visible suffix; the possessed noun must be morphologically bare. The solver's first inference: the possessive relation is marked on the possessor, not the possessed. This is the dependent-marking discovery. Ensure that in every possessive NP in the entire dataset, the possessor is the marked element and the possessed noun is unmarked, so no alternative analysis is possible.

3. **Possessive classification: the suffix is not invariant.** Introduce possessive NPs where the same possessor (e.g., 1sg) is paired with possessed nouns from different classes. The 1sg forms should be presented as opaque units (e.g., *nati*, *nale*, *nalo*) — do NOT decompose them into a stem plus ending, because the linking consonants (t, l, l) do not form a clean stem+suffix analysis. The solver should notice that the 1sg possessor does not have a single invariant form but rather different surface forms depending on the possessed noun. The solver's inference: the possessive suffix is class-conditioned. The problem setter should provide enough tokens (at least 2 nouns per class, 3–4 classes total) for the solver to group nouns by their associated possessor form with reasonable confidence.

4. **3pl allomorphy: a second layer of variation.** Introduce 3pl possessive NPs across at least two classes. The 3pl forms should be presented as opaque units (e.g., *kiu* for one class, *kao* for another). Do NOT present a single 3pl stem with class-specific endings, because the surface forms do not decompose cleanly into a uniform stem plus suffix. The solver should notice that the 3pl also exhibits class-conditioned variation, and that the allomorphy (ki- vs. ka-) is a surface fact to be catalogued rather than a rule to be explained. This reinforces the classification system and prevents the solver from over-generalizing a single "possessive suffix."

5. **Noun-before-modifier order.** Introduce 3–4 noun phrases with adjectival modifiers (and optionally one relative clause) in which the head noun precedes the modifier. The modifier is unmarked. The solver's inference: modifiers follow the head noun. To make this robust, use at least two different modifier types (e.g., an adjective and a relative clause) so the pattern is not attributable to a single lexical item. If adjectives and relative clauses are formally identical in the data (as they are in Amele), this is fine — the solver will simply see a broader class of post-nominal modifiers.

6. **The central asymmetry: possessor vs. other modifiers.** Construct at least one sentence containing a possessive NP that also has an adjectival modifier on the possessed noun. This single construction should make the asymmetry visible: the possessor is (a) pre-nominal and (b) marked, while the adjective is (c) post-nominal and (d) unmarked. The solver's key "aha!" inference: the possessor is the unique pre-nominal, marked modifier in the NP; all other modifiers are post-nominal and unmarked. This integrates the dependent-marking discovery (step 2), the classification discovery (steps 3–4), and the modifier-order discovery (step 5) into a single typological observation. Ensure the data contains at least one such combined NP so the solver can see the contrast in a single context.

7. **Conjunctional possessive: predicative possession.** Introduce 2–3 sentences expressing "I have X" / "They have Y" using a conjunction that is identical to the coordinate conjunction 'and' (which also functions as 'with'). The possessive reading must be contextually unambiguous — for example, contrast a sentence like "I and the house" (possession: "I have the house") with a sentence like "I and the child went" (coordination: "I and the child went"). The solver's inference: (a) possession can be expressed without the possessive suffix, (b) the strategy is a conjunction, and (c) this conjunction is polyfunctional (and/with/possessive predicate). The solver should recognize that attributive possession (suffix on possessor, pre-nominal) and predicative possession (conjunction, no suffix) are two distinct syntactic strategies for the same semantic relation.

8. **Optional: adjectives = relative clauses.** If space allows, include one or two examples where a relative clause modifies a noun in exactly the same formal way as an adjective (post-nominal, unmarked). The solver's failed attempt to distinguish the two is itself informative and reinforces the post-nominal modifier pattern. This is optional and should not be required for solving the core questions.

9. **Complexity ramp: combined constructions.** Construct 2–3 sentences that combine multiple systems simultaneously: (a) a possessive NP with a class-conditioned suffix + an adjectival modifier on the possessed noun + a verb with person marking; (b) a conjunctional possessive in a clause that also contains a post-nominal modifier; (c) a sentence where the possessed noun is itself modified and the whole NP is the subject or object of a verb. These sentences should not introduce any new rules but should require the solver to integrate previously discovered patterns.

10. **Question design: progressive difficulty.** Design 6–8 translation questions (mix of target-to-English and English-to-target) with the following progression:
    - Q1–Q2 (easy, target-to-English): Test basic word order + one possessive NP with a class-conditioned suffix. All forms appear verbatim in the data.
    - Q3 (medium, target-to-English): Test the asymmetry — a sentence containing both a possessive NP (pre-nominal, marked) and an adjectival modifier (post-nominal, unmarked) in the same clause. The specific combination of noun + class + modifier should not appear verbatim in the data, requiring the solver to apply the classification rule to a new noun.
    - Q4 (medium, target-to-English): Test the conjunctional possessive in a context where the solver must distinguish it from coordination.
    - Q5 (medium-hard, English-to-target): Require the solver to produce a possessive NP with a class-conditioned suffix for a possessor/possessed combination not seen verbatim in the data. The solver must correctly select the appropriate opaque form (e.g., *nati* vs. *nale* vs. *nalo* for 1sg, or *kiu* vs. *kao* for 3pl) based on the possessed noun's class.
    - Q6 (hard, English-to-target): Require the solver to produce a sentence combining a possessive NP (with correct class-conditioned suffix) + a post-nominal modifier + a verb. The solver must place the possessor before the possessed noun, apply the correct suffix, and place the modifier after the possessed noun.
    - Q7 (hard, English-to-target): Require the solver to produce a conjunctional possessive sentence that also contains a post-nominal modifier, testing the solver's ability to distinguish the two possession strategies and apply modifier order correctly.
    - Q8 (hardest, English-to-target): A sentence combining at least three systems: possessive classification + modifier order + conjunctional possessive (or negation/aspect if introduced). The solver must integrate all discovered rules simultaneously.

11. **Verification checklist.** Before finalizing:
    - Confirm that no possessive NP in the data allows the possessive marker to be reanalyzed as belonging to the possessed noun. In every case, the possessor must be the marked element.
    - Confirm that the possessive classification is discoverable: for each class, at least two different nouns should appear with the same possessor form, and at least two different classes should appear with the same possessor (showing the form changes).
    - Confirm that the 1sg and 3pl forms are presented as opaque units (or with explicitly noted allomorphy) and that no question requires the solver to decompose them into sub-morphemes.
    - Confirm that the asymmetry (possessor = pre-nominal + marked; other modifiers = post-nominal + unmarked) is visible in at least one data sentence and is tested in at least two questions.
    - Confirm that the conjunctional possessive is distinguishable from coordination in the data (at least one contrastive pair).
    - Confirm that every rule required by the questions is uniquely determined by the data, and that no question requires a form or rule absent from the data.
    - Confirm that the two core features (dependent marking, noun-before-modifier) are each tested in at least three questions and are always tested in combination with at least one other rule.
