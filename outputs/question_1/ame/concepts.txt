

TARGET-LANGUAGE: Amele (Trans-New Guinea, Madang, Mabuso)

CORE

1. Dependent marking in possessive NPs (source 81A-1 → WALS 24A-2)
   - Target-language realization: The possessor bears a possessive suffix; the possessed noun is unmarked. Structure: [Possessor-POSS] Possessed.
   - Intended solver inference: By comparing possessive and non-possessive constructions, the solver identifies which element carries the possessive marker and determines it is the possessor (not the possessed) that is marked.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

2. Noun before modifier (source 82A-1 → WALS 87A-2, 88A-2, 89A-2, 90A-1)
   - Target-language realization: Adjectives, demonstratives, numerals, and relative clauses all follow the head noun.
   - Intended solver inference: By observing multiple modifier types in context, the solver generalizes that modifiers follow the noun (head-initial NP).
   - Priority: REQUIRED

TARGET-SPECIFIC

1. Possessive classification (WALS 59A-4: more than five classes)
   - Phenomenon: The possessive suffix is not invariant; it varies depending on the possessed noun, reflecting a classification system with more than five classes.
   - Intended solver inference: The solver notices that the possessive suffix changes across different possessed nouns and attempts to group nouns by which suffix they take, revealing a classification system.
   - Interaction with CORE: Complicates the dependent marking concept — the solver must not only identify WHERE marking falls but also that it VARIES based on the possessed noun, adding a classification dimension to the marking pattern.
   - Difficulty contribution: HIGH — adds a layer of pattern recognition and classification discovery.
   - Priority: HIGH

2. Conjunctional possessive (WALS 117A-4) + 'and' = 'with' (WALS 63A-2)
   - Phenomenon: Predicative possession ("I have X") is expressed using a conjunction that is identical to the coordinate conjunction 'and' (which is also identical to 'with').
   - Intended solver inference: The solver discovers that "having" is not expressed by a verb but by a conjunction, and that this same word also functions as 'and'/'with'. This reveals a non-obvious strategy for expressing possession.
   - Interaction with CORE: Creates a contrast between attributive possession (suffix on possessor, pre-nominal) and predicative possession (conjunction, no suffix). The solver must distinguish these two strategies and understand they express different syntactic roles of the same semantic relation.
   - Difficulty contribution: HIGH — the use of a conjunction for possession is typologically distinctive and non-obvious; the solver must recognize that a seemingly simple conjunction is doing possessive work.
   - Priority: HIGH

3. Genitive-Noun order (WALS 86A-1) as asymmetry with other modifiers
   - Phenomenon: The possessor (genitive) precedes the possessed noun, while all other modifiers (adjectives, demonstratives, numerals, relative clauses) follow the noun.
   - Intended solver inference: The solver notices that the possessor is the UNIQUE pre-nominal element in the NP, and that it is also the unique marked modifier. This reveals a systematic asymmetry: possessor = pre-nominal + marked; all other modifiers = post-nominal + unmarked.
   - Interaction with CORE: Combines both CORE concepts into a single elegant insight — the possessor is the exception to the post-nominal modifier pattern, and it is the only modifier that is marked. This is the central typological observation of the puzzle.
   - Difficulty contribution: MEDIUM-HIGH — the solver must integrate information from multiple constructions to see the full pattern.
   - Priority: HIGH

4. Adjectives and relative clauses collapsed (WALS 60A-4)
   - Phenomenon: Adjectives and relative clauses are formally identical in Amele.
   - Intended solver inference: The solver may attempt to distinguish adjectives from relative clauses and find they are formally the same, which confirms that the "noun before modifier" pattern applies to a broader class than just adjectives.
   - Interaction with CORE: Extends the noun-modifier order concept to include relative clauses, making the post-nominal modifier pattern more robust and harder to attribute to a single modifier type.
   - Difficulty contribution: MEDIUM — the solver's failed attempt to distinguish adjectives from relative clauses is itself informative.
   - Priority: OPTIONAL

INTERACTIONS

1. Dependent marking + Genitive-Noun order + Noun before modifier:
   - The possessor is the unique pre-nominal, marked modifier; all other modifiers are post-nominal and unmarked. This three-way interaction creates the central puzzle insight: a clean asymmetry that the solver must discover by comparing possessive and non-possessive NPs.

2. Possessive classification + Dependent marking:
   - The possessive suffix is not a single invariant morpheme but varies by possessed noun class. The solver must discover both the locus of marking AND the classification system simultaneously, creating a two-dimensional pattern to extract.

3. Conjunctional possessive + Dependent marking:
   - Two distinct strategies for expressing possession: attributive (suffix on possessor, pre-nominal) vs. predicative (conjunction, no suffix). The solver must recognize these as two expressions of the same semantic relation in different syntactic contexts, and understand why the suffix is absent in the predicative construction.

4. Conjunctional possessive + 'and' = 'with':
   - The same word serves as coordinate conjunction, comitative, and possessive predicate. The solver must recognize the polyfunctionality and understand that the possessive reading arises from context, not from a dedicated possessive morpheme.

CONSTRAINTS

- The puzzle data must provide enough possessive examples to allow the solver to (a) identify the possessor vs. possessed, (b) notice the suffix, and (c) detect the classification system. At minimum, examples should cover 3-4 different possessive classes to make the classification discoverable without being overwhelming.
- The conjunctional possessive must be presented in a context where the possessive reading is clear (e.g., contrasted with a non-possessive coordination), so the solver can distinguish "I and X" (possession) from "I and X" (coordination).
- The asymmetry between possessor (pre-nominal, marked) and other modifiers (post-nominal, unmarked) must be clearly visible in the data — the puzzle should include at least one possessive NP and at least two non-possessive NPs with different modifier types.
- The possessive classification should not be so fine-grained that it becomes unmanageable from limited data; the puzzle should present enough tokens for the solver to group nouns into classes with reasonable confidence.
- No prior knowledge of Amele or Trans-New Guinea languages should be required; all patterns must be inferable from the provided data alone.
