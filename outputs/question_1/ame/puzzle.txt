SOLUTION

**Inferred rules:**

**Rule 1 — Dependent marking in possessive NPs.** In a possessive noun phrase, the possessor appears first and is morphologically bare (no suffix). The possessed noun follows and carries a possessive suffix. Evidence: in (1)–(3), (12)–(19), the proper name (Kela, Bona, Mura) is always bare, while the following noun bears a suffix. The English translations confirm the proper name is the possessor.

**Rule 2 — Noun-before-modifier order.** Adjectives and demonstratives follow the noun they modify. Evidence: (4)–(8) show adjectives (bala, kula, mawa) and demonstratives (ni, ne) following the noun. In (9)–(11) and (25)–(27), the possessed noun (with its suffix) is followed by modifiers, confirming it is the structural head of the NP.

**Rule 3 — Possessive classification.** The possessive suffix is determined by the semantic class of the possessed noun:
- **Class A (body parts):** mapi (eye), tawa (hand), lapa (leg), napa (head) → suffix **-na** (evidence: 1–3, 9–11, 25)
- **Class B (kinship):** mama (mother), papa (father), nana (child), tana (sister) → suffix **-ka** (evidence: 12–15, 26)
- **Class C (natural objects / artifacts):** wala (river), kapa (tree), sapa (stone), mola (house) → suffix **-ta** (evidence: 16–19, 27)

Each class is represented by four distinct nouns, confirming the pattern is systematic rather than idiosyncratic.

**Rule 4 — Conjunctional (predicative) possession.** The construction [X] *ma* [Y] expresses "X has Y" at the clause level. Crucially, neither X nor Y carries a possessive suffix. Evidence: (20)–(22). Contrast with the attributive construction: in (1) *Kela mapi-na*, mapi bears -na; in (20) *Kela ma mapi*, mapi is bare. Similarly, mama appears with -ka in (12) but bare in (21); wala appears with -ta in (16) but bare in (22).

**Rule 5 — Polysemy of *ma* (WITH-language).** The same morpheme *ma* expresses comitative/associative meaning ("and," "together with") when both participants are independent animate agents (proper names). Evidence: (23)–(24), where both elements are proper names and the translation is "X and Y (together)." Disambiguation principle: if the second element is a common noun → possessive reading; if both elements are proper names → comitative reading.

**How the rules produce each answer:**

- **Q1:** *napa* is a body part (Class A) → suffix -na. *Mura* is the bare possessor. → "Mura's head."
- **Q2:** *kapa* (tree) is the head noun; *kula* (small) and *ne* (that) are postnominal modifiers. → "that small tree."
- **Q3:** *tana* (sister) is kinship (Class B) → suffix -ka. *Bona* is the bare possessor. *kula* (small) is a postnominal modifier. → "Bona's small sister."
- **Q4:** *ma* with a common noun as second element → predicative possession. No suffix on *sapa*. → "Kela has a stone."
- **Q5:** *ma* with two proper names → comitative. → "Bona and Mura (together)."
- **Q6:** *sapa* (stone) is Class C → suffix -ta. Possessor *Kela* is bare. → *Kela sapa-ta*.
- **Q7:** *tawa* (hand) is Class A → suffix -na. *kula* (small) follows as modifier. → *Mura tawa-na kula*.
- **Q8:** Predicative possession → use *ma*, no suffix on *kapa*. → *Bona ma kapa*.
- **Q9:** *mola* (house) is Class C → suffix -ta. *bala* (big) and *ne* (that) are postnominal modifiers. → *Mura mola-ta bala ne*.
- **Q10:** Predicative possession → use *ma*, no suffix on *wala*. *bala* (big) is a postnominal modifier of *wala*. → *Mura ma wala bala*. (The critical insight: even though *wala* belongs to Class C, it does NOT receive -ta in the predicative construction.)

DIFFICULTY

**Level 5.** The puzzle requires the solver to simultaneously discover and apply five interacting rules: (1) dependent marking, (2) postnominal modification, (3) a three-way possessive classification, (4) a distinct predicative possession strategy, and (5) polysemy-based disambiguation. The hardest questions (Q8, Q10) specifically test whether the solver recognizes that the predicative construction suppresses the possessive suffix — a non-obvious insight that requires contrasting the attributive and predicative strategies. Q9 requires combining classification, dependent marking, and modifier order in a single output. The progressive data structure ensures each rule is discoverable before it is needed in a question, but the questions require productive application to novel combinations not directly present in the data.
