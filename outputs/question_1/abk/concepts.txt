

TARGET-LANGUAGE

Abkhaz (Northwest Caucasian)

CORE

1. Concept: Locus of Marking in Possessive Noun Phrases (source 81A, adapted)
   - Source value: Dependent marking (possessor marked)
   - Target-language realization: Head marking — the possessed noun (head) carries a possessive prefix encoding the possessor (WALS 24A-1, 57A-1). E.g., "house-1SG" = "my house."
   - Intended solver inference: The possessor is not a separate word or a marker on the possessor; it is a prefix fused to the possessed noun. The solver must identify which morpheme is the possessive prefix and which is the noun stem.
   - Priority: REQUIRED
   - Note: This is a CONTRAST with the source language (Tawala has dependent marking). The puzzle exploits this contrast: the solver must discover that the marking strategy is the opposite of what the source pattern would predict.

2. Concept: Noun before modifier (source 82A)
   - Source value: Noun before modifier
   - Target-language realization: Noun-Adjective order (WALS 87A-2). The modifying adjective follows the head noun.
   - Intended solver inference: The head noun precedes its modifier. This is consistent with the source and provides a stable "head-first" anchor in the nominal domain.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

TARGET-SPECIFIC

1. Phenomenon: Possessive prefixes (WALS 57A-1)
   - Intended solver inference: The possessive markers are specifically prefixes (not suffixes, clitics, or separate words). The solver must identify the prefix as distinct from the noun stem.
   - Interaction with CORE: Directly instantiates the head-marking concept; the prefix is the morphological realization of head marking.
   - Difficulty contribution: Medium. The solver must segment the word into prefix + stem, which requires identifying the noun stem across different possessive forms.
   - Priority: HIGH

2. Phenomenon: Plural suffix (WALS 33A-2, 34A-6)
   - Intended solver inference: Plurality is marked by a suffix on the noun. When combined with a possessive prefix, the noun appears as [poss-prefix + stem + plural-suffix].
   - Interaction with CORE: Creates a prefix-suffix sandwich that demonstrates the language uses both prefixing and suffixing on the same noun. The solver must distinguish the possessive prefix from the plural suffix.
   - Difficulty contribution: High. The solver must identify two different affixes on the same word and determine which is possessive and which is plural.
   - Priority: HIGH

3. Phenomenon: Postpositions (WALS 85A-1)
   - Intended solver inference: Locative/oblique phrases use postpositions (noun + postposition), consistent with the head-first tendency.
   - Interaction with CORE: Reinforces the head-first pattern in the nominal domain (Noun-Adjective, Noun-Postposition). Creates a contrast: the possessor is NOT a separate post-nominal element but is fused as a prefix, while other modifiers (adjectives, postpositions) are separate post-nominal elements.
   - Difficulty contribution: Medium. The solver must distinguish postpositions from adjectives (both follow the noun) and recognize that the possessor uses a different strategy (prefix) than other modifiers.
   - Priority: HIGH

4. Phenomenon: SOV word order (WALS 81A-1)
   - Intended solver inference: If verbal data is included, the verb follows its arguments.
   - Interaction with CORE: Consistent with the overall head-final / dependent-final tendency in the clause, while the nominal domain shows head-first order. This cross-domain contrast is typologically interesting.
   - Difficulty contribution: Low-Medium. Only useful if verbal data is included; may overcomplicate the puzzle.
   - Priority: OPTIONAL

INTERACTIONS

1. Possessive prefix × Plural suffix:
   - Interaction: Both attach to the same noun, creating a [prefix-stem-suffix] structure.
   - Additional inference enabled: The solver can confirm that the prefix is possessive (varies by person/number of possessor) and the suffix is plural (varies by number of possessed), by observing which morpheme changes in which contexts.

2. Noun-Adjective × Postpositions × Head-marking possessives:
   - Interaction: Adjectives and postpositions are separate post-nominal elements, while the possessor is a prefix on the noun.
   - Additional inference enabled: The solver can identify a systematic distinction: the possessor is "fused" to the head (head-marking), while other modifiers are "free" post-nominal elements. This is a meaningful typological contrast that the solver can discover from the data.

3. Head-marking possessives × Noun-Adjective order:
   - Interaction: Both concepts involve the relationship between a noun and its modifier/possessor. The noun is the head in both cases, but the marking strategy differs (prefix for possessor, separate word for adjective).
   - Additional inference enabled: The solver can recognize that "head" is a consistent concept: the noun is the head in both possessive and adjective constructions, but the language uses different strategies for different types of modification.

CONSTRAINTS

- The puzzle data must include enough possessive examples (at least 3-4 different possessors) for the solver to identify the possessive prefix pattern and distinguish it from the noun stem.
- The plural suffix must appear in at least one example alongside a possessive prefix to demonstrate the prefix-suffix interaction.
- If postpositions are included, they must be clearly distinguishable from adjectives (e.g., by semantic content or by appearing in a different syntactic context).
- The puzzle must NOT include data that would make the solver think the possessive marker is a separate word (e.g., no spaces or clear word boundaries that could be misinterpreted).
- The head-marking pattern must be unambiguous: the same noun stem should appear with different possessive prefixes, making it clear that the prefix is the variable element.
- If SOV word order is included, it should be in a separate section or clearly marked as verbal data to avoid confusion with the nominal-domain patterns.
- The puzzle should not require the solver to know Abkhaz phonology or orthography; all patterns should be inferable from the distributional data alone.
