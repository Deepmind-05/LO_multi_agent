

TARGET-LANGUAGE

Abkhaz (abk), Northwest Caucasian

CONSTRUCTION RATIONALE

1. Establish the fixed phonological inventory. The language has exactly four vowels: a, e, i, o. The consonant inventory includes three ejective stops (pʼ, tʼ, kʼ) contrasted with their plain counterparts (p, t, k), plus the following non-ejective consonants: s, m, n, r, l, w, y, b, d, g, f. The ejective is marked orthographically with an apostrophe (ʼ). The solver must identify the 4-vowel inventory from the full lexicon and the ejective contrast from minimal pairs. No other phonological processes (stress, tone, vowel harmony) are present.

2. Fix the closed lexicon. Use exactly the following items. Nouns: fana (house), amre (water), tʼari (child), tari (mother), kʼaba (dog), kaba (tree), sore (path), pʼano (stone), pano (father). Adjectives: lare (big), wone (good), kʼari (old), bile (new). Verb stems: ra (go), kʼa (see), ba (eat), ta (fall), wa (give). The minimal pairs are: tari/tʼari (t/tʼ), kaba/kʼaba (k/kʼ), pano/pʼano (p/pʼ). All four vowels appear across the lexicon: a (fana, kaba, lare), e (amre, sore, lare, bile), i (tʼari, tari, kʼari, bile), o (sore, pʼano, pano, wone).

3. Fix the possessive prefix paradigm. The possessed noun bears a prefix encoding the possessor's person. The possessor itself is morphologically unmarked (no free pronoun appears). The paradigm is: 1sg = ma-, 2sg = da-, 3sg = zero. There is no plural distinction in the possessive prefix; number of the possessor is not encoded. Examples: ma-fana (my house), da-fana (your house), fana (his/her/the house), kʼaba fana (the dog's house).

4. Fix the verbal person-marking paradigm. The verb encodes person through two slots: a prefix (P/S slot) and a suffix (A slot). The P/S prefix paradigm is identical to the possessive prefix paradigm: 1sg = ma-, 2sg = da-, 3sg = zero. The A suffix paradigm is: 1sg = -ri, 2sg = -li, 3sg = zero. In intransitive clauses, only the P/S prefix appears (marking S). In transitive clauses, both the P prefix and the A suffix appear. The linear order is always P-prefix + verb stem + A-suffix. This yields ergative alignment: S (intransitive subject) is marked by the same prefix as P (transitive patient), while A (transitive agent) is marked by a distinct suffix.

5. Fix the remaining morphological and syntactic rules. Plural is marked by the suffix -ba on the noun (obligatory for plural, absent for singular). Adjectives follow the head noun (NAdj order). The basic clause order is SOV (subject NP, object NP, verb). Negation is a preverbal particle ga placed immediately before the verb. Stative clauses (noun + adjective) have no verb. Possessive noun phrases have the structure: [Possessor NP] [possessive-prefix + possessed noun], where the possessor NP is unmarked.
