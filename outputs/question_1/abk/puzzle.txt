SOLUTION

**Inference chain:**

**(a) Phonology.** The data contain exactly four vowels: a, e, i, o. Three ejective/plain consonant contrasts are identifiable from minimal pairs: t/tʼ (tari 'mother' vs. tʼari 'child'), k/kʼ (kaba 'tree' vs. kʼaba 'dog'), p/pʼ (pano 'father' vs. pʼano 'stone'). The apostrophe marks the ejective member of each pair.

**(b) Word order and modifier order.** Sentences (1), (2), (9)–(15), (18), (22) show SOV order (NP precedes V). Sentences (1), (3), (4), (16) show that adjectives follow the noun (NAdj order): kʼaba lare, tʼari wone, kaba lare, tʼari-ba lare. Sentences (8), (17), (21) show that the possessor NP precedes the possessed noun: kʼaba fana, pano pʼano, tʼari-ba fana-ba. Thus the language has a mixed modifier order: adjectives are post-nominal, while genitives (possessors) are pre-nominal.

**(c) Possessive head-marking.** Sentences (5)–(8) show that the possessor is unmarked and the possessed noun bears a prefix encoding the possessor's person: ma- (1sg), da- (2sg), ∅ (3sg). This is head-marking: the grammatical head (the possessed noun) carries the agreement morpheme.

**(d) The 3PL possessive suffix and the -ba homomorph.** Sentences (16) and (18) establish that -ba is a plural suffix on nouns: tʼari-ba 'children', amre-ba 'waters'. Sentences (21) and (22) introduce a new use of -ba: in a possessive construction with a plural possessor, the possessed noun bears -ba as a 3PL possessive suffix. In (21) tʼari-ba fana-ba 'The children's house', the translation confirms that fana-ba means 'their house' (singular house, 3PL possessor), not 'houses' (plural). The -ba on tʼari marks the possessor's plurality; the -ba on fana marks the 3PL possessor on the head. Thus -ba is a **homomorph** with two distinct functions: (i) a plural suffix on nouns in non-possessive contexts, and (ii) a 3PL possessive suffix on the possessed noun in possessive constructions. The possessive paradigm is therefore: 1sg = ma- (prefix), 2sg = da- (prefix), 3sg = ∅, 3pl = -ba (suffix).

**(e) Intransitive verbal person marking.** Sentences (9)–(11) show that intransitive verbs bear a prefix marking the intransitive subject (S): ma- (1sg), da- (2sg), ∅ (3sg). This prefix paradigm is identical to the possessive prefix paradigm (for 1sg and 2sg).

**(f) Transitive verbal person marking and ergative alignment.** Sentences (12)–(14) and (19) show that transitive verbs bear two person markers: a prefix (P slot) and a suffix (A slot). The P prefix paradigm is identical to the intransitive S prefix (ma-/da-/∅). The A suffix paradigm is: -ri (1sg), -li (2sg), ∅ (3sg). Comparing (9) ma-ra 'I go' (S=1sg → ma-) with (13) ma-kʼa-li 'You see me' (P=1sg → ma-) shows that S and P are marked by the same prefix, while A is marked by a distinct suffix. This establishes **ergative alignment** (S=P≠A).

**(g) Linear order of person markers.** In all transitive examples, the P prefix precedes the verb stem and the A suffix follows it: P-prefix + stem + A-suffix.

**(h) Negation.** Sentence (15) ga-ma-ra 'I don't go' contrasts with (9) ma-ra 'I go', showing that ga- is a negation prefix attached to the verb (preceding the P/S prefix).

**(i) Independence of possessive marking and verbal P-marking.** Sentence (20) ma-fana kʼa-ri 'I see my house' is critical: the ma- on fana is a possessive prefix (marking the possessor of the house as 1sg), while the ∅ P-prefix on kʼa indicates the patient is 3rd person. The -ri A-suffix indicates the agent is 1sg. The patient "my house" is a 3rd-person entity (a house), regardless of who possesses it. Sentence (22) tʼari-ba fana-ba kʼa-ri 'I see the children's house' confirms this principle with the 3PL possessive: the -ba on fana marks the 3PL possessor, but the patient is still a 3rd-person entity (a house), so the P-prefix is ∅. The possessive marking on the noun (whether a prefix or a suffix) never affects the verbal P-marking; the patient's person is determined by the referent of the noun phrase as a whole.

**Answers:**

**Q1.** da-wa-ri: P=2sg (da-), stem=wa (give), A=1sg (-ri) → "I give you."

**Q2.** tʼari-ba lare: tʼari-ba = child-pl (children); lare = big (NAdj) → "The big children."

**Q3.** kʼaba lare fana ga-kʼa-ri: kʼaba lare = big dog (NAdj); fana = house (3sg possessor, ∅ prefix) → "the big dog's house." Verb: ga (neg) + ∅ (3sg P) + kʼa (see) + -ri (1sg A) → "I don't see [it]." Full: "I don't see the big dog's house."

**Q4.** "You see the big tree." A=2sg → -li; P=3rd person (the tree) → ∅. Verb: kʼa-li. Object: kaba lare (NAdj). SOV: **kaba lare kʼa-li**.

**Q5.** "I don't see your child." The object "your child" = da-tʼari (2sg possessive prefix + child). The patient is a 3rd-person entity (a child), so P=∅. A=1sg → -ri. Negation: ga-. Verb: ga-kʼa-ri. SOV: **da-tʼari ga-kʼa-ri**.

**Q6.** "The old mother's big house." Possessor: tari kʼari (mother + old, NAdj). Possessed: fana lare (house + big, NAdj). Possessor is 3sg → ∅ prefix on possessed noun. Full: **tari kʼari fana lare**.

**Q7.** "You see the new father's stone." A=2sg → -li; P=3rd person (the stone) → ∅. Verb: kʼa-li. Object: pano bile pʼano (father + new [NAdj] + stone [3sg possessor, ∅ prefix]). SOV: **pano bile pʼano kʼa-li**.

**Q8.** ma-tʼari-ba kʼa-li: The ma- on tʼari-ba is a possessive prefix (1sg possessor) → "my children." The patient "my children" is a 3rd-person entity, so the P-prefix on the verb is ∅. The A-suffix -li indicates the agent is 2sg (you). Full: "You see my children."

**Q9.** kʼaba-ba fana-ba ga-kʼa-ri: kʼaba-ba = dog-pl (dogs); fana-ba = house + 3PL possessive suffix (their house) → "the dogs' house." Verb: ga (neg) + ∅ (3sg P, the house is a 3rd-person entity) + kʼa (see) + -ri (1sg A) → "I don't see [it]." Full: "I don't see the dogs' house." The -ba on kʼaba marks the possessor's plurality; the -ba on fana marks the 3PL possessor on the head. Neither affects the verbal P-marking.

**Q10.** "The children's house is big." Possessor: tʼari-ba (child + PL = children). Possessed: fana-ba (house + 3PL possessive suffix = their house). Adjective: lare (big, NAdj, follows the possessed noun). Stative clause (no verb). Full: **tʼari-ba fana-ba lare**.
