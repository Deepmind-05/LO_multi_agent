SOLUTION

**Inferred rules:**

1. **Possessive prefix (head marking):** The possessor is encoded as a prefix fused to the possessed noun. The paradigm is:
   - a- = 1SG ("my")
   - u- = 2SG ("your (sg)")
   - ∅ (zero) = 3SG ("his/her")
   - i- = 1PL ("our")

   *Evidence:* Examples 1–4 show the same stem *rə* with four different prefixes, each corresponding to a different possessor. Examples 5–7 confirm the pattern with stem *wə*. Example 8 shows *gə* bare (= 3SG). Example 23 shows *ra* bare (= 3SG). The prefix is orthographically fused to the stem, confirming it is a bound morpheme on the head noun.

2. **Noun–Adjective order:** The adjective is a separate word that follows the noun (head-first order).

   *Evidence:* Example 9 (a-rə gəla = "my big house"), example 10 (u-wə təla = "your (sg) old child"), and example 21 (tə dala = "his long path") all show the noun preceding the adjective.

3. **Plural suffix with allomorphs:** Plurality of the possessed noun is marked by a suffix that has two allomorphs conditioned by the final vowel of the stem:
   - *-ra* after stems ending in /ə/
   - *-la* after stems ending in /a/

   When a possessive prefix is present, the structure is [prefix + stem + plural-suffix].

   *Evidence for -ra after /ə/:* Examples 11 (a-rə-ra), 12 (u-wə-ra), 13 (i-gə-ra), 14 (rə-ra) all show -ra following stems ending in /ə/ (rə, wə, gə).

   *Evidence for -la after /a/:* Examples 24 (a-ra-la), 25 (ra-la), 27 (ba-la) all show -la following stems ending in /a/ (ra, ba).

   *Critical disambiguation:* The plural allomorph *-la* (fused, marked by hyphen) is a distinct morpheme from the postposition *la* (free, marked by space). Example 28 (a-ra la = "in my road") shows *la* as a separate postposition following the singular stem *ra*, while example 24 (a-ra-la = "my roads (pl)") shows *-la* as a fused plural suffix. Example 29 (i-ba-la gəla la = "in our big trees (pl)") contains BOTH: the fused *-la* (plural suffix on *ba*) and the free *la* (postposition) in the same phrase.

4. **Postpositions:** Locative/directional relations are expressed by separate postpositions following the noun phrase:
   - la = "in/on"
   - na = "to/toward"

   *Evidence:* Examples 15–16 introduce la and na as separate words following the noun. Their meanings are clear from the translations.

5. **Linear order within the noun phrase:** The full order is [possessive prefix + stem (+ plural suffix)] + adjective + postposition.

   *Evidence:* Example 17 (a-rə gəla la = "in my big house") shows adjective before postposition. Example 18 (u-wə-ra wəla na = "to your (sg) new children (pl)") confirms the same order with a plural noun. Example 29 (i-ba-la gəla la = "in our big trees (pl)") confirms the order with the -la allomorph.

6. **Conjunction:** *ma* = "and," appearing between coordinated noun phrases.

   *Evidence:* Example 19 (a-rə ma u-wə = "my house and your (sg) child").

7. **Critical segmentation: stem *ra* vs. suffix *-ra*:** The noun stem *ra* ("road") is identical in form to the plural allomorph *-ra* (after /ə/-final stems). This creates a segmentation ambiguity in forms containing the sequence *ra-ra*. The correct analysis is determined by cross-referencing the paradigm:
   - Example 22: *a-ra* = "my road" (singular) → the single *ra* after the prefix is the stem.
   - Example 23: *ra* = "his road" (singular) → the bare stem is *ra*.
   - Example 24: *a-ra-la* = "my roads (pl)" → must be [a- + ra + -la]; the stem is *ra*, the suffix is *-la* (the /a/-final allomorph).
   - Example 25: *ra-la* = "his roads (pl)" → [ra + -la]; confirms the second *-la* is the suffix.
   - Parallel with example 14: *rə-ra* = "his houses (pl)" → [rə + -ra] structure with the /ə/-final allomorph.

   The solver must use the singular/plural contrast (examples 22–23 vs. 24–25) and the allomorph pattern to correctly segment forms.

8. **Critical segmentation: suffix *-la* vs. postposition *la*:** The plural allomorph *-la* (after /a/-final stems) is homographic with the postposition *la* ("in/on"). The distinction is:
   - *-la* is a bound morpheme (fused to the stem, marked by hyphen)
   - *la* is a free word (separate from the noun, marked by space)

   *Evidence:* Example 28 (a-ra la = "in my road") vs. example 24 (a-ra-la = "my roads (pl)"). The same surface string "ra la" vs. "ra-la" has different meanings. Example 29 (i-ba-la gəla la) contains both in a single phrase, confirming they are distinct morphemes.

**How the rules produce each answer:**

- **Q1:** *u-* (2SG) + *mə* (mother) → "your (sg) mother."

- **Q2:** *i-* (1PL) + *ra* (road, singular — no plural suffix present) + *dala* (long) → "our long road."
  - Key: The single *ra* after *i-* is the stem, not the plural suffix. Compare example 22 (a-ra = singular) with example 24 (a-ra-la = plural). The absence of a second suffix confirms singular.

- **Q3:** *u-* (2SG) + *ra* (road) + *-la* (plural allomorph after /a/-final stem) + *gəla* (big) → "your (sg) big roads (pl)."
  - Key: The *-la* is fused to the stem (hyphenated), identifying it as the plural suffix, not the postposition. Compare with example 28 where *la* is a separate word.

- **Q4:** *a-* (1SG) + *gə* (boat) + *la* (in/on, free postposition) → "in my boat."
  - Key: *la* is a separate word (space before it), identifying it as the postposition. The stem *gə* ends in /ə/, so the plural allomorph would be *-ra*, not *-la*. There is no ambiguity here.

- **Q5:** *i-* (1PL) + *ba* (tree) + *-la* (plural allomorph after /a/-final stem) + *wəla* (new) + *na* (to/toward) → "to our new trees (pl)."
  - Key: *-la* is fused to the stem (hyphenated), identifying it as the plural suffix. *na* is the free postposition. The solver must not confuse the fused *-la* with the postposition *la*.

- **Q6:** 1PL → *i-*; boat → *gə*; plural → *-ra* (stem ends in /ə/); new → *wəla* (adjective after noun) → **i-gə-ra wəla**.
  - Key: The solver must select the correct allomorph (-ra, not -la) based on the stem's final vowel.

- **Q7:** 2SG → *u-*; road → *ra*; plural → *-la* (stem ends in /a/); big → *gəla*; in/on → *la* → **u-ra-la gəla la**.
  - Key: This is the most critical question. The output contains BOTH the fused plural suffix *-la* (part of the noun) AND the free postposition *la* (separate word). The solver must produce *u-ra-la gəla la* with correct hyphenation: the first *-la* is attached to the stem, the second *la* is a separate word.

- **Q8:** 3SG road → *ra* (zero prefix); long → *dala*; to/toward → *na*; and → *ma*; 1PL tree → *i-ba*; plural → *-la* (stem ends in /a/); new → *wəla*; in/on → *la* → **ra dala na ma i-ba-la wəla la**.
  - Key: Requires correct application of zero prefix, allomorph selection for *ba* (-la), adjective order, postposition placement, and conjunction in a single coordinated structure.

DIFFICULTY

**Level 5.** The puzzle requires identifying multiple morphological and syntactic rules and applying them in interaction. The primary difficulty elevations over a simpler puzzle are:

1. **Allomorph discovery:** The plural suffix is not invariant; it has two allomorphs (-ra, -la) conditioned by the stem's final vowel. The solver must discover this pattern from the distributional data rather than assuming a single invariant suffix.

2. **Homographic morpheme disambiguation (-la vs. la):** The plural allomorph *-la* is homographic with the postposition *la*. The solver must use orthographic cues (hyphen vs. space) and distributional evidence to distinguish the two, and must correctly apply both in a single output (Q7). This creates a genuine competing-analysis situation: a form like *ra-la* could initially be misread as "road in/on" before the solver recognizes the allomorph pattern.

3. **Homophonic stem-suffix interaction (ra vs. -ra):** The stem *ra* ("road") is identical in form to the plural allomorph *-ra* (after /ə/-final stems). The solver must use the singular/plural paradigm to correctly segment forms like *a-ra* (singular) vs. *a-ra-la* (plural).

4. **Combined application under ambiguity:** Questions Q7 and Q8 require the solver to simultaneously apply allomorph selection, prefix selection, adjective order, postposition placement, and conjunction while correctly managing the -la/la and ra/-ra ambiguities. Q7 specifically demands producing a string where the same phonological sequence "la" appears as two different morphemes (fused suffix and free postposition) in the correct positions.

5. **Competing analyses resolved by later evidence:** A solver who encounters examples 24–25 early might hypothesize that *-la* is simply the postposition "in/on" attached to the noun. This hypothesis is only resolved by example 28 (showing *ra la* as a separate-word postposition) and example 29 (showing both in one phrase). The puzzle is designed so that the full set of evidence is needed to arrive at the correct analysis.
