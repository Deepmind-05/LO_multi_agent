QUESTIONS

SOLUTION

**Inferred rules:**

1. **SOV word order.** Examples 1–6 consistently show Subject + Object + Verb order. The subject is a pronoun or noun in unmarked form; the verb is final.

2. **Accusative case suffix -ŋa on object nouns.** In all transitive clauses (1–6), the object noun bears the suffix -ŋa. The subject noun (example 6: *táŋ*) is unmarked.

3. **Noun–Adjective order.** Examples 7–8 show the adjective following the noun (*páw táŋ* = big house).

4. **Possessive prefixes on the possessed noun (head marking).** Examples 9–14 show that the possessed noun bears a prefix encoding the possessor's person: *tá-* (1sg), *ku-* (2sg), *no-* (3sg). The possessor appears as a separate word before the possessed noun (Genitive–Noun order). Third-person noun possessors (e.g., *máŋ* in example 12) take the 3sg prefix *no-*.

5. **Two possessive strategies.** Attributive possession (within a noun phrase) uses the possessive prefix (examples 9–14). Predicative possession (as a clause) uses the verb *páŋ* "have" with the possessed noun as a plain accusative object (examples 15–17). The possessed noun in the "have" construction does NOT bear a possessive prefix.

6. **Negation suffix -na on the verb.** Examples 18–21 show that negation is expressed by the suffix -na attached to the verb stem, consistent across different verbs (*ŋáŋ-na*, *sáŋ-na*, *páŋ-na*).

7. **Interrogative suffix -ka on the verb.** Examples 22–25 show that polar questions are marked by the suffix -ka on the verb, distinct from -na.

8. **Past tense suffix -ta on the verb.** Examples 26–28 show a past tense marker -ta on the verb.

9. **Verbal suffix order: stem + tense + negation + question.** Example 29 (*ŋáŋ-ta-na*) shows tense before negation. Example 30 (*sáŋ-ta-ka*) shows tense before question. Example 31 (*páŋ-ta-na-ka*) shows the full order: tense + negation + question.

10. **Plural suffix -ri on nouns.** Examples 34–35 show plural -ri on nouns. The order on nouns is: stem + plural + accusative (examples 34–35: *táŋ-ri-ŋa*, *míŋ-ri-ŋa*).

11. **Possessive prefix + plural + accusative can co-occur.** Example 37 (*tá-míŋ-ri-ŋa*) shows the full noun morphology: possessive prefix before the stem, plural and accusative suffixes after.

12. **Internally-headed relative clauses.** In examples 32–33 and 38, the head noun appears inside the modifying clause (in the object position of the embedded clause), not outside it. The RC functions as a noun phrase (subject in 32, object in 33 and 38). The head noun retains its accusative marking inside the RC.

**How the rules produce each answer:**

- **Q1** (*nó no-sáw*): 3sg possessor *nó* + possessed noun *sáw* with 3sg prefix *no-* (rule 4).
- **Q2** (*tíŋ no-míŋ*): 3sg noun possessor *tíŋ* + possessed noun *míŋ* with 3sg prefix *no-* (rule 4, as in example 12).
- **Q3** (*kú míŋ-ŋa páŋ-na*): SOV clause with "have" verb (rule 5), object with accusative (rule 2), negation suffix (rule 6).
- **Q4** (*nó sáw-ŋa páŋ-ka?*): SOV clause with "have" verb, object with accusative, interrogative suffix (rules 1, 2, 5, 7).
- **Q5** (*tá tá-táŋ-ŋa sáŋ*): SOV clause (rule 1), object with possessive prefix + accusative (rules 2, 4, as in example 36).
- **Q6** (*kú kúŋ-ŋa ŋáŋ-ta-na-ka?*): SOV clause, object with accusative, verb with tense + negation + question suffixes in that order (rules 1, 2, 9).
- **Q7** (*tá ku-míŋ-ri-ŋa sáŋ*): SOV clause, object with 2sg possessive prefix + plural + accusative (rules 1, 2, 4, 10, 11, as in example 37).
- **Q8a** (*tá tá-páw táŋ*): Attributive possessive NP as subject + adjectival predicate (rules 3, 4). **Q8b** (*nó sáw-ŋa páŋ*): Predicative "have" clause (rules 1, 2, 5).
- **Q9** (*tá míŋ-ŋa sáŋ táŋ*): Internally-headed RC (head *míŋ* in object position of embedded clause) + adjectival predicate (rules 3, 12, as in example 32).
- **Q10** (*tá tá-máŋ páw-ŋa wáŋ táŋ*): Internally-headed RC with 1sg possessive prefix on the subject of the embedded clause (*tá-máŋ* = my mother) + adjectival predicate (rules 4, 12, as in examples 32–33).
- **Q11** (*kú tá táŋ-ŋa wáŋ sáŋ-ta-na-ka?*): SOV main clause with internally-headed RC as object (head *táŋ* in object position of embedded clause) + main verb with tense + negation + question suffixes (rules 1, 2, 9, 12, as in examples 33, 38).

DIFFICULTY

**Level 4.** The puzzle requires the solver to identify and integrate multiple interacting systems: a head-marking possessive prefix system, a dual possessive strategy (prefixal vs. verbal), a coherent suffixing verbal morphology with a fixed suffix order, accusative case marking, and—most challenging—an internally-headed relative clause structure. The questions progress from single-pattern application (Q1–Q2) through multi-rule combinations (Q3–Q7) to the most complex integration of RC structure with stacked verbal morphology (Q11). The internally-headed RC is the primary source of difficulty, requiring the solver to recognize a non-standard clause structure and apply it in novel contexts with additional morphological complexity.
