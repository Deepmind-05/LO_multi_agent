

TARGET-LANGUAGE: Amele (Trans-New Guinea > Madang > Mabuso)

CONSTRUCTION RATIONALE

1. **Establish the possessive class system as the central puzzle mechanism.** Select 4–6 possessive classes (not more, per Constraint 9). Assign each class a semantically motivated but not perfectly predictable grouping (e.g., body parts/kinship, animals, plants, inanimate objects, abstract/conceptual nouns, human beings). The semantic motivation should be loose enough that at least one noun's class membership is not obvious from meaning alone — this creates a genuine inference task where the solver must rely on the suffix allomorph rather than semantic intuition. Assign a distinct suffix allomorph to each class. The allomorphs must be phonologically distinct (Constraint 7): ensure no two classes share an allomorph that differs by only one segment in a way that could be confused in the data. The allomorphs should be suffixes on the possessor (dependent marking, WALS 57A-2), not on the possessed noun.

2. **Design the person/number paradigm for the possessive suffix.** The possessive suffix must vary by person and number of the possessor (at minimum: 1SG, 2SG, 3SG, 1PL, 2PL, 3PL — or a subset thereof). For each person/number value, the suffix has one allomorph per possessive class. This creates a matrix: rows = person/number, columns = possessive class. The solver must reconstruct this matrix from the data. To make the puzzle non-trivial, ensure that: (a) at least two person/number values share a similar allomorph pattern across classes (creating a potential for confusion that the data must resolve); (b) at least one person/number value has a distinctive allomorph that is not predictable from the others (a "surprise" allomorph that the solver must notice). Do NOT make the allomorphs purely regular (e.g., a single suffix with person/number prefixes) — the class-based variation must be the primary source of form, with person/number as a secondary layer.

3. **Select the noun inventory.** For each possessive class, select at least 2 distinct possessed nouns (Constraint 1). This minimum of 2 per class is essential: with only one noun per class, the solver cannot confirm that the class boundary is real (a single noun could be an exception). Select a total of 10–15 possessed nouns across all classes. Among these, include: (a) at least 3 human nouns (for the plural interaction in Step 6); (b) at least 2 nouns whose semantic category is ambiguous between two classes (these are the "surprise" nouns — the solver must use the suffix, not meaning, to assign them); (c) a mix of nouns that will appear in both attributive and predicative possessive constructions (for Step 5).

4. **Design the negative evidence for gender and numeral classifiers.** This is critical for the CORE inference that the classes are specifically "possessive classes" and not a general gender or classifier system. The data must include: (a) at least 3 instances where an adjective follows a noun from different possessive classes, with the adjective form being identical regardless of the noun's class (WALS 87A-2: Noun-Adjective order, no agreement); (b) at least 2 instances where a demonstrative follows a noun from different possessive classes, with the demonstrative form being identical regardless of class (WALS 88A-2: Noun-Demonstrative order); (c) at least 2 instances where a numeral follows a noun from different possessive classes, with no class-based modification of the numeral (WALS 89A-2: Noun-Numeral order, no classifiers). The adjectives, demonstratives, and numerals used should be the same forms across all instances (to make the absence of agreement maximally visible). This negative evidence should be distributed across multiple parts of the puzzle (not all in one block) so the solver accumulates the constraint gradually.

5. **Design the conjunctional possessive and the 'and/with/has' overlap.** Select a single lexical item that serves three functions: (a) coordinative conjunction ("and"), (b) comitative preposition/postposition ("with"), (c) predicative possessive marker ("has"). The predicative possessive construction should have the structure: [Possessor] [conjunction] [Possessed Noun] (with no possessive suffix on the possessor in this construction — the suffix is only used in attributive possession). The comitative construction should have the structure: [NP1] [conjunction] [NP2] (meaning "NP1 with NP2"). The coordinative construction should have the structure: [NP1] [conjunction] [NP2] (meaning "NP1 and NP2"). The solver must distinguish these three uses from context. To create difficulty, include at least one sentence where the conjunctional possessive and the comitative are structurally identical but differ only in semantic interpretation (the solver must use context to disambiguate). This tests the solver's ability to recognize that one form has three functions and that the possessive class system does NOT apply in the predicative construction (no suffix on the possessor).

6. **Design the human-only plural system.** Select a plural marking strategy (e.g., a suffix, a prefix, or a suppletive stem change) that applies ONLY to human nouns and is OBLIGATORY for them (WALS 34A-3). Non-human nouns must remain unmarked regardless of number. Include in the data: (a) at least 2 human nouns appearing in plural (with the plural marker) and at least 2 human nouns appearing in singular (without the marker); (b) at least 2 non-human nouns appearing in contexts where plural reference is semantically clear (e.g., "the animals ran," "the trees fell") but WITHOUT any plural marking. The critical interaction: a human noun must appear in a possessive construction where its possessive class is NOT the "human" class (if one exists) — or, if there is no dedicated "human" possessive class, a human noun must appear in a class that also contains non-human nouns. This forces the solver to recognize that the human/non-human distinction (for plural) and the possessive class distinction are orthogonal systems.

7. **Construct Part 1: Baseline possessive data.** Provide 12–18 word/phrase–gloss pairs showing attributive possessive constructions. The structural template is: [Possessor + POSS.SUFFIX] [Possessed Noun] (GenN order, WALS 86A-1). This part must: (a) include all person/number values, each appearing at least twice with different possessed nouns; (b) include at least 2 nouns from each possessive class (so the solver can begin grouping); (c) include at least one instance of each class's suffix allomorph for at least 2 different person/number values (so the solver can see the class × person/number interaction); (d) include the "surprise" nouns (semantically ambiguous class membership) — the solver will initially misassign them based on meaning, but the suffix will correct this; (e) NOT include any modifiers, numerals, or predicative possession (these are reserved for later parts). The solver's expected discovery sequence in this part: first, identify that the possessor is marked (not the possessed noun); second, notice that the suffix form varies; third, begin grouping possessed nouns by which suffix they trigger; fourth, notice that the suffix also varies by person/number of the possessor.

8. **Construct Part 2: Modifier and numeral data (negative evidence).** Provide 8–12 phrases showing nouns with adjectives, demonstratives, and numerals. The structural templates are: [Noun] [Adjective] (WALS 87A-2), [Noun] [Demonstrative] (WALS 88A-2), [Noun] [Numeral] (WALS 89A-2). This part must: (a) use nouns from at least 3 different possessive classes (so the solver can compare); (b) use the SAME adjective, demonstrative, and numeral forms across all instances (making the absence of class-based variation maximally salient); (c) include at least one instance where a human noun appears with a numeral (showing no classifier) and at least one where a non-human noun appears with a numeral (also no classifier); (d) include at least one instance where a human noun appears in plural with an adjective (showing that the plural marker is on the noun, not the adjective). The solver's expected discovery: the modifiers do not change form based on the noun's possessive class → the classes are NOT a gender system. The numerals do not trigger class-based forms → the classes are NOT numeral classifiers. The plural marker appears only on human nouns → a separate, simpler classification.

9. **Construct Part 3: Predicative possession and conjunction data.** Provide 6–10 sentences showing: (a) at least 3 predicative possessive constructions (X has Y) using the conjunction; (b) at least 2 comitative constructions (X with Y) using the same conjunction; (c) at least 2 coordinative constructions (X and Y) using the same conjunction; (d) at least 1 sentence where the conjunctional possessive and comitative are structurally ambiguous (requiring context to disambiguate). The structural template for all three is: [NP1] [CONJ] [NP2]. The solver's expected discovery: the same word is used in all three functions; the possessive class suffix does NOT appear in predicative possession (the possessor is unmarked); the attributive and predicative strategies are distinct. This part also provides the opportunity to include at least one human noun in plural in a predicative possession (e.g., "the men have a house") to reinforce the orthogonality of plural and possessive class.

10. **Construct Part 4: Production and application.** Provide 8–10 English glosses for the solver to translate into Amele. Design these to test the full range of discovered knowledge: (a) at least 2 items require selecting the correct possessive class suffix for a NEW possessed noun (not seen in Parts 1–3) — the solver must determine the class from semantic knowledge of the system; (b) at least 1 item requires producing a predicative possessive (testing that the solver knows NOT to use the suffix); (c) at least 1 item requires producing a comitative or coordinative construction (testing that the solver can use the conjunction in a non-possessive function); (d) at least 1 item involves a human noun in plural in a possessive construction (testing orthogonality of plural and possessive class); (e) at least 1 item involves a "surprise" noun (semantically ambiguous class) where the solver must commit to a class based on the system they have reconstructed; (f) at least 1 item combines multiple elements (e.g., a human plural possessor + a possessed noun from a specific class + an adjective on the possessed noun). Order items from easiest to hardest.

11. **Verify uniqueness, solvability, and internal consistency.** Check the following: (a) The possessive class matrix (person/number × class) is uniquely determined by the data — no two different matrices can produce the same surface forms. (b) The class boundaries are uniquely determined — no alternative grouping of nouns into classes is consistent with all the suffix allomorphs. (c) The "surprise" nouns are genuinely ambiguous from meaning alone but unambiguous from the suffix — the solver cannot resolve them without having identified the class system. (d) The negative evidence (Part 2) is sufficient to rule out both gender and classifier interpretations — a solver who has only seen Part 1 might hypothesize gender, but Part 2 must eliminate that hypothesis. (e) The conjunctional possessive is clearly distinguishable from attributive possession in the data — the structural difference (presence/absence of suffix) must be unambiguous. (f) The human/non-human plural distinction is clearly observable — the solver can identify which nouns are human from the data without external knowledge. (g) No accidental homophony or ambiguity exists that could confuse the solver (e.g., a possessive suffix allomorph that is identical to a free morpheme in the data). (h) The total number of distinct forms the solver must track is manageable (aim for no more than ~30 distinct forms across the entire puzzle).

12. **Write the problem preamble and instructions.** Include: (a) a brief context for Amele (location in Papua New Guinea, language family, speaker community size) to frame the puzzle without giving away any linguistic properties; (b) a phonology note listing any non-ASCII characters and their approximations (e.g., if labial-velar consonants are used, note them; if clicks appear, note them); (c) clear instructions for each part: Part 1 = "Identify the pattern of possessive marking. What is marked, and how does the marking vary?"; Part 2 = "What do the modifiers and numerals tell you about the noun classification system? What type of classification is this?"; Part 3 = "Identify the function of the word [X] in each sentence. How is predicative possession expressed? How does it differ from attributive possession?"; Part 4 = "Translate the following into Amele."; (d) point values reflecting difficulty (e.g., Part 1: 4 marks, Part 2: 3 marks, Part 3: 4 marks, Part 4: 5 marks, for a 16-mark problem); (e) a note that the solver should use only the data provided and general linguistic knowledge (no prior knowledge of Amele is assumed or required).
