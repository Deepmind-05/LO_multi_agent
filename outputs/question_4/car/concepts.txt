

TARGET-LANGUAGE: Carib (Cariban, South America)

CORE

1. Concept: Dependent marking in possessive noun phrases (source 81A-1)
   - Target-language realization: Possessive prefixes on the possessed noun (WALS 57A-1). The possessor is encoded as a prefix on the possessed noun, e.g., a 1SG prefix on a noun meaning "my house."
   - Intended solver inference: The solver observes that the possessor (the dependent in the possessive relationship) is marked on the possessed noun (the head), and identifies this as dependent marking.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

2. Concept: Ergative alignment in verbal person marking (replaces source 100A-3, which does not fit Carib)
   - Target-language realization: Ergative alignment (WALS 100A-3). Intransitive subjects (S) are marked with the same set of person markers as transitive patients (P); transitive agents (A) are marked with a distinct set. Both A and P are marked on the verb (WALS 102A-5). No zero realization of 3rd person (WALS 103A-2).
   - Intended solver inference: By comparing person markers across intransitive and transitive clauses, the solver identifies that S and P share a marker set while A has a separate set, revealing ergative alignment.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

TARGET-SPECIFIC

1. Phenomenon: Inclusive/exclusive distinction in 1st person plural
   - Intended solver inference: The solver observes two distinct 1PL forms (in pronouns and/or possessive prefixes) and infers a semantic distinction between "we including you" and "we excluding you." The verbal inflection differentiates only inclusive (WALS 40A-4), while independent pronouns distinguish both (WALS 39A-5).
   - Interaction with CORE: The inclusive/exclusive distinction is reflected in possessive prefixes (e.g., "our-incl. house" vs. "our-excl. house"), enriching the possessive marking system. It also appears in the verbal person marking, adding a dimension to the ergative alignment puzzle.
   - Difficulty contribution: MEDIUM. A well-known typological feature; the solver can use general linguistic knowledge to hypothesize the inclusive/exclusive distinction once two 1PL forms are observed.
   - Priority: HIGH

2. Phenomenon: 3rd person semantic (gender) distinctions limited to 3rd person
   - Intended solver inference: The solver observes that 3rd person pronouns (and 3rd person possessive prefixes) have multiple forms corresponding to different semantic categories (e.g., masculine/feminine or animate/inanimate), while 1st and 2nd person do not show this distinction. The key insight is the asymmetry: the distinction is restricted to 3rd person (WALS 44A-2).
   - Interaction with CORE: Different 3rd person semantic categories produce different possessive prefixes, adding complexity to the possessive system. In the verbal system, 3rd person A and 3rd person P would each have multiple forms, complicating the ergative alignment analysis.
   - Difficulty contribution: MEDIUM-HIGH. The restriction to 3rd person is the critical insight; the solver must notice that 1st and 2nd person lack this distinction.
   - Priority: HIGH

3. Phenomenon: A and P person markers fused (single core-argument slot)
   - Intended solver inference: The solver observes that the verb has a single set of person markers that serve as S in intransitive clauses and P in transitive clauses, while A is marked by a separate morpheme. The "fusion" of A and P in the verb template (WALS 104A-5) means they appear as an adjacent unit, but the S/P set is distinct from the A set.
   - Interaction with CORE: This is the morphological mechanism that realizes the ergative alignment. It clarifies the structure of the verbal person marking system and helps the solver distinguish the S/P marker set from the A marker set.
   - Difficulty contribution: MEDIUM. Helps the solver parse the verbal morphology correctly; without this insight, the solver might misanalyze the alignment.
   - Priority: HIGH

4. Phenomenon: Syncretism in person/number marking
   - Intended solver inference: The solver observes that some person/number combinations share the same marker (WALS 29A-2), requiring careful comparison to distinguish all forms.
   - Interaction with CORE: Syncretism adds complexity to both the possessive prefix system and the verbal person marking, requiring the solver to use context to disambiguate.
   - Difficulty contribution: MEDIUM. Prevents the puzzle from being too straightforward; the solver must account for shared forms.
   - Priority: OPTIONAL

INTERACTIONS

1. Possessive marking × Ergative alignment:
   - Both systems involve marking relationships between arguments. The possessive system shows dependent marking (possessor marked on possessed), while the verbal system shows ergative alignment (S/P marked together, A marked separately). The solver can draw a parallel: in both systems, one argument is "primary" (the possessed noun / the S-P core) and the other is marked differently (the possessor prefix / the A marker).
   - Additional inference: The solver can recognize a consistent pattern of "core vs. peripheral" marking across the grammar.

2. Inclusive/exclusive × Possessive marking:
   - The 1PL inclusive/exclusive distinction is reflected in the possessive prefix system, creating two distinct 1PL possessive prefixes. This enriches the possessive paradigm and gives the solver additional data to work with.
   - Additional inference: The solver can confirm the inclusive/exclusive analysis by observing that the two 1PL possessive prefixes behave differently in context.

3. 3rd person distinctions × Ergative alignment:
   - The 3rd person semantic distinctions multiply the number of 3rd person forms in both the S/P set and the A set. This creates a more complex pattern that the solver must untangle.
   - Additional inference: The solver must determine which 3rd person forms belong to the S/P set and which to the A set, using the semantic categories as a guide.

4. Inclusive/exclusive × Ergative alignment:
   - In verbal inflection, only inclusive is differentiated (WALS 40A-4). This means that in the verbal person marking, 1PL-inclusive has a distinct form while 1PL-exclusive may be syncretic with another form. This asymmetry between the pronominal and verbal systems is an additional layer of complexity.
   - Additional inference: The solver must notice that the verbal system does not fully reflect the pronominal distinction, and account for this asymmetry.

CONSTRAINTS

1. The puzzle must provide sufficient data to demonstrate ergative alignment: at minimum, intransitive and transitive clauses with 1st, 2nd, and 3rd person arguments must be shown, allowing the solver to compare S/P markers with A markers.

2. The possessive prefix system must be clearly distinguishable from verbal morphology. Possessive prefixes should appear on nouns, not verbs, to avoid confusion.

3. The inclusive/exclusive distinction must be inferable from the data: at least two 1PL forms with different referential meanings must be presented (e.g., in different sentences with different participants).

4. The 3rd person semantic distinctions must be clearly limited to 3rd person: 1st and 2nd person forms must NOT show this distinction, so the solver can identify the asymmetry.

5. No morphological case marking should appear in the puzzle data (consistent with WALS 49A-1, 50A-1), to keep the focus on person and possessive marking.

6. The puzzle should avoid relying on the solver's knowledge of Carib-specific vocabulary. All linguistic patterns must be inferable from the morphological and distributional evidence alone.

7. Syncretism (if included) must not make the puzzle unsolvable: the solver must be able to disambiguate syncretic forms using context or additional data points.
