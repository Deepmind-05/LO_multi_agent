

TARGET-LANGUAGE: Abkhaz (Northwest Caucasian)

CORE

1. Possessive marking (adapted from source 81A: Dependent marking)
   - Target-language realization: Head marking — the possessed noun bears a possessive prefix indicating the possessor (e.g., 1sg, 2sg, 3sg masc/fem/neut, 1pl, 2pl, 3pl). The possessor noun/pronoun is unmarked.
   - Intended solver inference: From parallel examples (e.g., "my house" vs. "your house" vs. "his house"), the solver discovers that the possessed noun carries a prefix encoding the possessor's person/number/gender, while the possessor itself is unmarked. This is the inverse of the source language's strategy and of English genitive strategy.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

2. Noun classification with agreement (adapted from source 100A: Noun classes without agreement / classifiers)
   - Target-language realization: Three-gender system (masculine, feminine, neuter) with semantic assignment and agreement. Gender is reflected in demonstratives, adjectives, and 3rd-person verbal morphology. Unlike the source (classifiers without agreement), Abkhaz gender triggers agreement on other elements.
   - Intended solver inference: From examples showing different forms of demonstratives/adjectives/verbs paired with different nouns, the solver discovers a three-way classification of nouns and that this classification is reflected in agreement on dependent elements.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

TARGET-SPECIFIC

1. Ergative alignment in verbal person marking
   - Phenomenon: The verb marks the A (transitive agent) and P/S (transitive patient / intransitive subject) with different morphemes. The same person is coded differently depending on whether it functions as A or as S/P.
   - Intended solver inference: By comparing intransitive sentences (where the subject is S) with transitive sentences (where the same person is A), the solver discovers that the verbal prefix/suffix for that person changes depending on its grammatical role.
   - Interaction with CORE: The 3rd-person gender system (from CORE #2) interacts with ergative alignment — the 3rd person P/S marker may reflect gender, while the 3rd person A marker may be distinct.
   - Difficulty contribution: High — requires the solver to abstract the alignment pattern from multiple examples.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

2. P precedes A in verbal person marking order
   - Phenomenon: On the verb, the absolutive (P/S) person marker precedes the ergative (A) person marker.
   - Intended solver inference: From transitive examples, the solver discovers a fixed morphological order: [P-marker][A-marker] on the verb.
   - Interaction with CORE: Combined with ergative alignment, this creates a specific verbal template that the solver must reconstruct. The gender of the 3rd-person P may be visible in this position.
   - Difficulty contribution: Medium — the ordering is discoverable once the solver identifies the two person markers.
   - Priority: HIGH

3. Obligatory plural marking on all nouns
   - Phenomenon: Every noun must be marked for number (singular or plural suffix). No noun appears without a number marker.
   - Intended solver inference: From examples, the solver notices that no noun is "bare" — there is always a suffix indicating singular or plural.
   - Interaction with CORE: The plural suffix co-occurs with the possessive prefix on the same noun, creating a layered nominal morphology: [possessive prefix][stem][plural suffix].
   - Difficulty contribution: Low-medium — a clear, consistent pattern that adds a layer to nominal analysis.
   - Priority: HIGH

4. Gender distinctions in 1st and 2nd person pronouns
   - Phenomenon: Independent pronouns for 1st and 2nd person show gender distinctions (e.g., "I" has different forms for male and female speakers), in addition to the expected 3rd-person gender.
   - Intended solver inference: From pronoun examples, the solver notices that even "I" and "you" have gendered forms, extending the gender system beyond the typical 3rd-person-only pattern.
   - Interaction with CORE: Extends the three-gender system (CORE #2) into the pronominal domain more broadly than expected.
   - Difficulty contribution: Medium — surprising but directly observable from data.
   - Priority: OPTIONAL

5. Evidentiality (indirect only)
   - Phenomenon: A verbal affix or clitic marks indirect evidentiality (the speaker did not directly witness the event).
   - Intended solver inference: From paired examples (direct vs. indirect), the solver discovers a verbal marker that distinguishes how the speaker knows about the event.
   - Interaction with CORE: Adds another morphological layer to the already complex verb, interacting with person/number and alignment markers.
   - Difficulty contribution: Medium-high — adds a semantic dimension to verbal morphology.
   - Priority: OPTIONAL

6. Postpositions
   - Phenomenon: Spatial and relational words follow the noun phrase (postpositions rather than prepositions).
   - Intended solver inference: From examples, the solver notices that relational words come after the noun.
   - Interaction with CORE: Consistent with the head-final typological profile (SOV, N-Adj, Rel-N).
   - Difficulty contribution: Low — a simple ordering observation.
   - Priority: OPTIONAL

7. Noun-Adjective order
   - Phenomenon: Modifying adjectives follow the noun.
   - Intended solver inference: From examples, the solver notices the N-Adj order.
   - Interaction with CORE: The adjective may show gender agreement with the noun it follows, linking the gender system (CORE #2) to word order.
   - Difficulty contribution: Low.
   - Priority: OPTIONAL

INTERACTIONS

1. Possessive prefix + Gender + Plural on the noun:
   - Concepts: CORE #1, CORE #2, Target #3
   - Interaction: The possessed noun simultaneously encodes possessor (prefix), its own gender (affecting agreement on other elements), and number (suffix). This creates a rich nominal template: [possessor-prefix][stem][plural-suffix].
   - Additional inference: The solver must parse multiple morphological layers on a single word and identify which layer encodes what.

2. Ergative alignment + P-before-A ordering + 3rd-person gender:
   - Concepts: Target #1, Target #2, CORE #2
   - Interaction: In a transitive sentence with a 3rd-person P and a 1st-person A, the verb shows [3sg-gendered-P-marker][1sg-A-marker]. The solver must identify both the alignment (which argument gets which type of marker) and the ordering (P before A) and the gender effect (3rd-person P marker reflects gender).
   - Additional inference: This is the central "aha" moment of the puzzle — three properties converge on the verbal morphology.

3. Head marking in possession + Head marking in clauses:
   - Concepts: CORE #1, Target #1
   - Interaction: The language consistently marks the head of the relationship (possessed noun in possession; verb in clauses). This typological consistency is a unifying principle the solver can discover.
   - Additional inference: The solver can use the head-marking pattern in one domain to predict the pattern in another.

4. Gender in pronouns + Ergative alignment:
   - Concepts: Target #4, Target #1
   - Interaction: If 1st/2nd person pronouns show gender, and the verb marks A and P differently, the solver must determine whether the verbal person markers also reflect the speaker's gender (for 1st person) or the addressee's gender (for 2nd person).
   - Additional inference: Creates a question of whether gender is a property of the person or of the grammatical role.

CONSTRAINTS

1. No passive or antipassive constructions exist in Abkhaz. The ergative alignment must be presented consistently across all transitive examples without alternative constructions that could confuse the solver.

2. Person/number marking on the verb is non-syncretic: every person/number combination has a distinct form. The puzzle data must reflect this — no two person/number values may share the same verbal marker.

3. Third person has no zero realization on the verb. The 3rd-person marker must always be overtly present, so the solver can always identify it.

4. The puzzle must provide sufficient examples to demonstrate: (a) all three genders, (b) both singular and plural, (c) both intransitive and transitive sentences with multiple person combinations, (d) at least one example showing the possessive prefix on a noun. Without sufficient coverage, the solver cannot reliably infer the full system.

5. The source concept of "classifiers without agreement" (100A-3) is explicitly NOT realized in Abkhaz. The adapted concept must be "noun classification WITH agreement." The puzzle must not present gender as a classifier system; agreement must be visible in the data.

6. The source concept of "dependent marking in possession" (81A-1) is the OPPOSITE of Abkhaz's strategy. The puzzle must clearly demonstrate head marking (prefix on possessed noun) and must not accidentally create ambiguity that could be read as dependent marking.
