

TARGET-LANGUAGE: Hakka (hak)

CONSTRUCTION RATIONALE

1. **Define the phonological inventory and tone system.** Establish a consonant inventory containing only voiceless obstruents (e.g., p, t, k, f, s, h) plus sonorants (m, n, l, r, w, y). No voiced/voiceless pairs may appear anywhere in the puzzle data. Define a six-tone system, labeled 1 through 6 in the puzzle (use a consistent notation such as numerical superscripts: ka¹, ka², etc.). The six tones should be presented as abstract labels; do not provide phonetic descriptions (e.g., "high level") in the solver-facing material, as this is unnecessary and could bias the solver. The tone labels are the only tonal information the solver will see. This step constrains all subsequent lexical and phonological design.

2. **Define the sandhi rule precisely.** The sandhi rule governs how underlying tones surface in multi-syllable sequences. The rule is: in a sequence of n syllables [S₁ S₂ … Sₙ], the final syllable Sₙ surfaces with its underlying tone. Every non-final syllable Sᵢ (where i < n) surfaces with a tone determined solely by the underlying tone of the immediately following syllable Sᵢ₊₁, via a fixed function f: {1,2,3,4,5,6} → {1,2,3,4,5,6}. The function f must be a bijection (permutation) so that the mapping is fully determined and invertible in principle. Choose a specific permutation; for example: f(1)=4, f(2)=6, f(3)=1, f(4)=3, f(5)=2, f(6)=5. Verify that f is not the identity (otherwise no sandhi occurs) and that f(3) is a tone value that will serve as a distinctive surface marker (in this example, f(3)=1). This rule is the central phonological mechanism of the puzzle. The solver must discover it entirely from the data.

3. **Define the possessive construction.** The possessive (genitive) construction in this language has the structure: Possessor + 个 + Possessed. The particle 个 is a monosyllabic morpheme with a fixed segmental form (choose a syllable such as "go" or "ko" — it must use only voiceless obstruents and be segmentally distinct from all content-word syllables in the vocabulary). The particle has a fixed underlying tone; assign it tone 3 (or another tone, but tone 3 is recommended because f(3) then determines the fixed surface tone of the possessor). The possessed noun carries no possessive morphology. The possessor is marked by the particle (dependent marking). The linear order is strictly Possessor → Particle → Possessed. This construction is the primary syntactic context in which sandhi operates over three or more syllables.

4. **Design the vocabulary set.** Select 8–12 monosyllabic content nouns spanning at least three semantic categories (e.g., kinship terms, body parts or objects, locations or abstract nouns). The critical constraint is that the underlying tones of the nouns must cover all six tone values: at least one noun must have underlying tone 1, at least one tone 2, and so on through tone 6. This ensures the solver can determine all six values of f from the data. All syllables must use only the voiceless-obstruent consonant inventory defined in Step 1. No noun's segmental form may be identical to the particle 个. No two nouns may be homophonous (same segmental form + same underlying tone). Provide this vocabulary as a glossed list showing each syllable with its underlying (citation) tone and an English gloss. This list is the solver's reference for underlying forms.

5. **Generate disyllabic data to establish the sandhi rule.** Create a set of disyllabic words (compounds, adjectival phrases, or other two-syllable lexical items) such that the second syllable in the set covers all six underlying tone values. For each disyllabic word [X Y], the surface form is: surface(X) = f(underlying(Y)), surface(Y) = underlying(Y). Present these as glossed items in the data section. The solver's task at this stage is to notice that the first syllable's surface tone is not its citation tone, to identify that the second syllable's surface tone equals its citation tone (the anchor), and to reconstruct the function f by tabulating: for each observed underlying tone of the second syllable, what is the surface tone of the first syllable? With six disyllabic examples covering all six second-syllable tones, the solver can determine f completely. Order these items so that they are not grouped by the second syllable's tone (to prevent the solver from trivially reading off the pattern); instead, interleave them with other data.

6. **Generate possessive phrase data to establish the construction.** Create trisyllabic possessive phrases of the form [Possessor + 个 + Possessed]. For each phrase, compute the surface tones: the possessed (final) syllable surfaces with its underlying tone; the particle 个 surfaces with f(underlying tone of the possessed); the possessor surfaces with f(underlying tone of 个) = f(3), which is a fixed value (e.g., tone 1 in the example permutation). Present these as glossed items. The solver should be able to: (a) identify the particle 个 by its consistent segmental form in the middle position; (b) observe that the first syllable (possessor) always surfaces with the same tone regardless of which noun is the possessor — this fixed tone is f(3); (c) observe that the particle's surface tone varies and equals f(underlying tone of the possessed); (d) confirm that the possessed noun's surface tone equals its citation tone. Include at least four possessive phrases with different possessed nouns (covering at least four different underlying tones of the possessed) so the solver can verify the particle's sandhi behavior across contexts. Also include at least two possessive phrases with different possessor nouns to demonstrate that the possessor's surface tone is invariant (always f(3)) regardless of the possessor's underlying tone.

7. **Generate nested possessive data for extended sandhi chains.** Create at least two examples of nested possessives of the form [N₁ + 个 + N₂ + 个 + N₃] (e.g., "my father's house"). In a five-syllable sequence, the sandhi applies iteratively: N₃ surfaces with its underlying tone; 个₂ surfaces with f(N₃_underlying); N₂ surfaces with f(个_underlying) = f(3) (fixed); 个₁ surfaces with f(N₂_underlying); N₁ surfaces with f(个_underlying) = f(3) (fixed). The solver should observe that in a nested possessive, all noun positions (odd positions: 1, 3, 5) surface with the fixed tone f(3) except the final noun (position 5), which surfaces with its underlying tone, while all particle positions (even positions: 2, 4) surface with tones determined by the following noun's underlying tone. This creates a clear structural signature: the alternating pattern of fixed-tone and variable-tone syllables reveals the possessive chain structure. These examples should appear after the simple possessive examples in the data, so the solver has already identified the basic construction before encountering the extended form.

8. **Introduce controlled contrasts to eliminate competing hypotheses.** Include the following contrasts in the data:
   - A pair of possessive phrases that differ only in the possessed noun's underlying tone (e.g., possessed with tone 1 vs. possessed with tone 4). The possessor's surface tone should be identical in both (confirming it is fixed at f(3)), while the particle's surface tone should differ (confirming it depends on the possessed's tone). This isolates the particle's sandhi behavior.
   - A pair of possessive phrases that differ only in the possessor noun (two different nouns with different underlying tones as possessor). The possessor's surface tone should be identical in both (confirming the possessor's underlying tone is erased by sandhi), while the rest of the phrase is identical. This confirms dependent marking: the possessor's identity is not phonologically encoded in its own tone.
   - A disyllabic word and a possessive phrase that share a surface tone pattern but differ in structure. For example, a disyllabic word [X Y] where surface(X) = f(3) might superficially resemble the first syllable of a possessive phrase. The glosses and the presence/absence of the particle 个 should disambiguate. This prevents the solver from overgeneralizing the fixed-tone pattern.
   - A disyllabic word where the second syllable's underlying tone is 3 (the same as the particle's underlying tone). This tests whether the solver can distinguish the particle from a content noun that happens to have the same underlying tone, using segmental form as the distinguishing cue.

9. **Assemble the full data set and verify internal consistency.** Combine all items (disyllabic words, simple possessives, nested possessives, contrast pairs) into a single flat list of 14–20 glossed items. Do not group items by rule or type. The list should be ordered to support progressive discovery: earlier items should provide evidence for the sandhi rule and the basic possessive construction; later items should introduce nested possessives and the controlled contrasts. Verify that: (a) every item's surface form is correctly computed from the underlying forms using the sandhi rule; (b) no item's gloss is ambiguous or could be parsed in more than one way; (c) the particle 个 never appears outside a possessive construction in the data; (d) no content noun is segmentally identical to 个; (e) all consonants in all syllables are voiceless; (f) the fixed surface tone f(3) does not accidentally match the underlying tone of a noun that appears in a non-possessive context in a way that creates confusion.

10. **Design the questions in two tiers with progressive difficulty.**
    - **Tier 1 (Analysis, 3–4 items):**
      - Item 1: Given a disyllabic word from the data, identify the underlying tone of the first syllable (requires inverting f, which the solver has already reconstructed). Alternatively: state the sandhi rule in the solver's own words or as a mapping table.
      - Item 2: Identify which syllable in a given possessive phrase is the possessor, which is the particle, and which is the possessed. Justify using tonal evidence (the fixed-tone syllable is the possessor; the segmentally identifiable syllable is the particle).
      - Item 3: Given a novel disyllabic word (not in the data) with known underlying tones, predict its surface form. This tests direct application of f.
      - Item 4 (optional): Given a novel possessive phrase with known underlying tones, predict its full surface form. This tests application of f across a three-syllable chain.
    - **Tier 2 (Production, 3–4 items):**
      - Item 5: Translate a novel simple possessive phrase (e.g., "mother's hand") into the target language, producing the correct surface form with all tones. The nouns used must have underlying tones that were seen in the data (so the solver knows them from the vocabulary list), but the specific combination must be novel.
      - Item 6: Translate a novel nested possessive (e.g., "the child's father's house") into the target language. This requires applying the sandhi rule across a five-syllable chain. The solver must correctly compute the surface tone of each of the five syllables.
      - Item 7: Translate a nested possessive where the final noun has an underlying tone that was NOT seen as a final syllable in any data example (but IS in the vocabulary list). This tests whether the solver has truly internalized the rule (final syllable retains underlying tone) rather than memorizing specific surface forms.
      - Item 8 (hardest): Translate a nested possessive with three levels (N₁ + 个 + N₂ + 个 + N₃ + 个 + N₄, seven syllables) where at least one of the nouns has an underlying tone that only appeared in a non-final position in the data. This tests full iterative application of the sandhi rule and the possessive construction simultaneously.

11. **Self-verify solvability.** Solve the entire puzzle using only the provided data and vocabulary list. Confirm that: (a) the sandhi function f is uniquely determined by the disyllabic data (all six values are constrained); (b) the possessive construction (order, particle identity, marking strategy) is uniquely determined by the possessive data; (c) the nested possessive pattern follows necessarily from the already-established rules (no new rule is needed); (d) every question in Tier 1 and Tier 2 can be answered using only rules evidenced in the data; (e) no question requires the solver to know the underlying tone of a syllable that never appears in a final position in the data AND is not in the vocabulary list (if the vocabulary list provides all underlying tones, this is automatically satisfied); (f) the most likely alternative analyses (e.g., "the particle is part of the possessor noun," "the fixed tone is a grammatical marker on the possessor rather than a sandhi effect," "the sandhi depends on the current syllable's tone rather than the next syllable's tone") are each ruled out by at least one contrast in the data. If any alternative analysis survives, add a targeted contrast item to the data.
