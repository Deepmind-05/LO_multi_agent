

TARGET-LANGUAGE

Cantonese (cnt)

CONSTRUCTION RATIONALE

1. **Establish the data format and vocabulary.** Present all data in romanization with tone numbers (e.g., ngo5 ge3 syu1). Provide a gloss for every example in the format: [romanization] = [English gloss]. Select a controlled vocabulary of 10–14 nouns spanning at least four semantic classes that will each take a distinct classifier. Recommended classes: (a) books/documents (classifier: bun1), (b) animals (classifier: zek1), (c) flat/plate-like objects (classifier: zoeng1), (d) vehicles or long objects (classifier: goeng1). Include at least two nouns per class so the solver can confirm classifier assignment is class-based, not item-specific. Also include 3–4 personal pronouns (ngo5 "I", nei5 "you", gaa3 "he/she", ngo5de1 "we", nei5de1 "you pl.", gaa3de1 "they") and a small set of numerals (jat1 "one", ji6 "two", saam1 "three"). Ensure no noun is homophonous with the possessive particle or any classifier.

2. **Introduce simple possessive constructions (3–4 examples) to establish the particle and its position.** Provide examples of the form [Pronoun] + ge3 + [Noun] with glosses like "my book," "your cat," "his table," "their car." These examples should vary the possessor (1st sg., 2nd sg., 3rd sg., 3rd pl.) and the possessed noun (across at least two different semantic classes). The solver should be able to infer from these examples: (a) there is a consistent element (ge3) that appears between the possessor and the possessed noun; (b) the possessor precedes the possessed noun; (c) ge3 is invariant across all examples (it does not change with person, number, or noun class). At this stage, the solver should NOT yet be able to determine whether ge3 is a bound affix or a free word—this will be resolved later.

3. **Introduce quantified noun phrases WITHOUT possessives (3–4 examples) to establish the classifier system.** Provide examples of the form [Numeral] + [Classifier] + [Noun] with glosses like "three books," "two cats," "one table," "three cars." Vary the numeral (one, two, three) and the noun class (at least three different classifiers). The solver should be able to infer: (a) there is an obligatory element between the numeral and the noun; (b) this element varies depending on the noun (not the numeral); (c) the same noun always takes the same classifier across different numerals. The solver should NOT yet be able to determine the semantic principle behind classifier selection (this requires seeing enough examples to group nouns by class).

4. **Introduce possessive constructions WITH quantified possessed NPs (3–4 examples) to show the interaction between the possessive and classifier systems.** Provide examples of the form [Pronoun] + ge3 + [Numeral] + [Classifier] + [Noun] with glosses like "my three books," "your two cats," "his one table," "their three cars." These examples demonstrate that the possessive particle ge3 precedes the entire quantified NP (numeral + classifier + noun). The solver should now be able to infer: (a) the possessed element in a possessive construction can be a complex NP containing a classifier; (b) the classifier is part of the possessed NP, not part of the possessive construction; (c) the possessive particle marks the relationship between the possessor and the entire possessed NP as a unit. This step depends on the solver having already identified both ge3 (from step 2) and the classifier (from step 3).

5. **Introduce evidence for the isolating typology and the free-word status of ge3 (2–3 examples).** Provide examples where ge3 appears in a context that makes its status as a separate word transparent. For instance, include a sentence where the possessor is a multi-word NP (e.g., "the teacher's book" = gaa3 ge3 syu1, where gaa3 is a noun meaning "teacher" rather than a pronoun). This shows that ge3 follows any possessor NP, not just pronouns, and that it is a separate element (not a suffix on the possessor, since it appears after a full noun). Additionally, include one example where the possessed noun is followed by another element (e.g., a demonstrative or a verb), showing that ge3 does not attach to the possessed noun. The solver should now be able to infer: (a) ge3 is a free word, not a bound morpheme; (b) the possessive marking is "independent"—it is associated with the possessor side of the construction, not the possessed noun; (c) Cantonese relies on separate particles and word order rather than affixation.

6. **Introduce nested possessives (2–3 examples) to test recursive application.** Provide examples of the form [Pronoun/Noun] + ge3 + [Noun] + ge3 + [Noun] with glosses like "my teacher's book," "the student's cat's owner" (or similar). These examples show that the possessive construction is recursive: the possessed element can itself be a possessive phrase. The solver should be able to infer: (a) the construction is right-branching (each ge3 introduces a new possessor for the preceding NP); (b) the structure is [Possessor1 + ge3 + [Possessor2 + ge3 + Head]]; (c) the classifier system does not interfere with nesting (classifiers only appear when a numeral is present). This step depends on the solver having internalized the basic possessive structure from steps 2 and 4.

7. **Introduce controlled contrasts (minimal pairs) to prevent alternative analyses.** Include the following contrasts:
   - A pair where the only difference is the possessor (e.g., "my book" vs. "your book") to confirm that ge3 is invariant and the possessor is the variable element.
   - A pair where the only difference is the possessed noun's semantic class (e.g., "three books" vs. "three cats") to confirm that the classifier is selected by the noun, not the numeral.
   - A pair where the only difference is the numeral (e.g., "two books" vs. "three books") to confirm that the classifier is invariant across numerals for the same noun.
   - A pair contrasting a possessive with a quantified NP vs. a possessive without a numeral (e.g., "my book" vs. "my three books") to show that the classifier appears only when a numeral is present, confirming its obligatoriness with numerals and optionality without them.
   - A pair where the possessor is a pronoun vs. a common noun (e.g., "my book" vs. "the teacher's book") to confirm that ge3 is a general possessive particle, not a pronoun-specific clitic.

8. **Add a small set of non-possessive sentences (2–3 examples) to provide context and prevent overgeneralization.** Include simple SVO sentences (e.g., "I read the book," "The cat eats") that do not contain ge3 or classifiers. These serve two purposes: (a) they confirm that ge3 is not a general sentence particle or aspect marker (it only appears in possessive constructions); (b) they show that the language has a basic SVO word order, which is consistent with the possessor-before-possessed order within the NP (a general pre-modification tendency). The solver should be able to use these to rule out the hypothesis that ge3 is a verb or a general grammatical marker.

9. **Design the questions in two tiers with progressive difficulty.**
   - **Tier 1 (Translation from target language to English, 4 items):**
     - Item 1: A simple possessive with a 3rd-person singular possessor and an unquantified possessed noun (tests basic identification of ge3 and word order).
     - Item 2: A possessive with a quantified possessed NP (tests identification of the classifier within the possessive structure).
     - Item 3: A possessive with a common-noun possessor (tests that the solver recognizes ge3 as a general particle, not pronoun-specific).
     - Item 4: A two-level nested possessive (tests recursive application of the possessive construction).
   - **Tier 2 (Translation from English to target language, 4 items):**
     - Item 5: A possessive with a quantified possessed NP using a classifier the solver has seen in the data but in a different possessive context (tests generalization of classifier assignment).
     - Item 6: A two-level nested possessive where the inner possessed NP is quantified (tests interaction of nesting and classifiers: e.g., "my teacher's three books").
     - Item 7: A possessive with a plural possessor and a quantified possessed NP (tests that the possessor NP can be complex and that the classifier system operates independently of the possessor).
     - Item 8: A three-level nested possessive with a quantified innermost NP (tests full recursive application combined with the classifier system; this is the hardest item and requires the solver to correctly apply all rules simultaneously).

10. **Verify the problem by solving it yourself using only the provided data.** Confirm that: (a) every rule needed to answer every question is uniquely determined by the data—specifically, the solver can identify ge3 as a possessive particle, determine its position, identify the classifier system, determine classifier selection by noun class, and apply the recursive possessive construction; (b) no question requires knowledge of a rule not evidenced in the data (e.g., if a question uses a classifier not seen in the data, that classifier must be inferable from the semantic class of the noun based on the pattern established in the data); (c) the minimal pairs in step 7 are sufficient to rule out the most likely alternative analyses (e.g., the solver might initially assume ge3 is a verb or a general particle—step 8's non-possessive sentences should eliminate this; the solver might assume the classifier is selected by the numeral—step 7's numeral-contrast pair should eliminate this); (d) the difficulty progression is smooth, with each question requiring one or two additional rules beyond the previous question; (e) the puzzle does not require any prior knowledge of Cantonese, Chinese characters, or tonal phonology beyond what is provided in the romanization.
