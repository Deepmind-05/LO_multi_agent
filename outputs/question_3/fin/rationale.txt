

TARGET-LANGUAGE

Finnish (Finno-Ugric, Uralic)

CONSTRUCTION RATIONALE

1. **Establish the basic possessive construction and its word order.** Begin the data set with a small set of simple possessive constructions (4–6 items) in which a full possessor NP precedes a possessed noun. The possessor NP should be a proper name or a personal pronoun in genitive form (e.g., a form like *Johan* for "John.GEN" or *meidän* for "we.GEN"). The possessed noun should carry a suffix. The surface order is: [Possessor.GEN] [Possessed+POSS.SUFFIX]. The solver's first discovery is the linear order: the possessor comes before the possessed noun (GenN). This is a structural discovery that orients the solver's parsing of all subsequent data. At this stage, the solver should not yet be able to determine what the suffix encodes — that requires the next step. The data at this stage should vary the possessor (different names/pronouns) and the possessed noun (different lexical items) to prevent the solver from associating the suffix with a particular noun.

2. **Introduce suffix-only possessive constructions to isolate the possessive paradigm.** After the full-NP constructions, provide a set of suffix-only forms (no overt possessor NP) where the possessed noun alone carries the possessive suffix (e.g., a form like *taloni* = "house+1SG.POSS" = "my house"). These forms are critical because they allow the solver to see the possessive suffix in isolation, without the genitive-marked possessor NP present. Provide at least 5 different person/number values across these items (ideally all 6: 1SG, 2SG, 3SG, 1PL, 2PL, 3PL). The solver should now be able to infer: (a) the suffix on the possessed noun encodes the possessor's person and number (head marking), and (b) the specific paradigm values. The 3SG form (*-nsa*) and 3PL form (*-n*) are less transparent than the others; ensure they appear in contexts where the possessor's person/number is unambiguous (e.g., paired with a 3SG or 3PL possessor in a full-NP construction elsewhere in the data). This step is the core "decoding" task of the puzzle.

3. **Establish the genitive case on the possessor and the agreement relationship.** Return to full-NP constructions and ensure the data includes at least 3–4 different possessor persons/numbers, each with a corresponding possessive suffix on the possessed noun. The solver should now notice that the genitive form of the possessor and the possessive suffix on the possessed noun are not independent: they encode the same person/number. This is the agreement (double-marking) discovery. The solver must recognize that the system is not simply "possessor has a genitive marker" + "possessed noun has an arbitrary suffix" but rather a concord system where both elements agree. To make this inference robust, include at least one minimal pair where the only difference is the possessor's person (e.g., 1SG possessor vs. 2SG possessor with the same possessed noun), so the solver can see both the genitive form and the possessive suffix change simultaneously.

4. **Introduce case marking on the possessed noun to establish morphological ordering.** Provide a set of items in which the possessed noun is case-marked (e.g., inessive "in," elative "out of," or adessive "on") AND carries a possessive suffix. The surface form will be: [Possessor.GEN] [Possessed+CASE+POSS.SUFFIX]. The solver must now segment the suffixal string into two morphemes and determine their order. The critical inference is that the case suffix precedes the possessive suffix (stem + case + possessive). To make this discoverable, the data must include both case-marked and uncase-marked forms of the same possessed noun with the same possessive suffix, so the solver can isolate the case suffix by comparison. For example, if the solver has already identified *-ni* as the 1SG possessive suffix from Step 2, then a form like *talossani* can be segmented as *talo* + *-ssa* + *-ni*, revealing that *-ssa* is the case suffix. Provide at least 2–3 different case markers to allow the solver to generalize the ordering rule. The solver should be able to predict the form of a novel case + possessive combination.

5. **(Optional, include if puzzle length allows) Introduce vowel harmony as a phonological constraint layer.** If included, the data must contain possessed nouns with both back-vowel stems (containing a, o, u) and front-vowel stems (containing ä, ö, y). The case suffixes should show clear alternation: back-vowel stems take back-vowel case suffixes (e.g., *-ssa*, *-sta*, *-lla*) and front-vowel stems take front-vowel case suffixes (e.g., *-ssä*, *-stä*, *-llä*). The possessive suffixes themselves are relatively stable across vowel classes (they do not alternate), which creates an important contrast: the solver must discover that harmony applies to case suffixes but not to possessive suffixes. This adds a phonological reasoning layer that is independent of the morphological/syntactic layer. The solver can use vowel harmony as a diagnostic to confirm correct segmentation: if a suffix in a complex form has a front vowel, the stem must contain a front vowel, which helps verify the morpheme boundary. Include at least 2 back-vowel stems and 2 front-vowel stems, each appearing in both case-marked and uncase-marked contexts.

6. **Design the vocabulary set with controlled properties.** Select 8–12 nouns from 2–3 semantic categories (e.g., body parts, household objects, natural features). Requirements: (a) at least 2 nouns with back-vowel stems and at least 2 with front-vowel stems (if vowel harmony is included); (b) at least 2 nouns that are semantically transparent and unlikely to be confused with grammatical morphemes; (c) avoid nouns whose stems end in a vowel that could be confused with a suffix vowel; (d) if plural marking is included, include at least one noun with a transparent plural and one with a less transparent plural. For possessor NPs, use a mix of proper names (for 3SG/3PL) and personal pronouns in genitive form (for 1SG, 2SG, 1PL, 2PL). The proper names should be short and phonologically distinct from any suffix.

7. **Construct the data set as a flat, ungrouped list of 14–20 items.** Do not group items by rule or provide section headings. The ordering should be designed so that simpler discoveries come first and more complex ones later, but the list should appear as a natural collection of sentences rather than a pedagogical sequence. A recommended ordering principle: items 1–4 should be simple full-NP possessives with 3rd-person possessors (establishing GenN order and the basic construction); items 5–9 should be suffix-only possessives covering the paradigm (establishing head marking and the paradigm); items 10–12 should be full-NP possessives with 1st/2nd person possessors (establishing agreement); items 13–16 should be case-marked possessed nouns with possessive suffixes (establishing morphological ordering); items 17–20 should be novel combinations that test generalization (e.g., a case-marked possessed noun with a 1PL possessor, or a front-vowel stem with a case suffix if harmony is included). The solver must induce all rules from the flat list simultaneously, but the ordering ensures that by the time they encounter complex items, the simpler rules are already inferable.

8. **Include targeted minimal pairs and controlled contrasts to prevent alternative analyses.** Specifically: (a) A pair where the only difference is the possessor's person (e.g., 1SG vs. 2SG) with the same possessed noun — isolates the possessive suffix paradigm and the genitive form. (b) A pair where the only difference is the possessed noun's vowel class (back vs. front) with the same case and possessive suffix — isolates vowel harmony (if included). (c) A pair where the only difference is the case on the possessed noun (e.g., inessive vs. adessive) with the same possessor and possessive suffix — isolates the case suffix and confirms the ordering rule. (d) A pair where the only difference is the presence or absence of the full possessor NP (suffix-only vs. full-NP construction) with the same possessor person — shows that the possessive suffix is the primary marker and the genitive NP is optional/redundant. (e) A pair where the possessed noun is singular vs. plural (if plural is included) with the same possessor — isolates the plural marker.

9. **Control for confounds and ambiguity.** (a) Ensure no possessor name or pronoun form is homophonous with any suffix. (b) Ensure the genitive suffix on the possessor is phonologically distinct from the possessive suffix on the possessed noun (in Finnish, the genitive is typically *-n* on names or a suppletive form on pronouns, while the possessive suffixes are *-ni, -si, -nsa, -mme, -nne, -n* — note that 3PL possessive *-n* and genitive *-n* are homophonous; handle this by ensuring 3PL possessive only appears in contexts where the possessor is clearly 3PL, e.g., a plural proper name or a 3PL pronoun). (c) Avoid using any noun that could be interpreted as a verb or adjective. (d) If vowel harmony is included, ensure that no stem contains both front and back vowels (which would create ambiguity in the harmony rule). (e) Ensure that the case suffixes are phonologically distinct from the possessive suffixes so the solver can segment them without ambiguity (in Finnish, this is generally the case: case suffixes are disyllabic like *-ssa, -sta, -lla* while possessive suffixes are monosyllabic like *-ni, -si*).

10. **Design the questions in two tiers with progressive difficulty.** Tier 1 (translation from target language to English, 4–5 items): Item 1 — a simple full-NP possessive with a 3SG possessor (tests GenN order + basic head marking). Item 2 — a suffix-only possessive with a 1PL possessor (tests paradigm knowledge). Item 3 — a full-NP possessive with a 2SG possessor where the possessed noun is case-marked (tests morphological ordering + agreement). Item 4 — a suffix-only possessive with a 3PL possessor (tests the less transparent 3PL form *-n*). Item 5 (if vowel harmony included) — a case-marked possessed noun with a front-vowel stem (tests harmony + ordering simultaneously). Tier 2 (translation from English to target language, 4–5 items): Item 6 — a full-NP possessive with a 1SG possessor and a case-marked possessed noun (tests genitive + ordering + agreement in a novel combination). Item 7 — a suffix-only possessive with a 2PL possessor and a case-marked possessed noun (tests paradigm + ordering). Item 8 — a full-NP possessive with a 3PL possessor and a case-marked possessed noun with a front-vowel stem (tests all systems simultaneously: 3PL genitive, 3PL possessive *-n*, case suffix with front-vowel harmony, GenN order). Item 9 (if included) — a construction that combines a plural possessed noun with a case suffix and a possessive suffix (tests the interaction of all three morphological markers). The final items should require the solver to combine 3–4 independently discovered rules in a single form, creating the puzzle's peak difficulty.

11. **Verify solvability and uniqueness of solution.** Solve the puzzle yourself using only the provided data. Confirm: (a) every rule needed to answer every question is uniquely determined by the data — no question requires a rule that is not evidenced; (b) the possessive paradigm is fully reconstructable from the data (all 6 forms appear or are inferable); (c) the morphological ordering rule is unambiguous (the solver cannot confuse case and possessive suffixes); (d) the agreement relationship is demonstrable (at least 2 possessor persons show matching genitive and possessive suffix); (e) if vowel harmony is included, the harmony rule is uniquely determined (the solver cannot propose an alternative conditioning); (f) the 3PL possessive *-n* is distinguishable from the genitive *-n* in all contexts where it appears (the possessor's number is always clear from context); (g) no question has more than one valid answer given the data. If any of these checks fail, revise the data set before finalizing.
