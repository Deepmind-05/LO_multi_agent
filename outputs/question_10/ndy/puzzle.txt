**Q3.**

(a) Basic word order is SVO: the subject precedes the verb, and the object follows the verb. There is no morphological case marking; grammatical relations are determined solely by linear position relative to the verb.

(b) Tense is marked by independent preverbal particles: *a* marks past, *bi* marks future, and the present/habitual is unmarked (zero). These particles appear between the subject and the verb.

(c) Negation is marked by the independent preverbal particle *no*, which appears after the tense particle (if present) and before the verb. The order is Subject + Tense + Negation + Verb.

(d) The definite article *de* precedes the noun it modifies. It marks a noun as definite (specific/known referent). Its absence indicates an indefinite noun.

(e) Relative clauses follow the head noun (postnominal). They use the gap strategy: the relativized element is simply absent from the relative clause. The gap's position (subject or object) is determined by the argument structure of the RC verb.

(f) Demonstratives (*di* = this, *da* = that) follow the noun (postnominal). They are single words with deictic meaning and do not contain a verb, distinguishing them from relative clauses.

## SOLUTION

**Rules inferred from data:**

**SVO word order (no case marking):** Sentences 1–4 establish that the first element is the subject (a pronoun) and the last element is the verb (intransitive). Sentences 5–9 extend this to transitive clauses: the element after the verb is the object. Sentence 6 (*fisi a si i*) is critical: the same noun *fisi* appears as subject (here) and as object (sentence 5) with no morphological difference, confirming that position alone determines grammatical role.

**Tense particles:** Sentences 1–3 show that *a* correlates with past meaning ("ran") and *bi* with future meaning ("will run"), while their absence gives present ("runs"). These are independent words (not affixes) because they appear between the subject and verb as separate tokens. Sentences 4, 7 confirm the pattern with different subjects and verbs.

**Negation:** Sentences 10–12 introduce *no*, which flips the truth value. Sentence 10 (*i no run*) shows *no* in the absence of tense. Sentences 11 (*i a no run*) and 12 (*den bi no bui fasi*) show that when both tense and negation are present, the order is Tense + Negation (a no, bi no). The solver distinguishes *no* from tense particles by its semantic function (truth-value reversal vs. temporal reference) and its distribution (it can appear without a tense particle, as in sentence 10).

**Definite article:** The contrast between sentences 5/13 (*fisi* vs. *de fisi*) and 7/15 (*fasi* vs. *de fasi*) shows that *de* marks definiteness. It precedes the noun and appears in both subject position (14) and object position (13, 15), confirming it is not a subject marker or tense particle.

**Postnominal relative clauses with gap:** Sentence 16 (*i a si de fisi den a bui*) shows a clause (*den a bui*) following a noun (*de fisi*). The verb *bui* is transitive (from sentence 7: *den bi bui fasi*), so it requires a subject and object. The RC has a subject (*den*) but no object — the missing object is the head noun *fisi*. This is the gap strategy. Sentence 17 (*de tanga i a si a run*) shows a RC with a subject (*i*) and a transitive verb (*si*) missing its object — the gap is the head noun *tanga*. Sentence 18 (*de tanga a run a si fisi*) shows a RC with an intransitive verb (*run*) and no subject — the gap is the subject, i.e., the head noun *tanga*.

**Postnominal demonstratives:** Sentences 19–20 show *di* and *da* following a noun. Unlike relative clauses, these are single words with no verb. The solver distinguishes them from RCs by the presence or absence of a verbal element in the postnominal material.

**How rules interact in the questions:**

- **Q1a** requires identifying the preverbal zone (*den a no* = subject + past + negation), the main verb (*si*), and the definite object (*de fisi*).
- **Q1b** requires recognizing the RC (*den a si*) modifying *de tanga*, identifying the gap (object of *si*), and parsing the main clause (*a singu*).
- **Q1c** requires parsing the preverbal zone in the RC (*i a no si* = subject + past + negation + verb), identifying the gap (object of *si*), and parsing the main clause with future tense (*a bi run*).
- **Q2c** requires producing a RC with a subject gap (omitting *de tanga* from the RC *a run*) and a definite object in the main clause.
- **Q2d** requires producing a RC with an object gap (omitting *de fasi* from the RC *i a si*) and a future main clause.

## DIFFICULTY

**Level 5.** The puzzle requires the solver to simultaneously manage multiple interacting systems: (1) parsing a complex preverbal zone with three distinct element types (subject, tense, negation) whose linear order must be inferred from distributional contrasts; (2) identifying postnominal relative clauses and determining the gap role through argument-structure reasoning; (3) distinguishing postnominal demonstratives from postnominal relative clauses; and (4) combining all of these in production tasks where the solver must generate correct gaps and preverbal sequences. The progressive layering of evidence (simple → complex) mirrors the increasing cognitive demands, and the hardest questions require applying four or more inferred rules in a single sentence.
