

TARGET-LANGUAGE: Supyire (Senufo, Niger-Congo, Côte d'Ivoire)

CONSTRUCTION RATIONALE

1. **Define the core grammatical parameters to be realized in the puzzle.** The puzzle must encode the following Supyire properties: (a) SOV basic constituent order with the verb in final position; (b) obligatory independent 3rd-person subject pronouns that are gendered, with no person or number marking on the verb itself; (c) a gender system with at least four (ideally five) non-sex-based classes, overtly marked by a definite affix on the noun and by the form of the 3rd-person pronoun; (d) an obligatory plural suffix on all nouns; (e) correlative relative clauses in which a gendered 3rd-person pronoun (the correlative) in a separate clause refers back to the head noun; (f) a small set of tense/aspect markers on or near the verb, including a perfective/imperfective contrast and a past/non-past distinction. These parameters interact: the gender system is the morphological backbone that links the definite affix, the subject pronoun, and the correlative element; the absence of verbal person marking makes word order and independent pronouns the sole evidence for argument structure; the correlative RC depends on the solver having identified the gender system.

2. **Choose a controlled vocabulary set.** Select content words that allow the solver to discover all target properties. You need: (a) 8–12 nouns distributed across at least 4 gender classes (2–3 nouns per class), chosen so that semantic or formal assignment patterns are at least partially discoverable (e.g., one class for animate beings, one for body parts, one for natural objects, one for abstract or cultural items — but do not make the assignment perfectly predictable; include at least one "surprise" noun whose class is not semantically obvious); (b) 2–3 intransitive verbs (e.g., 'come', 'sleep', 'fall'); (c) 2–3 transitive verbs (e.g., 'see', 'eat', 'build'); (d) 3rd-person singular pronouns, one form per gender class (these serve as the obligatory subject pronouns and as the correlative elements in relative clauses); (e) optionally, 1–2 postpositions (e.g., 'in', 'on') if you wish to include the postposition concept. Ensure all nouns are countable so that the plural suffix is always relevant. Ensure semantic roles (agent, patient) are unambiguous in intended translations.

3. **Design the gender system and its morphological exponents.** Define 4–5 gender classes. For each class, specify: (a) a distinct definite affix form (a suffix on the noun, e.g., -a, -i, -u, -e, -o — choose forms that are phonologically distinct from the plural suffix); (b) a distinct 3rd-person singular pronoun form (e.g., a set of short monosyllabic or disyllabic forms). The definite affix and the pronoun must be morphologically distinct from each other (the solver should not be able to confuse a definite affix with a pronoun or vice versa). The definite affix is the primary overt gender marker on the noun; the pronoun is the primary gender marker in subject position. Both must be consistent: a noun of class X always takes the class-X definite affix, and when that noun is the semantic subject, the class-X pronoun is used. This dual marking is the central discovery mechanism for the gender system.

4. **Design noun phrase morphology: plural suffix and affix order.** Define a single obligatory plural suffix (e.g., -n or -r) that appears on all nouns in the plural. Specify the morphological order within the noun phrase. A natural order for Supyire (given its weakly suffixing tendency) is: Noun Stem + Plural Suffix + Definite Affix (e.g., bird-PL-DEF). This means the definite affix is the outermost suffix, which is typologically natural and makes the definite affix the most visible element. The solver must discover both the plural suffix and the order. Ensure the plural suffix is phonologically distinct from all definite affix forms so the solver can segment them.

5. **Design the clause structure and argument expression.** Specify that: (a) the basic word order is SOV; (b) the subject is always expressed by an independent gendered 3rd-person pronoun in initial position; (c) the verb carries no person, number, or gender marking — it is "bare" with respect to arguments; (d) in transitive clauses, the object is a full NP (noun + plural? + definite) appearing between the subject pronoun and the verb; (e) in intransitive clauses, the structure is simply [Subject Pronoun] [Verb+TAM]. This means the solver's only evidence for identifying the subject is the initial pronoun, and the only evidence for identifying the object is its position (between pronoun and verb) and its NP morphology. The absence of verbal agreement is what makes word order critical and what makes the pronoun the anchor for subject identification.

6. **Design the tense/aspect system.** Define 3–4 TAM markers. A natural set for Supyire: (a) a perfective marker (e.g., a prefix or a preverbal particle); (b) an imperfective marker; (c) a past marker (which may combine with perfective or imperfective to give "past perfective" vs. "past imperfective"); (d) a zero form for non-past. You may include a remoteness distinction in the past (e.g., recent past vs. remote past) for additional complexity, but this is optional — the solver should be able to identify at minimum the perfective/imperfective contrast and the past/non-past distinction. Place TAM markers in a consistent position (preverbal or as a verbal prefix). Ensure TAM markers are morphologically distinct from the definite affix, the plural suffix, and the pronouns so the solver can segment them.

7. **Design the correlative relative clause structure.** Specify that relative clauses are correlative: the head noun appears in the main clause, and the relative clause is a separate clause that contains a correlative element — a gendered 3rd-person pronoun that agrees with the gender of the head noun. The RC is non-reduced (it has its own verb with TAM). The linear order should be: [Subject Pronoun] [Head NP] [Correlative Pronoun] [RC arguments] [RC Verb+TAM] [Main Verb+TAM]. The correlative pronoun occupies the argument position in the RC that corresponds to the head noun (e.g., if the head noun is the object of the RC verb, the correlative appears in object position within the RC). This structure means the solver must: (a) recognize that two verbs in a single sentence indicate a complex clause; (b) identify the correlative pronoun as the link between the head noun and the RC; (c) use the correlative's form to confirm the head noun's gender. The RC should be clearly distinguishable from a simple sequence of two clauses by the presence of the correlative pronoun and the fact that the head noun is "extracted" from the RC.

8. **[Optional] Design additional elements for increased complexity.** If you wish to add layers: (a) Postpositions: define 1–2 postpositions that follow the NP they modify (e.g., NP + 'in'). These appear in preverbal position, consistent with SOV. They provide additional evidence for the "modifier follows head" pattern. (b) Double negation: define a negative particle that can appear preverbally, postverbally, or both (with both being optional, not obligatory). Provide data showing that single negation and double negation are both grammatical with the same meaning. (c) Split predication: define a locational predicate (e.g., a verb meaning 'be at') that is distinct from nominal predication. This adds a clause-type dimension. Include only those optional elements that you believe will create meaningful inference without overwhelming the solver.

9. **Compose the data set — Layer 1: Simple intransitive sentences.** Provide 2–3 intransitive sentences with different subject pronouns (from different gender classes) and different TAM markers. Pattern: [Pronoun_X] [Verb+TAM]. These sentences establish: (a) the verb is in final position; (b) the initial element is a pronoun that functions as the subject; (c) the verb has TAM markers; (d) the pronoun varies (hinting at a classification system, though the solver cannot yet interpret the variation). The solver should be able to infer SOV order and the existence of TAM from this layer alone. The pronoun variation is a seed for the gender discovery that will be resolved in later layers.

10. **Compose the data set — Layer 2: Simple transitive sentences.** Provide 2–3 transitive sentences. Pattern: [Pronoun_X] [Noun+Def_Y] [Verb+TAM]. These sentences establish: (a) the word order is SOV (confirming Layer 1); (b) the NP between the pronoun and the verb is the object; (c) nouns have internal morphology (the solver now sees a suffix on the noun for the first time in a transitive context); (d) the definite affix on the object is visible. At this point, the solver can identify the basic clause structure but may not yet understand what the noun suffixes mean. The definite affix is now in the data but its function is not yet clear.

11. **Compose the data set — Layer 3: Gender discovery through varied nouns.** Provide 3–4 additional transitive sentences (or a mix of transitive and intransitive) that use different object nouns from different gender classes. The key is that the definite affix now varies across sentences in a way that correlates with the noun. The solver should be able to: (a) notice that the suffix on the noun is not constant; (b) group nouns by which suffix they take; (c) notice that the subject pronoun in Layer 1 also varied, and hypothesize that the pronoun variation and the definite affix variation mark the same property (gender/class). This is the central discovery of the puzzle. Ensure that at least 3–4 distinct definite affix forms appear, and that the corresponding pronoun forms from Layer 1 can be cross-referenced. The solver should be able to build a mapping: pronoun form ↔ definite affix form ↔ gender class.

12. **Compose the data set — Layer 4: Plural.** Provide 2–3 sentences with plural objects (and optionally plural subjects, though the subject is expressed by a pronoun, so plural subjects would require a plural pronoun or a different strategy — it is simpler to keep subjects singular and only vary the object number). Pattern: [Pronoun] [Noun+Pl+Def] [Verb+TAM]. These sentences allow the solver to: (a) identify the plural suffix (it is a new, consistent element that appears on some nouns but not others); (b) determine the morphological order (Pl before Def, or Def before Pl); (c) confirm that the definite affix is still present in the plural (it is not replaced by the plural suffix). The plural suffix should be phonologically distinct from all definite affixes so the solver can segment it.

13. **Compose the data set — Layer 5: Correlative relative clauses.** Provide 2–3 sentences containing relative clauses. Include at least: (a) a RC modifying the object of a transitive verb (e.g., "He saw the bird that the child ate"); (b) a RC modifying the subject (e.g., "The bird that the child ate fell" — here the head noun is the subject of the main clause, and the correlative in the RC agrees with the bird's gender). The pattern for (a) is: [Pronoun_subject] [Head_NP] [Correlative_Pronoun_head_gender] [RC_argument] [RC_Verb+TAM] [Main_Verb+TAM]. The solver should be able to: (a) recognize that two verbs indicate a complex structure; (b) identify the correlative pronoun as the element linking the head noun to the RC (it appears in an argument position within the RC and its form matches the head noun's gender); (c) confirm their gender assignments (the correlative's form should match the definite affix of the head noun). This layer depends on the solver having already identified the gender system (Layer 3) — the correlative's gender agreement is the test.

14. **Compose the data set — Layer 6: Complex sentences combining multiple features.** Provide 2–3 sentences that combine: (a) a transitive main clause with a gendered subject pronoun and a definite-marked object; (b) a relative clause modifying one of the arguments; (c) a specific TAM value; (d) optionally, a postpositional phrase or a negative particle. These are the most complex sentences in the data set and require the solver to apply all induced rules simultaneously. Ensure at least one sentence has a RC whose internal verb is transitive (creating a nested structure with two objects and two verbs). These sentences serve as the "integration test" — if the solver can parse them correctly, they have successfully induced all rules.

15. **[Optional] Compose additional data for optional elements.** If you included postpositions (Step 8a), add 1–2 sentences with a postpositional phrase in preverbal position. If you included double negation (Step 8b), add 2–3 sentences: one with single preverbal negation, one with single postverbal negation, and one with both — all with the same meaning, so the solver can infer that double negation is optional. If you included split predication (Step 8c), add 1–2 locational sentences that are structurally distinct from nominal sentences.

16. **Verify the data set for unique inducibility.** Check the following: (a) SOV order is unambiguous — the verb is always final, the pronoun is always initial, and there is no sentence where the order could be misread as SVO or OVS. (b) The gender system is discoverable — at least 3–4 distinct definite affix forms appear, and the corresponding pronoun forms from intransitive sentences can be matched to them. The solver should be able to build a consistent mapping without contradiction. (c) The plural suffix is distinguishable from the definite affix — no noun has a plural suffix that is identical to a definite affix form. (d) The correlative pronoun in RCs is distinguishable from the subject pronoun — they may be the same form (since both are 3rd-person gendered pronouns), but their positions differ (subject pronoun is clause-initial; correlative is in an argument position within the RC). Ensure the RC structure is clear enough that the solver can identify which pronoun is the subject and which is the correlative. (e) TAM markers are distinguishable from all other morphemes. (f) No rule can be inferred from a single sentence alone — each rule requires at least two data points. (g) No sentence is ambiguous in a way that would lead to an incorrect rule induction.

17. **Design Question 1 (Translation to English): Progressive difficulty.** Create 3 sub-questions of increasing complexity: (a) a simple intransitive or transitive sentence using only features from Layers 1–2 (tests basic SOV parsing and TAM identification); (b) a transitive sentence with a plural object and a specific gender class (tests noun phrase morphology: plural + definite affix); (c) a sentence with a correlative relative clause (tests RC parsing and gender agreement). The most complex sub-question should require the solver to identify the head noun, the correlative, the RC verb, and the main verb, and to correctly assign gender to the head noun.

18. **Design Question 2 (Translation to Supyire): Progressive difficulty.** Create 3–4 sub-questions: (a) a simple intransitive sentence with a specific TAM value (tests pronoun selection and TAM); (b) a transitive sentence with a plural object from a specific gender class (tests noun phrase construction: stem + plural + definite affix, and correct subject pronoun); (c) a sentence with a relative clause modifying the object (tests correlative RC construction: correct correlative form matching the head noun's gender, correct RC argument structure); (d) optionally, a sentence combining a RC with a postpositional phrase or negation. Each sub-question should test a different combination of rules not directly present as an identical sentence in the data set.

19. **Design Question 3 (Explanation/Description).** Require the solver to explicitly state the rules they have induced. This should include: (a) the basic word order and how arguments are identified; (b) the gender system (how many classes, what marks them, what the assignment patterns are); (c) the noun phrase structure (plural suffix, definite affix, their order); (d) the relative clause structure (how it is formed, what the correlative is, how it agrees); (e) the TAM system (what distinctions exist, how they are marked). This question confirms that the solver has correctly identified all rules rather than merely pattern-matching on surface forms.

20. **Final ambiguity check on questions.** For each question, verify that the intended answer is unique given the induced rules. Specific checks: (a) In translation-to-English questions, ensure the RC attachment is unambiguous (the correlative's position within the RC should make clear which argument it fills). (b) In translation-to-Supyire questions, ensure the gender of each noun is unambiguous given the data (if a noun's gender class was not explicitly shown in the data, either provide it in the question stem or accept multiple answers). (c) For the most complex question, if multiple readings are possible due to RC attachment ambiguity, either accept multiple answers explicitly or revise the sentence to eliminate the ambiguity. (d) Ensure that no question requires knowledge of Supyire beyond what is inferable from the data set.
