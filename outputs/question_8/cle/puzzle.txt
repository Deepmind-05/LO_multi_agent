

CONTEXT

Below are sentences from a language of southern Mexico, each followed by its English translation. Tone is marked in the orthography: acute accent (´) = high tone, grave accent (`) = low tone, no diacritic = mid tone.

DATA

1. a-kɔ́ tɔ a — I am drinking water
2. n-kʷa pa n — You are hitting the child
3. t-tɛ́ tɔ t — He/She drank water
4. a-pa kʷa a — I am seeing the dog
5. t-pan pa t — He/She saw the child
6. a-mɔ́ a — I am sleeping
7. t-lɛn t — He/She went
8. t-mɔ́ t — He/She is sleeping
9. t-mɔ̀ t — He/She slept
10. a-kɔ́ tɔ aw — We (incl.) are drinking water
11. a-tʃi kʷa aʔ — We (excl.) hit the dog
12. n-pa pa nw — You (pl.) are seeing the child
13. t-tɛ́ tɔ tw — They drank water
14. ma a-pa kʷa a — I am not seeing the dog
15. n-lɛ nw — You (pl.) are going
16. t-mɔ̀ tw — They slept

Vocabulary: *tɔ* = water; *kʷa* = dog; *pa* = child

QUESTIONS

**Q1.** Translate the following sentences into English. (Give one translation per sentence.)

(a) n-tɛ́ tɔ n

(b) t-kʷa pa tw

(c) a-lɛn aʔ

(d) ma t-pan kʷa t

**Q2.** Translate the following English sentences into the target language.

(a) You (pl.) are drinking water

(b) He/She hit the child

(c) We (incl.) are sleeping

(d) They saw the dog

(e) I am not going

**Q3.** (a) Identify which verbs in the data are **suppletive** (the root changes entirely for the perfective) and which are **regular** (the root is retained and a suffix is added). List each verb with its imperfective and perfective forms.

(b) State the generalization that distinguishes the two strategies.

(c) A new verb "run" has the imperfective form *lɔ́* and is suppletive, with perfective root *tɔ́*. What is the form of "I ran"?

**Q4.** Consider the sentence:

*a-tʃi pa aʔ*

(a) Provide the correct English translation.

(b) Explain why the verbal prefix alone is insufficient to determine the subject's person and number.

(c) Identify the specific cue that resolves the ambiguity and state what it tells you.

**Q5.** Describe the relationship between the verbal agreement system and the independent pronoun system with respect to first person plural. Why is the verbal system unable to distinguish all first-person-plural values, and which subsystem carries the full distinction?

**Q4.**
(a) We (excl.) hit the child.
(b) The prefix *a-* is used for both 1SG ("I") and 1PL ("we"), so it cannot distinguish between a singular and a plural subject.
(c) The independent pronoun *aʔ* in the final (subject) position is the 1PL exclusive form, which resolves the ambiguity by specifying that the subject is "we (excl.)" rather than "I."

**Q5.** The verbal prefix *a-* is identical for 1SG, 1PL inclusive, and 1PL exclusive; it does not encode number or the inclusive/exclusive distinction. The independent pronoun system, by contrast, distinguishes all three values: *a* (1SG), *aw* (1PL inclusive), and *aʔ* (1PL exclusive). Thus the pronominal subsystem carries the full person/number/inclusivity specification, while the verbal agreement system is underspecified for first person.

SOLUTION

**Inferred rules:**

1. **VOS word order.** The basic clause structure is Verb–Object–Subject. This is established by the consistent pattern that the first element is a verb (it bears a person prefix and changes form), the second NP is the object, and the final NP is the subject. Evidence: in every transitive sentence, the verbal prefix matches the final NP (e.g., sentence 1: *a-* matches *a* "I"; sentence 2: *n-* matches *n* "you").

2. **Subject-only verbal agreement.** The verb carries a prefix marking the subject's person/number. There is no object marking on the verb. Evidence: the prefix always corresponds to the subject NP, never the object NP.

3. **Syncretic subject markers.** The verbal prefixes are: *a-* (1SG/1PL), *n-* (2SG/2PL), *t-* (3SG/3PL). Each prefix is shared by a singular and a plural value. Evidence: sentence 1 (*a-* + *a* = 1SG) vs. sentence 10 (*a-* + *aw* = 1PL incl.) vs. sentence 11 (*a-* + *aʔ* = 1PL excl.) all use *a-*; similarly for *n-* (sentences 2, 12, 15) and *t-* (sentences 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 13, 16).

4. **Independent pronouns resolve syncretism.** The pronouns are: *a* (1SG), *n* (2SG), *t* (3SG), *aw* (1PL incl.), *aʔ* (1PL excl.), *nw* (2PL), *tw* (3PL). They distinguish singular from plural and inclusive from exclusive, resolving the ambiguity left by the verbal prefix.

5. **Aspect system: suppletive vs. regular.** Two aspects are attested: imperfective (ongoing/habitual) and perfective (completed). For suppletive verbs ("drink," "hit," "sleep"), the root changes entirely for the perfective. For regular verbs ("see," "go"), the suffix *-n* is added to the imperfective root. Evidence: "drink" *kɔ́* → *tɛ́* (different root); "see" *pa* → *pan* (same root + *-n*).

6. **Tone is morphologically relevant.** The verb "sleep" uses the same segments for both aspects but differs in tone: *mɔ́* (H, imperfective) vs. *mɔ̀* (L, perfective). Evidence: sentences 8 and 9 are identical except for tone and have different translations.

7. **Preverbal negation.** The negative particle *ma* precedes the verb. Evidence: sentence 14 (*ma a-pa kʷa a*).

**How the rules produce each answer:**

- **Q1(a):** *n-* = 2SG/2PL; final *n* = 2SG → subject is "you (sg.)"; *tɛ́* = perfective "drink"; *tɔ* = water (object). → "You drank water."
- **Q1(b):** *t-* = 3SG/3PL; final *tw* = 3PL → subject is "they"; *kʷa* = imperfective "hit"; *pa* = child (object). → "They are hitting the child."
- **Q1(c):** *a-* = 1SG/1PL; final *aʔ* = 1PL excl. → subject is "we (excl.)"; *lɛn* = perfective "go" (intransitive). → "We (excl.) went."
- **Q1(d):** *ma* = negative; *t-* = 3SG/3PL; final *t* = 3SG → subject is "he/she"; *pan* = perfective "see"; *kʷa* = dog (object). → "He/She did not see the dog."
- **Q2(a):** 2PL subject → *n-* prefix, *nw* pronoun; imperfective "drink" → *kɔ́*; object "water" → *tɔ*. VOS: *n-kɔ́ tɔ nw*.
- **Q2(b):** 3SG subject → *t-* prefix, *t* pronoun; perfective "hit" → *tʃi*; object "child" → *pa*. VOS: *t-tʃi pa t*.
- **Q2(c):** 1PL incl. subject → *a-* prefix, *aw* pronoun; imperfective "sleep" → *mɔ́*; intransitive (no object). VOS: *a-mɔ́ aw*.
- **Q2(d):** 3PL subject → *t-* prefix, *tw* pronoun; perfective "see" → *pan*; object "dog" → *kʷa*. VOS: *t-pan kʷa tw*.
- **Q2(e):** Negative → *ma*; 1SG subject → *a-* prefix, *a* pronoun; imperfective "go" → *lɛ*; intransitive. NegVOS: *ma a-lɛ a*.
- **Q3(a–b):** Direct comparison of verb forms across the data reveals the two strategies.
- **Q3(c):** "I ran" = 1SG subject (*a-* prefix, *a* pronoun) + perfective "run" (*tɔ́*, suppletive root) + intransitive. → *a-tɔ́ a*.
- **Q4:** *a-* is syncretic (1SG/1PL); *tʃi* = perfective "hit"; *pa* = child (object); *aʔ* = 1PL excl. (subject). The pronoun *aʔ* resolves the syncretism. → "We (excl.) hit the child."
- **Q5:** The verbal prefix *a-* covers 1SG, 1PL incl., and 1PL excl. without distinction. The pronouns *a*, *aw*, *aʔ* carry the full specification. The verbal system is underspecified; the pronominal system is the sole carrier of the inclusive/exclusive and number distinctions for first person.

DIFFICULTY

**Level 4.** The puzzle requires the solver to simultaneously discover VOS word order, subject-only agreement, a syncretic person/number system, a two-strategy aspect system (suppletive vs. regular), tone as a morphological feature, and an inclusive/exclusive asymmetry between two subsystems. The central "aha" moment (VOS + agreement) is followed by layered complications: syncretism forces reliance on independent pronouns, the aspect system requires cross-verb comparison to abstract a generalization, and tone adds a phonological dimension that solvers focused only on segmental content may initially overlook. Questions require productive assembly (Q2), metalinguistic generalization (Q3), and multi-cue ambiguity resolution (Q4–Q5), creating a clear difficulty gradient within a single coherent system.
