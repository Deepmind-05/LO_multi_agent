

TARGET-LANGUAGE: Otomí (Mezquital)

CONSTRUCTION RATIONALE

1. **Define the verb template and parameter inventory.** Establish the verb structure as a fixed concatenative sequence: TAM prefix → A (subject) prefix → P (object) prefix → root. Select a manageable set of values for each slot. For TAM, choose at least three values (e.g., past, present, future or past, progressive, habitual) with distinct, non-overlapping prefix forms. For A and P, include at least: 1sg, 2sg, 3sg, 1pl, 3pl. Critically, 3sg in both A and P positions is realized as zero (no prefix). All other person/number values must have distinct, non-empty prefixes. Ensure that no A prefix is identical to any P prefix (or if they share a segment, the full forms are distinguishable in context). Ensure that no prefix is a substring of any root in a way that would create accidental segmentation. This step establishes the generative grammar from which all data will be produced.

2. **Choose 10–14 verb roots.** Include a mix of transitive verbs (e.g., chop, steal, bury, hang, overtake, listen, love, bite, fill, tie) and intransitive verbs (e.g., go, grow, be sick). For intransitive verbs, the P slot is simply absent (no object prefix appears). Ensure roots are phonologically distinct from all prefixes. If incorporating the optional morpheme-boundary-ambiguity concept, select one transitive root whose initial segment is identical to (or a minimal variant of) one of the P prefixes, and plan to show this verb only with that particular P value in the data (see Step 5). If incorporating the optional irregular-verb concept, select one intransitive root that will alternate between two forms depending on person/number (e.g., root A with 3sg, root B with 1sg and 1pl), and ensure the alternation is visible in at least three data items.

3. **Design the data set as full sentences, not isolated verb forms.** Because VOS word order is a target-specific concept that the solver must discover, the data must present complete clauses in the form: Verb NP₁ NP₂ (where NP₁ is the object and NP₂ is the subject). Each sentence should include a clear English translation. The NPs should be simple pronouns or noun phrases with unambiguous person/number (e.g., "I", "you", "he", "we", "they", or "the man", "the women"). Generate 18–24 sentences with the following coverage requirements:
   - Each TAM value appears with at least 3 different roots.
   - Each A value (including zero/3sg) appears with at least 3 different roots.
   - Each P value (including zero/3sg) appears with at least 3 different roots.
   - At least 4 sentences have a 3sg subject (zero A marker) and at least 4 have a non-3sg subject (overt A marker), creating the contrast needed to infer zero = 3sg for A.
   - At least 4 sentences have a 3sg object (zero P marker) and at least 4 have a non-3sg object (overt P marker), creating the contrast needed to infer zero = 3sg for P.
   - At least 3 sentences have both A and P overtly marked (neither is 3sg), so the solver can see both person slots simultaneously and identify their relative order.
   - At least 2 intransitive sentences (no P slot) to confirm that intransitives simply omit the object prefix.
   - If the irregular verb is included, it appears in at least 3 person/number combinations showing the alternation.
   - If the ambiguous verb is included, it appears ONLY with the P value that creates the boundary ambiguity (never with a different P value that would resolve the ambiguity).
   - The VOS order is consistent across all sentences (no variation).

4. **Verify the data set for unintended ambiguities and sufficient contrast.** Check that: (a) no two different parses of any verb form yield the same English translation (except the deliberately ambiguous verb, if included); (b) the zero-marking of 3sg is unambiguous—no root begins with a segment that could be reanalyzed as a person prefix in a way that creates a false reading; (c) the A-before-P order is consistently recoverable from the data (i.e., in sentences where both are overt, the leftmost person prefix always corresponds to the subject NP and the rightmost to the object NP); (d) the irregular verb's alternation cannot be reanalyzed as two different regular verbs; (e) the TAM prefix is never confused with a person prefix (no TAM prefix is identical to or a substring of any person prefix in a way that creates ambiguity).

5. **Design Question 1 (Parsing: Target → English).** Provide 3–4 novel sentences (not in the data) that require the solver to decompose the verb and assign NP roles. Choose sentences that: (a) combine a familiar root with a TAM value and person/number combination not seen together in the data (testing template generalization); (b) include a 3sg subject or 3sg object (testing whether the solver correctly interprets zero as 3sg rather than "no information"); (c) if the irregular verb is included, use it in a person/number not attested in the data (testing generalization of the alternation); (d) if the ambiguous verb is included, use it with a P value different from the one seen in the data (testing whether the solver can resolve the boundary ambiguity using the new P prefix as a segmentation cue). Accept multiple valid translations where genuine ambiguity exists.

6. **Design Question 2 (Construction: English → Target).** Provide 5–6 English sentences requiring the solver to build full target-language sentences. Choose sentences that: (a) require a transitive verb with a 3sg object (testing zero-marking of P); (b) require a transitive verb with a 3sg subject (testing zero-marking of A); (c) require a transitive verb with both subject and object non-3sg (testing full A+P assembly in correct order); (d) require an intransitive verb with a TAM value not seen with that verb in the data (testing that intransitives omit the P slot); (e) require a transitive verb with a specific TAM value and a 3sg subject + non-3sg object (testing the interaction of zero A with overt P); (f) if the irregular verb is included, require it in a person/number not directly attested. The solver must produce the full sentence in VOS order with the correctly assembled verb. Accept morpheme-boundary notation (e.g., "ta: + kä") to acknowledge that the solver may not know exact phonological rules at boundaries.

7. **Design Question 3 (Metalinguistic: Identify the zero-marking pattern).** Ask the solver to explain what it means when a verb has no person prefix in the A position, and what it means when it has no person prefix in the P position. This tests whether the solver has correctly inferred that zero is specifically diagnostic of 3sg (not a general "unmarked" default or "no argument"). The solver should be able to articulate that the absence of a marker is informative and specifically indicates third-person singular. If the irregular verb is included, this question can also ask the solver to identify which verb deviates from the regular pattern and explain how.

8. **Design Question 4 (Word order: Identify the canonical order and justify it).** Ask the solver to state the canonical word order of the language and explain how they determined it from the data. The solver should identify VOS and explain that the verb morphology (specifically, the A marker corresponding to the second NP and the P marker corresponding to the first NP) is the evidence. This tests whether the solver integrated verb parsing with NP role assignment to discover the word order, rather than simply assuming SVO or SOV.

9. **Calibrate difficulty progression.** Ensure that the questions test different cognitive operations on the same underlying rules: Q1 requires pattern recognition and parsing (applying the template to novel combinations, handling zero-marking); Q2 requires productive construction (assembling the template from scratch, including word order); Q3 requires metalinguistic articulation (explaining the zero-marking pattern or identifying irregularity); Q4 requires integration and justification (connecting verb morphology to word order). Each question should be solvable only if the solver has correctly induced all core rules, but the questions should test different aspects of that knowledge. The difficulty should increase from Q1 to Q4: Q1 is the most mechanical (parse known elements), Q2 requires more assembly, Q3 requires explicit articulation of an implicit rule, and Q4 requires the solver to demonstrate they understand the interaction between morphology and syntax.

10. **Final validation.** Re-solve the entire problem from the solver's perspective using ONLY the provided data. Confirm that: (a) every rule needed to answer every question is uniquely inducible from the data (morpheme order, zero = 3sg, A-before-P, VOS order, intransitive omission of P); (b) no question has an unintended third or fourth answer; (c) the zero-3sg inference is forced by the contrast (the solver cannot reasonably interpret zero as "unspecified" or "default" given the data); (d) the VOS inference is forced (the solver cannot reasonably assign the first NP as subject given the verb morphology); (e) if the irregular verb is included, its alternation pattern is generalizable to the person/number tested in Q2; (f) if the ambiguous verb is included, the two parses are the ONLY two possible parses given the data, and the Q1 item with a different P value resolves the ambiguity in favor of one parse; (g) no prior knowledge of Otomí or Oto-Manguean languages is required at any step.
