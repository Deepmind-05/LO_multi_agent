SOLUTION

**Inferred rules:**

**1. Numeral system (base 5, zero-omitting positional notation):**

From Table 1, the solver identifies two classes of tokens:
- *Digits* (appear in multiple positional slots): tara, mala, yala, wala
- *Positional markers* (appear in fixed positions, always followed by a digit or end of expression): kuru, bana, dara

The values are determined as follows:
- kuru alone = 5 → kuru marks the ×5 position; a marker without a preceding digit encodes an implicit value of 1.
- tara kuru = 10 → tara × 5 = 10, so tara = 2.
- tara kuru yala = 13 → 2×5 + yala = 13, so yala = 3.
- wala kuru = 20 → wala × 5 = 20, so wala = 4.
- bana alone = 25 → bana marks the ×25 position (implicit 1).
- dara alone = 125 → dara marks the ×125 position (implicit 1).
- The base is 5 (since 5, 25, 125 = 5¹, 5², 5³).
- Entry 10 confirms mala = 1: wala(4)×125 + yala(3)×25 + mala(?)×5 + tara(2) = 582 → 500 + 75 + mala×5 + 2 = 582 → mala = 1.

Zero omission: In entry 9 (dara kuru = 130), the ×25 position has value 0 and is omitted from the surface form. Similarly, the units position (value 0) is omitted in entries 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, and 10.

The structure of a numeral expression is: [digit₁][marker₁][digit₂][marker₂]…[digitₙ], where the final bare digit (no marker following) is the units value. A marker without a preceding digit means 1 at that position.

**2. Word order (SOV):**

In every sentence, the verb is the final element. The subject precedes the object/oblique, which precedes the verb. This is consistent SOV order.

**3. Case marking (postpositional clitics):**

- Nominative (subject): zero (unmarked)
- Accusative (object): -wa
- Dative (oblique): -nga

Evidence: In sentence 1, wara-wa is the object (accusative). In sentence 7, tana-nga is the oblique (dative). The subject is always unmarked.

**4. Plural suffix:**

-ri marks plural on the noun stem. Evidence: gura-ri = "dogs" (sentences 2, 7, 8, 9, 10), wara-ri-wa = "fishes (acc)" (sentence 9), tana-ri-nga = "trees (dat)" (sentence 10), muru-ri = "birds" (sentence 11).

**5. Suffix order:**

The right edge of a noun has the fixed order: [stem] + [plural -ri] + [case clitic]. Evidence: wara-ri-wa (stem + plural + accusative) in sentence 9; tana-ri-nga (stem + plural + dative) in sentence 10.

**6. Adjective order (NAdj):**

Adjectives are separate words that follow the noun phrase (including its suffixes). Evidence: muru kura (sentence 4), gura nala (sentence 5), muru pala (sentence 6), muru-ri kura (sentence 11), wara-wa pala (sentence 12).

**7. Verb tense:**

-na marks past tense; zero marks present. Evidence: wana (present) vs. wana-na (past) in sentences 1 vs. 2; tura vs. tura-na in sentences 4 vs. 6; mura vs. mura-na in sentences 7 vs. 8.

**8. Negation:**

bura is a clause-initial negation word. It appears at the absolute beginning of the clause, before the subject. It is not adjacent to the verb. Evidence: sentences 3, 6, 8 all begin with bura, followed by the subject.

**9. Distinguishing nala (adjective "new") from yala (digit 3):**

nala appears as a single word directly after a noun (adjective position), in the same distribution as kura and pala. yala appears within multi-token numeral sequences alongside positional markers. The syntactic context disambiguates: in sentence 5 (gura nala wara-wa wana), nala is an adjective modifying gura; in Table 1 entry 3 (gura tara kuru yala), yala is the units digit in a numeral expression.
