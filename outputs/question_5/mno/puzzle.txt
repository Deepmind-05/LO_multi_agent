ANSWERS

**Q1.** The basic word order is SOV (Subject–Object–Verb). In sentence (1): *tɨp* = S, *ʔaw* = O, *ʔaʔ* = V.

**Q2.** *na* is a postposition meaning "in." It follows the noun *sɨn-m*, showing that Mono uses postpositions (the adposition follows its complement), consistent with a head-final adpositional system.

**Q3.** The head noun is *ʔaw* (dog) and the relative clause is *tɨp ʔaʔ* (man see). The relative clause follows the head noun, indicating an NRel (noun–relative clause) structure, consistent with head-finality.

**Q4.** The negative particle *ʔu* is in clause-initial position, before the subject. This differs from the typical SOV expectation of preverbal negation (immediately before the verb). Mono exhibits a NegSOV pattern rather than the more common SOVNeg or SONegV pattern.

**Q5.** In (15), the first constituent is the postpositional phrase *sɨn-m na* (house-OBL in). The question particle *ʔi* appears in second position, immediately after this first constituent. In (13) and (14), the first constituent is the subject, and *ʔi* again follows it. The general rule is: the question particle occupies second constituent position (after the first constituent of the clause), regardless of what that constituent is.

SOLUTION

**Rules inferred from the data:**

1. **SOV word order** (sentences 1–5): The verb is consistently final. The controlled contrast in (1) vs. (2) (same verb, swapped nouns) confirms that the first noun is the subject and the second is the object.

2. **Head-final adpositions** (sentences 6–7): Postpositions follow their nominal complement (*sɨn-m na*, *ʔal-m ʔe*), establishing a head-final adpositional system.

3. **NRel structure** (sentence 8): The relative clause follows the head noun (*ʔaw* + *tɨp ʔaʔ*), consistent with head-finality.

4. **NegSOV** (sentences 9–12): The negative particle *ʔu* is clause-initial, before the subject. This is confirmed across four sentences with different subjects (including a complex NP in (12)). The particle is not adjacent to the verb, ruling out preverbal negation.

5. **Second-position question particle** (sentences 13–16): The particle *ʔi* follows the first constituent of the clause. In (13)–(14) the first constituent is the subject; in (15)–(16) it is a fronted postpositional phrase. This demonstrates that *ʔi* is in second constituent position, not simply "after the subject."

6. **Suffixing nominal morphology with fixed order** (sentences 17–29): Nouns bear suffixes for PL (-k), OBL (-m), DEM (-ʔ), and DEF (-n) in the fixed order PL → OBL → DEM → DEF. This order is uniquely determined by the data: (21) shows PL < OBL < DEF, (22) shows OBL < DEM, (23) shows DEM < DEF, and (27) confirms the full sequence.

7. **Possessive prefix asymmetry** (sentences 12, 17, 28, 29): Possessive marking is prefixal (ʔa- = 1SG, ʔe- = 2SG), while all other nominal affixes are suffixal. The co-occurrence of prefix and suffix on the same stem (e.g., ʔa-ʔaw-k in (12)) confirms that they are distinct morphological processes on opposite sides of the stem.

8. **Verb tense/aspect suffixes** (sentences 30–34): Verbs take suffixes -s (PAST) and -t (FUT). These are identified by contrast with the unmarked (present) form in (1).

**How the rules interact:**

- The head-final pattern (SOV, postpositions, NRel) provides the baseline structural expectation. Negation violates this expectation by being clause-initial rather than preverbal, creating the puzzle's central tension.
- The question particle's second-position slot is independent of both the SOV order and the negation position, creating a three-way positional typology: clause-initial (NEG), second position (Q), clause-final (V).
- The possessive prefix is the sole exception to the otherwise suffixing nominal system, and its interaction with suffix stacking (prefix + stem + suffixes) is tested in the production questions.
- The production questions (Q10) require simultaneous application of word order, particle position, suffix order, possessive prefix placement, and verb tense marking.

DIFFICULTY

**Level 4.** The puzzle requires sustained inductive reasoning across multiple interacting domains. The solver must: (a) establish the head-final baseline from multiple domains; (b) override the typological expectation of preverbal negation in an SOV language (the central "counter-expectation" challenge); (c) distinguish second-position from "after the subject" for the question particle using fronted-constituent data; (d) triangulate a four-suffix ordering from combinatorial evidence; (e) identify the prefix–suffix asymmetry; and (f) integrate all rules simultaneously in the production questions. The difficulty arises from the interaction of concepts and the need to resist typological priors, not from any single opaque rule.
