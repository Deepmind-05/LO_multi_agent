Selected target-language-specific concepts (functioning as the core concept set):

1. Suffixing morphology for case and tense-aspect
   - Realization: Case markers and tense/aspect markers are suffixes on the relevant word (object for case, verb for tense/aspect). WALS 26A-2 (strongly suffixing), 51A-1 (case suffixes), 69A-2 (tense-aspect suffixes).
   - Intended solver inference: From multiple sentences, the solver identifies that grammatical markers attach to the right edge of the host word, distinguishing them from free particles.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

2. Dependent marking (object-marking / ergative alignment)
   - Realization: The object (P) receives a case suffix; the subject (A/S) is unmarked. WALS 23A-2, 25A-2 (consistently dependent-marking).
   - Intended solver inference: The solver identifies that the marked argument is the object, not the subject, by observing which argument bears the case suffix across transitive and intransitive clauses.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

3. SOV basic word order
   - Realization: Subject precedes object, object precedes verb. WALS 81A-1, 82A-1, 83A-1.
   - Intended solver inference: The solver identifies constituent order from the consistent positioning of the verb at the end and the object immediately before it.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

4. Inclusive/exclusive 2nd person plural distinction
   - Realization: Two distinct 2PL pronoun forms: inclusive (speaker + addressee) and exclusive (addressee + third party, excluding speaker). WALS 39B-2.
   - Intended solver inference: The solver identifies two distinct 2PL forms and determines which is inclusive vs. exclusive from contextual clues in the data (e.g., co-reference with 1SG in one form).
   - Priority: HIGH

5. Plurality marked by a separate word (not a suffix)
   - Realization: A free plural word is used rather than a plural suffix. WALS 33A-7.
   - Intended solver inference: The solver notices that plurality is NOT expressed by a suffix (unlike case and tense) but by an independent word, creating a contrast with the suffixing pattern.
   - Priority: HIGH

6. Preverbal negative particle (NegSOV)
   - Realization: A negative particle appears at the beginning of the clause, before the subject. WALS 144A-5 (NegSOV), 143E-1 (preverbal negative word), 144B-1 (beginning, not immediately preverbal).
   - Intended solver inference: The solver identifies the negative particle by its invariant position at clause-initial position and its contrast with affirmative sentences.
   - Priority: HIGH

7. Initial question particle
   - Realization: A question particle appears at the very beginning of the sentence. WALS 92A-1.
   - Intended solver inference: The solver identifies the question particle by its invariant initial position and its contrast with declarative sentences.
   - Priority: HIGH

8. Productive full and partial reduplication
   - Realization: Reduplication (full or partial) is a productive morphological process, likely marking aspect (e.g., iterative, distributive, or continuative). WALS 27A-1.
   - Intended solver inference: The solver identifies the reduplicated form and infers its grammatical function (aspectual) by comparing it to the non-reduplicated form in context.
   - Priority: HIGH

9. Optional (often omitted) subject pronouns
   - Realization: Subject pronouns may be dropped; the verb or context suffices. WALS 101A-5.
   - Intended solver inference: The solver must handle sentences where the subject is absent and infer the subject from context or from the verb's morphology.
   - Priority: HIGH

10. No possessive affixes; possessor is marked (dependent marking in possession)
    - Realization: The possessed noun does not receive a possessive suffix. Instead, the possessor is marked (likely by a case suffix or genitive marker). WALS 57A-4, 24A-2.
    - Intended solver inference: The solver identifies that possession is expressed by marking the possessor (not the possessed noun), consistent with the overall dependent-marking strategy.
    - Priority: HIGH

11. Interrogative-based indefinite pronouns
    - Realization: Indefinite pronouns (e.g., "someone," "something") are derived from or identical to interrogative forms. WALS 46A-1.
    - Intended solver inference: The solver notices that the same (or closely related) form is used in both questions and indefinite statements, and infers the derivational relationship.
    - Priority: OPTIONAL

12. Small vowel inventory (2–4 vowels)
    - Realization: Djapu has a reduced vowel system. WALS 2A-1.
    - Intended solver inference: The solver can identify the vowel inventory from the data and note its smallness.
    - Priority: OPTIONAL

13. No fricatives
    - Realization: The consonant inventory lacks fricatives. WALS 18A-3.
    - Intended solver inference: The solver identifies the absence of fricatives from the phonological data.
    - Priority: OPTIONAL

14. No second-person imperatives (morphologically)
    - Realization: Djapu lacks a dedicated morphological imperative for 2nd person. WALS 70A-5.
    - Intended solver inference: If imperative data is included, the solver notices the absence of a dedicated imperative form for 2nd person.
    - Priority: OPTIONAL

9. The puzzle must not require the solver to know that Djapu is Pama-Nyungan or Australian. All inferences must be derivable from the data alone using general linguistic knowledge.
