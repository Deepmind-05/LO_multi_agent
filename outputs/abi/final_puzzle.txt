*Notes on the data*  

* Possession can be marked either by a **prefix** or a **suffix** on the possessed noun (examples 5‑7).  
* For **kinship nouns** (mother, father, husband, granddaughter) the **suffix** position is obligatory; the prefix form is ungrammatical (example 10).  
* The **existential verb** *ka* bears a person‑agreement prefix (examples 1‑3, 9).  
* When a numeral larger than one modifies a noun, the noun must carry the **plural suffix –ra** (example 4).  
* Negation is expressed by the pre‑verbal particle *na* (example 3).  
* Polar questions are signalled only by a trailing “?” (example 8).  
* The locational construction *verb + noun + pa + possessor* expresses “X has Y” (examples 1‑4).  
* **Possessor affixes are only used when the possessor is a pronoun**; when the possessor is a lexical noun (e.g., *family*, *house*), the noun appears without an affix and the possessor follows the preposition *pa*.

**SOLUTION / RATIONALE**

1. **Possessor marking** – Alienable nouns (*key, book, house, etc.*) may take either a **prefix** (*na‑, ne‑, ti‑*) or a **suffix** (*‑na, ‑ne, ‑ti*) that indexes the pronoun possessor. Both give the same meaning, so two forms are possible for sentences 1, 2, 6, 7, 8, 9.  
2. **Kinship‑noun restriction** – For kinship nouns (*mother, father, husband, granddaughter*) only the **suffix** position is grammatical; the prefix form is ungrammatical. Consequently sentences 5, 10 use the suffix *‑ti* (her) on *ma‑ma* and *sri‑man* respectively.  
3. **Locational predicative possession** – “X has Y” is expressed by the existential verb *ka* with a person‑agreement prefix, followed by the possessed noun, the preposition *pa*, and the possessor noun or pronoun: **[person‑prefix‑ka] + [Noun] + pa + [Possessor]**. This pattern appears in all answers.  
4. **Negative particle** – The particle *na* precedes the verb to negate the possession: *na i‑ka* “does not have”, *na e‑ka* “you do not have”, etc.  
5. **Polar questions** – Identical word order to statements; a trailing “?” marks a polar question (example 3). No auxiliary or fronting is required.  
6. **Plural suffix** – When a numeral > 1 modifies a noun, the noun must bear the suffix *‑ra* (vowel‑harmonised). This is why *house‑ra*, *book‑ra*, *room‑ra*, *key‑ra* appear with *du*, *tri*, *dá*.  
7. **Numbers** – The puzzle supplies *pá* (one), *du* (two), *tri* (three), and *dá* (ten). The numeral is placed before the noun phrase (e.g., *du house‑ra*).  
8. **Mass nouns** – *cig* behaves as a mass noun: it never takes the plural suffix and can appear without a numeral.  
9. **Possessor affix limitation** – Possessor affixes are only used when the possessor is a pronoun. When the possessor is a lexical noun (e.g., *family*, *house*), the possessed noun appears without an affix and the possessor follows *pa*. This restriction explains the single‑form answers for questions 4 and 9.  

Applying these rules yields exactly the translations listed in the **ANSWERS** section.
