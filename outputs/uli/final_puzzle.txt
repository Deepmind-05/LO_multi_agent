**CONTEXT**  
Ulithian (Eastern Malayo‑Polynesian, Oceanic) expresses the meaning “X has Y” in two basic ways:

1. **Locative‑existential construction** – a locative preposition *i* “at/in” followed by the possessor, then the existential verb *eʔe* “exist/there is”, then the possessed noun.  
   *i <possessor> eʔe <noun>* → “X has Y”.  
   Negation is a preverbal particle *te* placed immediately before the verb: *i <possessor> te eʔe <noun>* → “X does not have Y”.  
   Yes‑no questions are formed by adding a “?” to a statement; word order does **not** change.

2. **Genitive‑noun construction** – a genitive particle *na* precedes the possessor, which is then followed directly by the possessed noun.  
   *na <possessor> <noun>* → “X has Y”.  
   This construction can be used with any noun, but kinship/inalienable nouns (e.g. mother, father, granddaughter) obligatorily use it.

Other grammatical facts needed for the puzzle  

* **Numerals** are followed by the obligatory classifier *‑ta* before a countable noun: *<num> ta <noun>* (e.g. *tolu ta buku* “three books”). A compound numeral such as *viti tasi* “twenty‑one” behaves as a single quantifier and is also followed by the classifier.  
* **Plural** can be marked on a noun with the suffix *‑i* (optional).  
* **Possessive suffixes** are used only with body‑part nouns. The suffix *‑gu* marks first‑person possessor, *‑mu* second‑person, *‑nu* third‑person: *lima‑gu* “my hand”. These nouns cannot appear in the two constructions above.  

All sentences below are given in a consistent **S V O** order; there are no articles or case affixes.

**QUESTIONS**  

1. How is negation expressed in the locative‑existential construction?  
2. Which type of nouns obligatorily use the genitive‑noun construction? Give two examples from the data.  
3. What is the Ulithian word order used to form a yes‑no question in the locative‑existential construction?  
4. Translate the Ulithian sentence “i na eʔe rua ta rumu.”  
5. Which nouns cannot appear in either of the two “has” constructions and instead use possessive suffixes? List the suffixes that mark first‑, second‑, and third‑person possessors.  
6. Provide the Ulithian translation for “You have three cars.”  

---  

**ANSWERS**  

1. The negation particle **te** is placed immediately before the existential verb *eʔe* (e.g., *i na te eʔe buku* “I do not have a book”).  
2. Kinship/inalienable nouns obligatorily use the genitive‑noun construction. Examples: **mama** “mother” (row 4) and **papa** “father” (row 5).  
3. A yes‑no question is formed simply by adding a question mark “?” at the end of the statement; the word order remains **S V O** (see rows 9‑11, 23‑24, 29).  
4. “i na eʔe rua ta rumu.” → **“I have two rooms.”** (row 25).  
5. Body‑part nouns (e.g., *lima* “hand”) cannot appear in the two main constructions; they use possessive suffixes. The suffixes are **‑gu** (1st person), **‑mu** (2nd person), **‑nu** (3rd person) (rows 18‑20).  
6. “You have three cars.” → **i ka eʔe tolu ta karu.** (pattern from rows 2, 13, 28).
