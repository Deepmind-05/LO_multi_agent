### TARGET CONSTRUCTION RATIONALE  

1. **Create the lexical inventory**  
   - Compile a short list of nouns: (a) countable objects (e.g., “knife”, “book”), (b) kinship terms (“mother”, “brother”), (c) body‑part nouns (“hand”), (d) a mass noun (“water”).  
   - Include pronouns, personal names, and a few common nouns for possessors.  
   - Add a set of numerals (1‑5, 10, 20) and one obligatory classifier (e.g., *‑ta* for objects).  
   *Purpose*: Provides the raw material for all later contrasts; ensures each semantic class needed for core and target‑specific concepts is present.  

2. **Establish the existential predicate and locative preposition**  
   - Invent a neutral verb meaning “exist/there is” (e.g., *eʔe*).  
   - Choose a preposition meaning “at/in” (e.g., *i*).  
   - Produce sentences of the form **PREP X V Y** (e.g., “i John eʔe book”).  
   *Controlled contrast*: Same verb with different prepositional phrases shows the locative possession pattern.  
   *Inference*: Learners see that the preposition + existential verb encodes possession.  

3. **Introduce the genitive particle construction**  
   - Define a genitive particle (e.g., *na*).  
   - Create sentences **GEN X Y** (e.g., “na John mother”).  
   - Use only kinship/inalienable nouns here.  
   *Contrast*: Same possessor and possessed noun appear in both locative and genitive forms, but only kinship nouns appear in the genitive pattern.  
   *Inference*: Identify the semantic restriction on the genitive construction.  

4. **Provide two dependent‑marking variants for the same possessor**  
   - For a set of ordinary nouns, give parallel sentences using (a) the locative construction and (b) the genitive construction (where allowed).  
   - Ensure at least three possessors (pronoun, name, common noun) appear in both variants.  
   *Purpose*: Demonstrates that both markings are semantically equivalent for “have”.  

5. **Add plural distinction**  
   - Create pairs: singular noun with existential verb, plural noun (marked by optional plural suffix *‑i* or by numeral 2) with the same verb.  
   - Keep the possessor constant to isolate the effect of number.  
   *Inference*: Learners detect how plurality is signalled (verb agreement or noun suffix).  

6. **Introduce numeral + obligatory classifier**  
   - Produce sentences with numerals followed by the classifier and a countable noun (e.g., “three‑CL knife”).  
   - Include both singular (numeral 1) and plural (> 1) contexts.  
   - Show that the classifier never appears without a numeral and never appears with the mass noun.  
   *Contrast*: Provide a sentence with a numeral but no classifier (ungrammatical) as a control in the data set (marked as “*”).  

7. **Insert multi‑word numeral expression**  
   - Use a compound numeral (e.g., “twenty‑one”) followed by the classifier and noun.  
   - Verify that the classifier follows the whole numeral phrase.  
   *Difficulty*: Forces learners to treat the numeral phrase as a single quantifier unit.  

8. **Create negative possession examples**  
   - Define a preverbal negative particle (e.g., *te*).  
   - Pair each affirmative locative sentence with a negative counterpart where *te* replaces the existential verb (or appears immediately before it).  
   - Keep possessor and possessed noun identical across the pair.  
   *Inference*: Identify the negative particle’s position and its function as “does not have”.  

9. **Add polar‑question pairs**  
   - Duplicate a subset of affirmative sentences, marking the duplicate with “?” but leaving word order unchanged.  
   - Ensure both locative and genitive constructions appear in question form.  
   *Inference*: Learners conclude that intonation alone signals a question.  

10. **Introduce the target‑specific possessive suffix**  
    - For body‑part nouns, create sentences where the possessed noun carries a suffix (e.g., *hand‑my*).  
    - Show that the same possessor cannot use the locative or genitive construction with these nouns (provide a negative control).  
    - Keep other nouns using only the two core constructions.  
    *Contrast*: Highlights a restricted morphological strategy.  

11. **Combine features in novel test items** (to be generated later)  
    - Mix plural nouns with classifiers, negative possession with numerals, genitive construction with a pronoun, etc., ensuring each test item draws on at least two previously demonstrated rules.  
    - For items that admit two valid answers (locative vs. genitive), include both variants in the data set.  

*Progression*: Steps 1‑4 establish the basic possession system; steps 5‑9 layer number, classifiers, negation, and questions; steps 10‑11 add the extra difficulty and prepare for combinatorial test items.

### VALIDATION CONSTRAINTS  

1. **CORE coverage** – All six core concepts (two possessor‑marking variants, locative predication, genitive‑noun order, negative word, intonation‑only questions, obligatory classifiers, number distinction) appear in multiple, contrastive examples.  
2. **Justified replacements** – No core concept is omitted; the genitive particle replaces the Nepali genitive suffix, which is attested in Ulithian (Genitive‑Noun order).  
3. **TARGET‑SPECIFIC relevance** – Possessive suffixes, multi‑word numerals, and optional plural markers are attested in Ulithian (57A‑2, 34A‑4) and are introduced only because they are naturally present and increase inferential load.  
4. **Evidence sufficiency** – Every rule is supported by at least three paired sentences (affirmative/negative, statement/question, singular/plural).  
5. **Contrastive design** – For each rule, a minimal pair isolates the variable (e.g., same possessor, different possessor‑marking; same noun, with vs. without classifier).  
6. **Internal consistency** – Word order (S V O, VOX for locative) is uniform across the dataset; no article or case affix appears, respecting 37A‑5, 38A‑5, 51A‑9.  
7. **Difficulty progression** – Early items involve a single construction; later items combine two or more constructions, culminating in items that require choosing between two valid variants.  
8. **No unsupported facts** – All morphological and syntactic properties are drawn from the WALS profile (e.g., obligatory classifiers, preverbal negative particle, lack of question particle).  
9. **Solvability** – The dataset provides all necessary evidence; no item relies on world knowledge or unstated grammatical facts.
