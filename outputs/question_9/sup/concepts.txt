

TARGET-LANGUAGE

Supyire (Senufo, Niger-Congo)

CORE

1. Noun class system
   - Target-language realization: 5+ non-sex-based noun classes with semantic and formal assignment (WALS 30A-5, 31A-3, 32A-3). Classes are identifiable through systematic phonological patterns in noun stems and agreement forms.
   - Intended solver inference: Identify the number of classes and their phonological signatures from the data; recognize that assignment is not purely semantic (some classes are determined by formal/phonological properties).
   - Priority: REQUIRED

2. Singular-plural distinction with obligatory plural suffix and stem alternations
   - Target-language realization: Plural marked by obligatory suffix on all nouns (WALS 33A-2, 34A-6). The suffix may be class-specific. Stem-internal phonological changes (vowel, tone) may accompany the suffix.
   - Intended solver inference: Identify the plural suffix (and whether it varies by class); identify any stem-internal alternations that co-occur with pluralization; distinguish suffixal marking from stem-internal change.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

3. Tone participating in class/number alternations
   - Target-language realization: Complex tone system (WALS 13A-3). Tone patterns on noun stems and/or agreement markers vary systematically across classes and/or number.
   - Intended solver inference: Identify tone as a phonological dimension of the class/number system; track tone patterns alongside segmental changes.
   - Priority: HIGH

4. Vowel quality alternations in class/number paradigms
   - Target-language realization: Large vowel inventory (7-14, WALS 2A-3) with contrastive nasal vowels (WALS 10A-1). Systematic vowel quality changes (including nasalization) in noun stems across class/number forms.
   - Intended solver inference: Identify systematic vowel alternation patterns; recognize that vowel changes are rule-governed, not arbitrary.
   - Priority: HIGH

5. Cross-paradigm correspondence (nominal agreement)
   - Target-language realization: Noun class information is encoded on multiple nominal elements: the noun itself (stem + suffix), demonstratives, adjectives, and 3rd person pronouns (which show gender distinctions in both singular and non-singular, WALS 44A-2). The verb does NOT show class agreement (WALS 29A-1, 102A-1).
   - Intended solver inference: Discover that class information is redundantly encoded across multiple word classes in a predictable, parallel way; use knowledge of one paradigm to predict forms in another.
   - Priority: REQUIRED

TARGET-SPECIFIC

1. Nasal vowel contrast as a phonological dimension of alternation
   - Phenomenon: Contrastive nasal vowels (WALS 10A-1) participate in class/number alternations alongside oral vowel quality changes.
   - Intended solver inference: Recognize nasalization as a distinct phonological dimension (not merely a "vowel change"); track it as an independent parameter alongside oral vowel quality and tone.
   - Interaction with CORE: Adds a third dimension to vowel alternations (oral quality × nasalization × tone), increasing the combinatorial complexity the solver must track.
   - Difficulty contribution: Medium. Solver must recognize that "a" and "ã" are distinct categories, not allophones, and that nasalization follows its own pattern.
   - Priority: HIGH

2. Class-specific plural suffixes
   - Phenomenon: The obligatory plural suffix (WALS 33A-2) may take different forms depending on noun class, creating a class-specific suffix paradigm parallel to the stem alternation paradigm.
   - Intended solver inference: Discover that the plural suffix is not uniform but varies by class; identify the class-specific suffix forms.
   - Interaction with CORE: Creates a second locus of class marking (suffix) that must be tracked alongside stem alternations; provides additional evidence for class identification.
   - Difficulty contribution: Medium. Solver must separate the suffix from the stem and identify class-specific suffix forms.
   - Priority: HIGH

3. Absence of verbal class/person agreement
   - Phenomenon: No verbal person/number marking of any kind (WALS 29A-1, 100A-1, 102A-1, 103A-1).
   - Intended solver inference: Recognize that the agreement system is purely nominal; do not expect verb forms to encode class information.
   - Interaction with CORE: Constrains the cross-paradigm correspondence to nominal elements only; prevents the solver from applying the Dinka verb-noun correspondence template.
   - Difficulty contribution: Low directly, but important as a design constraint that shapes the puzzle's structure.
   - Priority: HIGH (as design constraint)

4. Gender distinctions in 3rd person pronouns (singular and non-singular)
   - Phenomenon: 3rd person pronouns show class/gender distinctions in both singular and non-singular (WALS 44A-2).
   - Intended solver inference: Use pronoun forms as an additional paradigm for identifying class patterns; predict pronoun forms from noun class.
   - Interaction with CORE: Provides a compact, easily comparable paradigm that reinforces the cross-paradigm correspondence; pronouns are short and transparent, making them good "test cases" for the solver.
   - Difficulty contribution: Low-Medium. Pronouns are simple enough to be accessible but still require class identification.
   - Priority: HIGH

INTERACTIONS

1. Noun class × nasal vowels × tone × vowel quality: Multiple phonological dimensions create a multi-parameter alternation system. The solver must track at least three independent dimensions (oral vowel quality, nasalization, tone) simultaneously across the class/number paradigm. This is the primary source of difficulty.

2. Noun class × cross-paradigm agreement: The same class information is encoded in the noun stem, the plural suffix, demonstratives/adjectives, and pronouns. The solver must discover this redundancy and use it for prediction. This is the primary source of the puzzle's "insight" (analogous to the Dinka verb-noun correspondence).

3. Plural suffix × stem alternations: The solver must decompose the plural form into (a) stem-internal changes and (b) the added suffix. If the suffix is class-specific, this creates an additional layer: the solver must identify both the stem alternation rule AND the class-specific suffix.

4. Nasal vowels × vowel quality: Nasalization and oral vowel quality are independent dimensions. A vowel may change in quality AND gain/lose nasalization in the same alternation. The solver must not conflate these.

5. Pronoun paradigm × noun paradigm: Pronouns provide a "clean" view of the class system (short, transparent forms) that can be cross-referenced with the more complex noun paradigms. This gives the solver a verification tool.

CONSTRAINTS

1. The puzzle must NOT include any verbal agreement with noun class or person. All agreement evidence must come from nominal elements (nouns, demonstratives, adjectives, pronouns).

2. The cross-paradigm correspondence must be between nominal elements only. The solver should be able to predict agreement forms on one nominal element from the form of another.

3. Plural marking must be presented as obligatory for all nouns (no "invariant" nouns). This ensures sufficient data for pattern discovery.

4. Tone must be represented in a consistent, unambiguous notation (e.g., diacritics: á, à, or a tone letter system). The solver must be able to distinguish tone from segmental identity.

5. Nasal vowels must be clearly distinguished from oral vowels in the notation (e.g., using tilde: ã, ẽ, etc.). The solver must be able to identify nasalization as a separate feature.

6. The number of noun classes should be discoverable from the data but not stated explicitly. The solver should be able to count the distinct class patterns.

7. The puzzle should provide enough exemplars per class to allow the solver to identify patterns with confidence (at least 2-3 nouns per class, each shown in singular and plural, with at least one agreement form).

8. Vowel alternations should be systematic and rule-governed (not suppletive), so that the solver can identify the underlying pattern and apply it to novel forms.

9. The puzzle should include at least one "test" item where the solver must predict an unseen form using the discovered rules, to verify that the cross-paradigm correspondence has been correctly identified.
