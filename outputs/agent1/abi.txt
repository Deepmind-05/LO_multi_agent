**TARGET-LANGUAGE ANALYSIS**  
- **Evidence from WALS / typological profile**  
  1. **No morphological case** (features 21A‑5, 28A‑1, 49A‑1, 50A‑1, 51A‑9).  
  2. **Possessive affixation**: both prefixes and suffixes are used on nouns (57A‑3).  
  3. **Plural marking** is a suffix on nouns (33A‑2).  
  4. **Negative particle** is attested (112A‑2) and can appear preverbal (143E‑1).  
  5. **Word order** is SVO with VOX pattern and prepositions (81A‑2, 84A‑1, 85A‑2).  
  6. **Genitive‑noun order** shows no dominant pattern; both Gen‑N and N‑Gen occur (86A‑3).  
  7. **No evidence for numeral classifiers** or a dedicated lexical “have” verb.  
  8. **No evidential, tense‑aspect, or perfect morphology** (65A‑2, 66A‑4, 68A‑4).  

- **Inferences for puzzle design**  
  * Possession can be expressed by a **possessor‑affix on the possessed noun** (dependent marking).  
  * Because both prefixes and suffixes exist, we can create **two morphologically distinct dependent‑marking variants** that are semantically equivalent.  
  * A **genitive construction** can be illustrated with the possessor preceding the noun (Gen‑N) and contrasted with the affixal strategy.  
  * Negation will be realized by the preverbal negative particle; its category (particle vs. verb) will be left ambiguous.  
  * Polar questions can be shown to differ from statements only by punctuation (intonation‑only), since the language lacks a dedicated interrogative particle in the profile.  
  * Numeral expressions will **not** involve classifiers; instead, number will be marked by the plural suffix on the noun, providing a target‑specific difficulty.  
  * The language’s lack of case means that any possessor‑genitive contrast must rely on word‑order or affixation, not case marking.

**TARGET CONSTRUCTION RATIONALE**  

1. **Define the lexical inventory**  
   - 8–10 core nouns: 4 countable objects (e.g., *dog*, *key*, *book*, *coin*), 2 kinship nouns (*mother*, *brother*), 1 mass noun (*water*), 1 relational noun (*room*).  
   - 4 pronouns (1st sg, 2nd sg, 3rd sg, 3rd pl).  
   - Numerals 1–3.  
   - Possessive affixes: prefix *na‑* and suffix *‑ri*.  
   - Genitive marker *‑ka*.  
   - Negative particle *ma*.  
   - Existential verb *exist* (optional).  
   *Purpose*: Provides a bounded set of forms for systematic paradigm building.  

2. **Create a baseline affirmative possession paradigm using the prefixal dependent‑marking**  
   - Sentences of the shape **[Possessor] na‑[Noun]** (e.g., “I na‑dog”).  
   - Vary possessor type (pronoun, proper name, common noun).  
   - Include singular and plural possessed nouns (plural suffix on noun).  
   *Contrast*: Shows that the possessor is encoded on the noun, not on the possessor.  

3. **Introduce the suffixal dependent‑marking variant**  
   - Parallel sentences **[Possessor] [Noun]‑ri** (e.g., “I dog‑ri”).  
   - Keep all other elements identical to step 2.  
   *Inference*: Learners see two morphologically distinct but semantically equivalent constructions.  

4. **Add the genitive‑noun construction for inalienable/relational nouns**  
   - Sentences **[Possessor] ‑ka [Noun]** (e.g., “my ‑ka mother”).  
   - Use only kinship/relational nouns to avoid overlap with affixal strategy.  
   - Provide both singular and plural where applicable.  
   *Contrast*: Demonstrates a different syntactic strategy that co‑exists with the affixal one.  

5. **Encode plurality on the possessed noun**  
   - Attach plural suffix *‑kʉ* to count nouns when plural (e.g., “dog‑kʉ”).  
   - Show that the possessor affix (prefix or suffix) remains unchanged.  
   *Inference*: Learners deduce that number is marked on the noun, not on the possessor.  

6. **Introduce the negative particle**  
   - Replace the affirmative existential element (or the verb *exist* if used) with *ma* in otherwise identical sentences (both prefix and suffix variants).  
   - Provide paired affirmative/negative examples for each possessor and noun.  
   *Contrast*: Highlights that *ma* functions as a negation marker, its category left ambiguous.  

7. **Create polar‑question counterparts**  
   - Duplicate every affirmative (and negative) sentence, copy the word order exactly, and append “?” instead of “.”.  
   - No additional morpheme is added.  
   *Inference*: Learners infer that intonation (punctuation) alone signals a question.  

8. **Add numeral expressions**  
   - Combine numerals with count nouns (e.g., “three dog‑kʉ”).  
   - Show that the plural suffix may co‑occur with the numeral, and that the same possessor affix applies.  
   *Contrast*: Reinforces that classifiers are not required; number is expressed via numeral + optional plural suffix.  

9. **Insert mass‑noun examples**  
   - Sentences like “I na‑water” (prefix) and “I water‑ri” (suffix) without plural suffix.  
   - Provide negative and question versions.  
   *Purpose*: Confirms that the plural suffix is absent for mass nouns.  

10. **Design translation questions**  
    - Combine the building blocks in novel ways:  
      a. Suffixal possession + plural noun + numeral + negative + question.  
      b. Genitive construction with kinship noun + pronoun possessor.  
      c. Prefixal possession of a mass noun in a polar question.  
    - Include at least two items where both prefix and suffix variants are acceptable, explicitly instructing that both answers are valid.  
    *Difficulty progression*: Starts with single‑affix sentences, then adds negation, number, mass nouns, genitive contrast, and finally multi‑feature combinations.  

**Dependencies**: Steps 2–3 establish the core affixal system; step 4 introduces the alternative genitive system; steps 5–9 layer number, negation, questions, and mass‑noun constraints; step 10 leverages all prior contrasts for the test items.

**VALIDATION CONSTRAINTS**  

1. **CORE coverage** – All five core concepts (C1‑C5) appear in multiple, contrastive examples.  
2. **Justified replacements** – The locational predicative possession from the source is omitted (no evidence in Abipón) and replaced by affixal possession; the classifier requirement is dropped (no evidence) and replaced by plural suffix marking.  
3. **TARGET‑SPECIFIC utility** – Each target‑specific phenomenon (T1‑T4) adds a non‑trivial inference step and interacts with at least one core concept.  
4. **Evidence sufficiency** – Every rule is supported by at least two paired sentences differing only in the targeted morpheme or construction.  
5. **Contrastive design** – Positive vs. negative, singular vs. plural, prefix vs. suffix, affix vs. genitive, statement vs. question are all systematically contrasted.  
6. **Internal consistency** – Morphological inventories (affixes, plural suffix, negative particle) are used consistently across all sentences; no form appears with conflicting functions.  
7. **No unsupported facts** – All claimed properties (absence of case, presence of both possessive prefixes and suffixes, negative particle, SVO order, plural suffix) are directly attested in the WALS profile.  
8. **Solvability** – The final translation set can be answered by mechanically applying the inferred rules; no world knowledge or unstated grammar is required.  
9. **Difficulty progression** – The construction sequence ensures learners first master a single rule, then combine two, then three, culminating in items that require simultaneous use of all core and target‑specific rules.  

*End of plan – the next generation agent should instantiate the lexical items and produce the concrete sentence set according to the steps above.*
