**TARGET-LANGUAGE**  
Occitan (ISO 639‑3 occ)

---

### TARGET-LANGUAGE ANALYSIS  

| Phenomenon | Evidence from WALS / linguistic literature | Inference / Design relevance |
|------------|--------------------------------------------|------------------------------|
| **Possession expressed with a lexical verb** – the language has the verb *aver* “to have”. (WALS 37A “Possession” reports that Romance languages use a verb.) | Confirmed by descriptive grammars of Occitan. | Provides a **verb‑based possession** pattern to replace the source’s locational construction. |
| **Dependent‑marking possessor variants** – (i) clitic pronoun attached to *aver* (e.g., *l’ai* “I have it”), (ii) prepositional phrase *a + NP* (e.g., *ai lo libre a Joan* “I have the book at Joan”). Both are attested in Romance languages (WALS 24A “Possessor marking”). | Documented in Occitan syntax (clitic pronouns for objects, dative *a* for indirect possessor). | Supplies two distinct, semantically equivalent possessor markings for the core puzzle. |
| **Genitive‑Noun order** – the possessor can precede the possessed noun with the preposition *de* (genitive). (WALS 86A “Genitive noun order” = Genitive‑Noun for Romance.) | Standard in Occitan (e.g., *lo libre de Joan* “John’s book”). | Directly maps the source’s Genitive‑Noun core concept. |
| **Negative particle** – *pas* follows the verb (e.g., *ai pas* “I do not have”). (WALS 112A “Negative morphemes” – particle). | Well‑attested in Occitan. | Mirrors the source’s “negative word of unclear category”. |
| **Polar questions by intonation only** – questions are marked solely by rising intonation; no obligatory interrogative particle is required (WALS 116A “Polar questions”). | Described for many Romance varieties, including Occitan. | Keeps the source’s intonation‑only question feature. |
| **Obligatory gender/number agreement of numerals** – cardinal numerals agree in gender (and sometimes number) with the noun they modify (e.g., *un libre* vs *una carta*). (WALS 55A “Numeral classifiers” – not present, but the related agreement is a robust Romance trait.) | Confirmed by Occitan grammars. | Serves as a **target‑specific** replacement for obligatory numeral classifiers, providing a comparable inferential step. |
| **Mass nouns** – nouns that lack a plural form and are used without a numeral (e.g., *aiga* “water”). | Standard lexical property. | Allows a contrast between countable and non‑count nouns, similar to the source’s mass‑noun requirement. |
| **Plural agreement on the verb** – *aver* shows number agreement with the subject (e.g., *avèm* “we have”). | Typical Romance verbal morphology. | Enables a plural‑possession contrast. |

No evidence suggests that Occitan employs obligatory numeral classifiers; therefore that source feature must be replaced.

### TARGET CONSTRUCTION RATIONALE  

1. **Create a minimal lexical inventory**  
   - **Purpose:** Provide nouns (countable, mass, kinship), pronouns, numerals (1–3), and the verb *aver* plus particle *pas*.  
   - **Data:** List of 12 nouns (e.g., *libre*, *carta*, *clau*, *aiga*, *maire*, *pare*, *casa*, *dinhs* “money”).  
   - **Contrast:** Count vs. mass; kinship vs. artefact.  
   - **Inference:** Learner knows which nouns can take numerals and which cannot.  

2. **Demonstrate basic verb‑based possession**  
   - **Purpose:** Establish the core “X has Y” pattern with *aver*.  
   - **Data:** Sentences like *Ai lo libre* “I have the book”.  
   - **Contrast:** Different subjects (pronoun, proper name, common noun).  
   - **Inference:** Possession expressed by *aver* + possessed noun.  

3. **Introduce two dependent‑marking possessor variants**  
   - **Purpose:** Show that the possessor can be a clitic or a prepositional phrase.  
   - **Data:** (i) *L’ai lo libre* “I have the book” (clitic), (ii) *Ai lo libre a Joan* “I have the book at Joan”.  
   - **Contrast:** Same meaning, different morphology.  
   - **Inference:** Both forms are equivalent for possession.  

4. **Add plural subject agreement**  
   - **Purpose:** Make number on the verb observable.  
   - **Data:** *Avèm los libres* “We have the books”.  
   - **Contrast:** Singular *ai* vs. plural *avèm*.  
   - **Inference:** Verb form signals possessor number.  

5. **Present the genitive‑noun construction with *de***  
   - **Purpose:** Provide the alternative “X’s Y” pattern.  
   - **Data:** *Lo libre de Joan* “Joan’s book”.  
   - **Contrast:** Same possessed noun with genitive vs. verb possession.  
   - **Inference:** *de* marks a genitive relationship; possessor precedes noun.  

6. **Restrict the genitive to kinship/inalienable nouns**  
   - **Purpose:** Create a semantic rule for when *de* is obligatory.  
   - **Data:** *Ma maire* (no *de*) vs *la maire de Joan* (genitive).  
   - **Contrast:** Kinship noun with and without *de*.  
   - **Inference:** Kinship nouns prefer the *de* construction; other nouns use verb possession.  

7. **Introduce negation with *pas***  
   - **Purpose:** Show the negative particle’s distribution.  
   - **Data:** *Ai pas lo libre* “I do not have the book”.  
   - **Contrast:** Positive vs. negative with identical lexical items.  
   - **Inference:** *pas* negates the possession predicate.  

8. **Demonstrate polar questions via intonation**  
   - **Purpose:** Indicate that adding “?” converts a statement to a question without lexical change.  
   - **Data:** *Ai lo libre?* “Do I have the book?” (same word order as statement).  
   - **Contrast:** Statement vs. question form.  
   - **Inference:** Question meaning is signaled solely by intonation/“?”.  

9. **Show numeral‑noun agreement (target‑specific)**  
   - **Purpose:** Establish obligatory gender agreement of numerals.  
   - **Data:** *Un libre* (masc), *Una carta* (fem), *Dos libres*, *Daus cartas*.  
   - **Contrast:** Masculine vs. feminine forms, singular vs. plural.  
   - **Inference:** Numeral must match noun gender (and number where applicable).  

10. **Combine numerals with possession**  
    - **Purpose:** Merge numeral agreement with the possession verb.  
    - **Data:** *Ai un libre* “I have a book”, *Ai dos libres* “I have two books”.  
    - **Contrast:** Singular vs. plural possessed noun, numeral presence vs. absence.  
    - **Inference:** Numeral‑classifier (here gender agreement) appears between verb and noun; verb does not change.  

11. **Integrate negation with numeral possession**  
    - **Purpose:** Test interaction of negative particle and numeral agreement.  
    - **Data:** *Ai pas dos libres* “I do not have two books”.  
    - **Contrast:** Positive vs. negative with numeral phrase.  
    - **Inference:** *pas* can co‑occur with numeral‑agreement structures.  

12. **Create polar questions for numeral possession**  
    - **Purpose:** Verify that intonation‑only questions work with numerals.  
    - **Data:** *Ai dos libres?* “Do I have two books?”  
    - **Contrast:** Statement vs. question with numeral.  
    - **Inference:** Question formation is independent of numeral presence.  

13. **Provide examples where both possessor‑marking variants are valid**  
    - **Purpose:** Ensure learners see the two‑variant equivalence in varied contexts (pronoun, proper name, common noun).  
    - **Data:** *L’ai lo libre*, *Ai lo libre a Joan*, *L’ai lo libre a la casa* etc.  
    - **Contrast:** Same meaning, different morphology.  
    - **Inference:** Both variants are permissible; answer key must accept both.  

14. **Supply contrastive examples where only the genitive construction is grammatical**  
    - **Purpose:** Reinforce the semantic restriction on *de*.  
    - **Data:** *La maire de Joan* (acceptable) vs **La maire Joan* (ungrammatical).  
    - **Contrast:** Presence vs. absence of *de* with kinship noun.  
    - **Inference:** Learner must select the genitive form for kinship nouns.  

15. **Assemble a final interlocking paradigm**  
    - **Purpose:** Ensure every rule is supported by multiple instances and that the same lexical items recur across contexts.  
    - **Data:** A matrix of sentences covering all combinations of: possessor type (clitic vs *a NP*), noun type (count vs mass vs kinship), number (singular vs plural), polarity (positive vs negative), interrogative (statement vs question), numeral presence (none vs 1‑3).  
    - **Contrast:** Systematic variation provides the necessary inferential evidence.  
    - **Inference:** Learners can generalize each rule and apply it to novel combinations in the test items.
