

CONTEXT

The sentences below are in Lezgian, a language spoken in the Caucasus region. The language has no gender system and does not mark person or number on the verb. Each sentence is followed by its English translation.

DATA

**Vocabulary**

| Lezgian | English |
|---------|---------|
| čul | child |
| xan | dog |
| war | house |
| dər | water |
| k'ar | man |
| t'ar | tree |

**Sentences**

(1) čul war-d.
    The child ran.

(2) xan war-d.
    The dog ran.

(3) k'ar-l čul k'ar-d.
    The man saw the child.

(4) čul-l xan t'ar-d.
    The child hit the dog.

(5) xan dər-d.
    The dog fell.

(6) k'ar war-r.
    The man runs.

(7) čul-l xan-d war-a.
    The child sat at the house.

(8) xan-l war-a xan-d.
    The dog sat at the house.

(9) k'ar-l dər-a dər-d.
    The man fell at the water.

(10) čul war-r-ma.
    The child doesn't run.

(11) k'ar-l čul k'ar-d-ma.
    The man did not see the child.

(12) xan-l war-a xan-d-ma.
    The dog did not sit at the house.

QUESTIONS

**Part A: Analysis**

A1. List the sentence numbers in which the first noun bears the suffix *-l*. What property do these sentences share that the remaining sentences lack?

A2. What is the function of the suffixes *-d* and *-r* on the verb? Cite specific sentences as evidence.

A3. What is the function of the suffix *-ma* on the verb? Cite specific sentences as evidence.

**Part B: Translation**

Translate each English sentence into Lezgian. Use the perfective or imperfective aspect as indicated.

B1. The dog saw the man. *(perfective)*

B2. The man fell. *(perfective)*

B3. The child sat at the tree. *(perfective)*

B4. The dog did not run. *(imperfective)*

B5. The man did not see the dog at the house. *(perfective)*

**Part C: Explanation**

C1. In sentence (7), the verb *xan-* is intransitive (it takes no object). Explain why the subject *čul* still bears the suffix *-l*.

C2. In sentence (3), the noun *čul* does not bear the suffix *-l*. Explain why.

ANSWERS

A1. Sentences (3), (4), (7), (8), (9), (11), (12). These sentences all contain more than one argument (either a patient noun or a locative phrase). The remaining sentences (1), (2), (5), (6), (10) contain only a single argument (the subject alone).

A2. *-d* marks perfective aspect (a completed event); *-r* marks imperfective aspect (an ongoing or habitual event). Evidence: sentence (1) *čul war-d* ("The child ran") and sentence (6) *k'ar war-r* ("The man runs") use the same verb stem *war-* with different aspect markers, corresponding to the perfective/imperfective contrast in the translations.

A3. *-ma* marks negation. Evidence: sentence (6) *k'ar war-r* ("The man runs") and sentence (10) *čul war-r-ma* ("The child doesn't run") differ only by the addition of *-ma*, which corresponds to the negation in the translation. Similarly, sentence (1) *čul war-d* ("The child ran") and sentence (11) *k'ar-l čul k'ar-d-ma* ("The man did not see the child") show *-ma* following the aspect suffix *-d*.

B1. xan-l k'ar k'ar-d.

B2. k'ar dər-d.

B3. čul-l xan-d t'ar-a.

B4. xan war-r-ma.

B5. k'ar-l xan war-a k'ar-d-ma.

C1. The suffix *-l* does not mark "the agent of a transitive verb." Its rule is: *-l* marks the subject of a clause when that clause contains another argument (a patient or a locative/oblique). In sentence (7), although *xan-* (sit) is intransitive, the clause contains a locative argument (*war-a*, "at the house"). Because there is another argument in the clause, the subject *čul* bears *-l*.

C2. In sentence (3), *čul* is the object (patient) of the transitive verb *k'ar-* (see). The suffix *-l* marks the subject (agent) of a clause that contains another argument, not the object. The subject *k'ar* already bears *-l*; the object remains in the unmarked (absolutive) form.

SOLUTION

**Rules to infer:**

1. **Word order (SOV):** The verb appears in final position in every sentence. Noun arguments precede the verb.

2. **The *-l* rule (subject marking):** The suffix *-l* marks the subject of a clause when that clause contains another argument (a patient or a locative/oblique). Specifically:
   - Intransitive clause with no other argument → subject is unmarked (sentences 1, 2, 5, 6, 10).
   - Transitive clause (subject + patient) → subject bears *-l*, patient is unmarked (sentences 3, 4, 11).
   - Intransitive clause + locative → subject bears *-l*, locative bears *-a* (sentences 7, 8, 9, 12).

   The critical evidence is the contrast between sentences (1)/(2)/(5) (intransitive, no other argument, subject unmarked) and sentences (7)/(8)/(9) (intransitive + locative, subject marked with *-l*). This contrast forces the solver to reject the hypothesis "*-l* marks transitive agents" and adopt the correct generalization.

3. **Locative suffix *-a*:** Marks a locative/oblique argument (sentences 7, 8, 9, 12).

4. **Aspect:** *-d* = perfective; *-r* = imperfective. Evidenced by the contrast between (1) *war-d* and (6) *war-r*.

5. **Negation:** *-ma* is a verbal suffix that follows the aspect marker. Evidenced by (10) *war-r-ma* vs. (6) *war-r*, and (11) *k'ar-d-ma* vs. (3) *k'ar-d*.

**How the rules produce each answer:**

- **B1** (*xan-l k'ar k'ar-d*): Transitive clause → agent *xan* bears *-l*; patient *k'ar* is unmarked; verb *k'ar-* + perfective *-d*.
- **B2** (*k'ar dər-d*): Intransitive, no other argument → subject *k'ar* unmarked; verb *dər-* + perfective *-d*.
- **B3** (*čul-l xan-d t'ar-a*): Intransitive + locative → subject *čul* bears *-l* (clause contains another argument: the locative); verb *xan-* + perfective *-d*; locative *t'ar* + *-a*. This is the key "trap" item: a solver who incorrectly generalizes "*-l* = transitive agent" would omit *-l* on *čul*.
- **B4** (*xan war-r-ma*): Intransitive, no other argument → subject *xan* unmarked; verb *war-* + imperfective *-r* + negative *-ma*.
- **B5** (*k'ar-l xan war-a k'ar-d-ma*): Transitive + locative → agent *k'ar* bears *-l*; patient *xan* unmarked; locative *war* + *-a*; verb *k'ar-* + perfective *-d* + negative *-ma*.

DIFFICULTY

Level 1 (introductory). The puzzle requires the solver to discover a non-obvious alignment rule (the *-l* rule) through controlled contrasts, identify three verbal suffixes (aspect × 2, negation), and apply multiple rules simultaneously in the translation items. The central difficulty lies in distinguishing the correct generalization ("*-l* marks the subject when the clause contains another argument") from the naive hypothesis ("*-l* marks transitive agents"), which is resolved by the intransitive + locative data in sentences (7)–(9). The trap item B3 discriminates between these two hypotheses.
