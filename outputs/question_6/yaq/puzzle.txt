

CONTEXT

The following sentences are in Yaqui, a Uto-Aztecan language spoken in northwestern Mexico. Each sentence is followed by its English translation.

DATA

1. naki tari-ri sapa-ka — The child drinks water.
2. kame naki-ri kita-ka — The dog eats the child.
3. naki kame-ri kita-na — The child ate the dog.
4. kame ma naki-ri kita-ka — The dog does not eat the child.
5. naki ma tari-ri sapa-na — The child did not drink water.
6. naki-ta tari-ri sapa-ka — The children drink water.
7. kame naki-ta-ri kita-ka — The dog eats the children.
8. naki kame kai naka-ka — The child is under the dog.

Lexicon:
- kame = dog
- tari = water
- naki = child
- kita = eat
- sapa = drink
- naka = be
- ma = (negative particle)
- kai = (postposition: under)

QUESTIONS

Q1. Translate into Yaqui: "The dog drinks water."

Q2. Translate into Yaqui: "The child does not eat the dog."

Q3. Translate into Yaqui: "The children eat the dog."

ANSWERS

Q1. kame tari-ri sapa-ka
Q2. naki ma kame-ri kita-ka
Q3. naki-ta kame-ri kita-ka

SOLUTION

**Identifying the rules:**

**Word order (SOV):** In every sentence, the verb (the element bearing a TAM suffix) appears in final position. The noun phrases precede the verb.

**Case marking (nominative-accusative):** The subject noun appears in its bare (unmarked) form; the object noun carries the suffix -ri. This is confirmed by *naki* appearing unmarked as subject in sentences 1, 3, 5, 6, 8 and marked with -ri as object in sentences 2, 4, 7. Similarly, *kame* is unmarked as subject in 2, 4, 8 and marked with -ri as object in 3.

**Negative particle:** The invariant word *ma* appears between the subject and the object in negative sentences (4, 5). It is a free word, not an affix.

**Plural suffix:** The suffix -ta marks plural. In sentence 6, *naki-ta* is a plural subject (unmarked case). In sentence 7, *naki-ta-ri* is a plural object (accusative case). This establishes the suffix order: stem + plural + case.

**TAM suffixes:** -ka marks non-past (sentences 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 8); -na marks past (sentences 3, 5). The verb form is determined solely by TAM, not by the identity or number of the subject.

**Postposition:** *kai* is a free word that follows the noun phrase it modifies, marking a locative relation ("under"). In sentence 8, *kame kai* means "under the dog."

**Solving the questions:**

**Q1:** "The dog drinks water."
- Subject: dog (singular, nominative) → kame
- Object: water (singular, accusative) → tari-ri
- Verb: drink (non-past) → sapa-ka
- Order: S O V → **kame tari-ri sapa-ka**

**Q2:** "The child does not eat the dog."
- Subject: child (singular, nominative) → naki
- Negative: ma
- Object: dog (singular, accusative) → kame-ri
- Verb: eat (non-past) → kita-ka
- Order: S ma O V → **naki ma kame-ri kita-ka**

**Q3:** "The children eat the dog."
- Subject: children (plural, nominative) → naki-ta
- Object: dog (singular, accusative) → kame-ri
- Verb: eat (non-past) → kita-ka
- Order: S O V → **naki-ta kame-ri kita-ka**
