

CONTEXT

The following data is from a language spoken in the Maybrat region of Papua, Indonesia. Each line shows a phrase or sentence followed by its English translation. The language uses a small set of morphological markers and has a relatively simple word order.

DATA

**A. Sentences**

1. na pa kapa — I see a man.
2. mu pa puna — You see a house.
3. ra pa turi — He sees a dog.
4. la pa wela — She sees a bird.
5. ta pa sapa — They see trees.
6. nari pa tala — We see rivers.
7. muri pa kapa — You (pl.) see men.
8. na pa sapa — I see a tree.

**B. Noun phrases with demonstratives**

9. di kapa — this man
10. de puna — this house
11. ti turi — that (near) dog
12. ke tala — that (far) river
13. di mura — this head
14. de tapa — this hand
15. di nara — this eye

**C. Possessive constructions**

16. na-mura — my head
17. mu-tapa — your (sg.) hand
18. ra-mura — his head
19. la-tapa — her hand
20. ta-nara — their eye
21. ne-kapa — my man
22. me-puna — your (sg.) house
23. re-turi — his dog
24. le-wela — her bird
25. te-sapa — their tree

QUESTIONS

**Q1.** In the data, the 3rd person singular has two different forms while all other person-number combinations have only one form. Identify the two 3rd person singular forms and state what semantic distinction they encode.

**Q2.** The demonstratives in Section B form a paradigm with two independent dimensions. Identify both dimensions and their values.

**Q3.** Using the patterns in Section C, produce the following possessive constructions:
(a) her dog
(b) their house

ANSWERS

**Q1.** The two 3rd person singular forms are *ra* (masculine) and *la* (feminine). They encode the sex (gender) of the referent.

**Q2.** The two dimensions are: (i) distance, with values proximal (d-), medial (t-), distal (k-); and (ii) gender, with values masculine (-i) and feminine (-e).

**Q3.**
(a) *le-turi*
(b) *te-puna*

SOLUTION

**Q1:** From the sentence data, *ra* appears with a masculine referent (ex. 3: "He sees a dog"; the dog is confirmed masculine by *ti turi* in ex. 11) and *la* appears with a feminine referent (ex. 4: "She sees a bird"; the bird is confirmed feminine by *de puna*-type patterns). All other person-number cells (1sg *na*, 2sg *mu*, 1pl *nari*, 2pl *muri*, 3pl *ta*) have a single form. The distinction is sex-based gender, restricted to 3rd person singular.

**Q2:** The solver isolates the two dimensions by controlled contrast:
- *di* (ex. 9, 13, 15) vs. *de* (ex. 10, 14): same distance (proximal), different vowel → the vowel encodes gender (i = masculine, e = feminine).
- *di* (ex. 9) vs. *ti* (ex. 11): same gender (masculine), different consonant → the consonant encodes distance (d = proximal, t = medial).
- *ke* (ex. 12) confirms k = distal and e = feminine.

Thus: consonant = distance (d, t, k); vowel = gender (i, e).

**Q3:**
(a) *turi* (dog) is a non-body-part noun, belonging to the same possessive class as *kapa*, *puna*, *wela*, and *sapa* (Class B, marked by the -e vowel in the prefix). The 3sg feminine Class B prefix is *le-* (attested in ex. 24: *le-wela* "her bird"). Therefore "her dog" = **le-turi**.
(b) *puna* (house) is also Class B. The 3pl Class B prefix is *te-* (attested in ex. 25: *te-sapa* "their tree"). Therefore "their house" = **te-puna**.

DIFFICULTY

Level 1 (easy). Each question requires a single, direct inference from the data: Q1 identifies a contrast in the pronoun paradigm; Q2 identifies two dimensions in the demonstrative paradigm by controlled contrast; Q3 applies the possessive prefix pattern to two new items. No question requires integrating multiple interacting systems or producing metalinguistic explanations.
